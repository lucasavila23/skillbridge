## LOCKED FRAMING

### Business idea (one line)
SkillBridge is an open platform where teams of tech students take on small, real,
**paid** "campaigns" for small businesses and come away with a **verified record of
shipped client work** — a portable credential, plus a bit of cash.

### PAIN POINT
A tech student with no internship and no industry network can't get the real,
client-facing project experience that employers treat as the deciding signal. Job
postings demand experience they can't obtain without a job; class and tutorial
projects are discounted; open marketplaces filter beginners out. *(66.8% of grads
name lack of experience as their #1 obstacle; ~35% of "entry-level" postings ask
for 3+ years; students with no internship average 0.77 job offers vs 1.61 for paid
interns.)*

### USER
A 2nd–4th-year tech student (software / web / data) who is solid in coursework but
has **no internship, no professional network**, and isn't in the connected /
top-GPA slice that gets pulled in through referrals. *(Referrals convert at
~30–60% to interview vs ~0.1–2% for cold applications — the networked students are
playing a different game.)*

### NEED
To accumulate a **verifiable track record of real, team-delivered client projects**
that an employer will recognise — before having any formal experience.

### INSIGHT
The founder's first job came from a startup **deliberately fishing for candidates
the big firms had rejected** — so demand for inexperienced talent exists, it's
just invisible and unmatched. Meanwhile the peers who "got in early" mostly did it
through **connections, not ability**, so a structured, merit-based way in matters
most precisely for students who have no network. And every existing option forces a
trade-off: real client work is gated behind course enrolment (Riipen, university
capstones), selection (MLH, Extern), or a paid bootcamp; the open, unselective
options are simulations (Forage) or beginner-hostile marketplaces (Upwork/Fiverr).
**Nobody combines real paying client + shipped deliverable + delivery team + open
access + a portable verified credential.**

### HOW MIGHT WE
How might we give an unconnected tech student an **open, merit-based** way to build
a **verified record of real, team-delivered client work** before they have any
formal experience?

### Combined sentence — `[User] needs [need] because [insight]`
> An unconnected 2nd–4th-year tech student needs to accumulate verified,
> team-delivered client projects because employers treat real client experience as
> the deciding hiring signal, the demand for junior talent already exists but is
> unmatched and invisible, and the normal route in runs on connections they don't
> have.

# Supporting material (working notes + research)

## The specific user and context
Tech students (software, web, data) who are **not** in the connected top tier — no
strong alumni network, no family industry contacts, not automatically funnelled
into an internship by a top-5% GPA or a name-brand programme. They have classroom
skills but no employer-recognised proof of ability, and they're entering the worst
entry-level tech market in years.

## Concrete recent situation (founder's story)
Looking for a first internship: applied to several bigger companies, rejected
multiple times, effectively for lack of experience. Eventually landed a role at a
**startup deliberately recruiting people without much experience** — picking up
candidates the larger firms had rejected. That became the founder's first real
experience. Reflection: *"If I'd had SkillBridge, I could have gotten experience
much faster."*

## How a *normal* tech student gets experience today
(The strong-GPA / well-connected student uses the career fair + alumni + referrals
and lands an internship relatively easily — **not the usual path.**)
1. **Class projects** — for a grade, discarded after; employers discount them.
2. **Personal side projects** — most stall here; tutorial-grade builds no longer
   differentiate (employers want "real problems, production-level thinking").
3. **Mass cold applications** — Class of 2024 applied to 21+ jobs on average;
   ~0.1–2% of cold applications reach an interview.
4. **Hackathons / open source** — one-off or high on-ramp; no ongoing client
   relationship, no shipped client deliverable.
5. **Unpaid / underpaid "experience" roles** — scarce and exclusionary (~40% of
   internships taken by students are unpaid).
6. **Eventually**: a break via a referral finally made, a company explicitly
   hiring juniors with no experience (the founder's route), or luck.

**The gap:** no accessible way to get *real client + real shipped deliverable +
team experience + a portable credential* without already having experience or
connections.

## Frustrations and hopes
- **Frustrations:** the experience catch-22; class work not counting; endless
  rejection with no feedback; no visible proof of capability; side projects that
  don't move the needle; freelance marketplaces stacked against beginners; the
  sense that only connected students get in. (72% of job seekers say the search
  harmed their mental health; 57% of the class of 2025 are pessimistic about
  starting their careers.)
- **Hopes:** a CV with real projects and real clients; a credential employers
  trust; a faster, less demoralising route in; some income while learning; a team
  to learn collaboration with.

## Market / environmental trend (candidates for *the* trend)
- New-grad hiring at the 15 largest tech firms **down >50% since 2019**; recent
  grads were ~7% of new hires in 2024 (down from ~11% in 2022).
- ~**666** SWE internship postings nationwide — ~**1 per 165 CS graduates/year**;
  US CS bachelor's degrees nearly doubled in a decade.
- Recent CS-grad **unemployment ~6.1%**, **underemployment ~41%**; bachelor's
  holders aged 20–29 unemployment >12%.
- AI coding tools raised the bar for junior roles; many teams stopped backfilling
  junior seats.
- **Skills-based hiring** rhetoric is high (~70–81% of employers claim it; ~53%
  dropped some degree requirements) **but implementation lags badly** — an HBS /
  Burning Glass 2024 study found fewer than 1 in 700 hires actually affected by
  degree-requirement removal. → A *verified real-work* record is more useful than
  a course certificate precisely because employers still discount unverifiable
  claims.

**Recommended trend to name in the exercise:** the collapse of the entry-level
on-ramp (>50% drop in big-tech new-grad hiring + internship scarcity) — it's the
most concrete and best-sourced.

## Existing alternatives — why each fails to close the gap

| Alternative | Model | Gap it leaves |
|---|---|---|
| Upwork / Fiverr | Open marketplace, solo | Beginners can't win bids vs rated freelancers; no team, no scaffolding, no credential |
| Forage | Free simulated virtual work experience, employer-funded | Not a real client; nothing shipped; completion cert understood as a simulation |
| Riipen | Employer projects embedded in university courses | Gated behind an enrolled course; usually unpaid; credential lives in a transcript |
| Parker Dewey | Short **paid** solo micro-internships ($200–600) | Solo not team; recruiting-funnel framing; no structured deliverable or portable credential |
| Extern (Paragon One) | Guided multi-week company projects + credential | Often scoped exercises not paid SMB deliverables; selective; not team-delivered |
| MLH Fellowship | 12-wk pods on corporate open-source, need-based stipend | Open-source only (no SMB client); competitive/selective |
| University capstones | Teams of 4–6, one semester, external client, faculty-run | Enrolled degree students only; cost-free to client (no pay); credit ≠ portable credential |
| Bootcamp dev-agency (CodeDay Labs, Sabio, Creating Coding Careers) | Mentored team ships real/OSS project | Tied to paid bootcamp enrolment or highly selective; OSS not paid client |
| Catchafire | Skills-based volunteering for nonprofits | Unpaid; aimed at experienced professionals; nonprofit-only; individual; no credential |

**SkillBridge's wedge:** real paying SMB client + real shipped deliverable + team +
open access (no enrolment/selection) + a portable, verifiable credential — no
competitor combines all five.

## Small-business demand (evidence)
- ~27–28% of US small businesses still have **no website** (2024–26); ~23–24%
  intend to build one soon; cost is a cited barrier for ~16–26% of those without.
- Typical small-business website spend: 36% spend **$1k–$10k**, median ~**$5k**;
  freelancer builds commonly $1.5k–$6k; agencies $5k–$20k.
- "Too small for an agency" is real — agencies often require ~$4k/mo retainer
  minimums, pushing small one-off jobs to freelancers.
- Risk perceptions about juniors centre on capability and lack of process/support;
  **structured onboarding/mentorship mitigates**.
- **Evidence gap:** no clean survey on SMB willingness to trust a *student team*
  for paid digital work, or on repeat/conversion rates → primary interviews needed.

## Credential credibility (evidence)
- Skills-based hiring adoption ~70–81% of employers (2024), up from 57% (2022);
  ~53% dropped some degree requirements — **but** <1 in 700 hires actually affected
  by degree-requirement removal (HBS / Burning Glass 2024). Certifications act as a
  **tie-breaker**, not an experience replacement.
- Portfolios / real work carry weight (directional, weak sourcing): ~86% of hiring
  pros review portfolios when available; GPA screening fell from 73% (2019) to
  ~42%.
- IBM: ~25% of US hires via skills-based pathways after 4 years, performance
  matching traditional hires; Accenture dropped degrees for ~40% of US roles.
- LinkedIn: recruiters search by **skills**, not certificate names; a **verified**
  credential (credential ID + verification URL + employer tag) is a real trust
  signal and can gate whether a profile surfaces in recruiter/AI search.

## Frequency / scale of the pain (evidence)
- Internship participation: ~two-thirds of 2024 graduating seniors did one (up
  from ~61% for 2023); a broader figure cites only 21.5% of *all* students in 2024
  (definitions differ).
- Offers by internship status: paid interns 1.61 offers, unpaid 0.94, **no
  internship 0.77**. Intern-to-fulltime conversion hit 63.1% (5-yr high) — no
  internship = starting well behind.
- "Need experience to get experience": ZipRecruiter — **66.8%** of grads cite lack
  of experience as their biggest obstacle; LinkedIn/Resume Target — 61% say it's
  the top entry-level issue; ~35% of "entry-level" postings ask for 3+ years.
- Mental health: 72% of job seekers say the search harmed their mental health
  (2024); 57% of class of 2025 pessimistic about starting their careers; 91% of
  Gen Z workers report at least occasional mental-health challenges.
- **Evidence gap:** no clean data isolating **CS/tech-major** internship rates or
  time-to-first-job for no-internship tech grads — general-population proxies only.

## Verification / certification (product core)
- **Model = A + C:** a platform-issued **"verified campaign"** record (deliverable
  attached/linkable, scope, dates, the student's role and team) **plus** a
  **rating / reference from the business**. Proof-of-work, not an exam.
- **Future:** push verified certifications to **LinkedIn** so they appear on the
  student's profile automatically.
- **Design constraint (from research):** verification must resist gaming (fake
  businesses, inflated ratings, ghost contributors) or the credential is worthless.
