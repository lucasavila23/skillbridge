# Pilot results — what they validate and what remains open

**Assessed:** 2026-10-01 · **Source:** user-supplied actual pilot findings ·
**Decision:** the evidence supports further bounded testing of SkillBridge.
Student need and delivery feasibility have stronger support; paid business
demand, the Madrid market estimate and improved hiring outcomes remain unproven.

The strongest supported proposition is: **help students obtain a manageable
client brief, finish useful work with bounded support, and show their individual
contribution with client acceptance.** Keep familiar tools and peer collaboration.
The report supports testing this proposition further, without establishing
that it is unique or that a marketplace can deliver it profitably at scale.

### Survey numbers and their proper use

Percentages below are recalculated from the reported counts and rounded to
whole percentages. One respondent changes a result by about **9.1 percentage
points**. These are sample descriptions, not regional estimates.

| Finding | Count / denominator | Use and limit |
|---|---|---|
| Applied within the last 12 months | 10/11 = 91% | Supports a relevant application context among respondents. |
| Received an offer | 1/11 = 9% overall; 1/10 = 10% among applicants | Remaining application categories total nine (3 interview/no offer, 4 no interview, 2 waiting). These outcomes do not establish that inadequate project evidence caused the result. |
| Experience beyond coursework; explaining contribution | 9/11 = 82% for each | Separate multiple-choice difficulties, not an exclusive ranking or proof that finding openings is irrelevant; 5/11 also selected finding opportunities. |
| Latest project lacked external user/client | 8/11 = 73% | Reported as 6 coursework + 2 unused personal projects; the other 3 had business/organisation/client work. This does not measure internship/referral eligibility. |
| Scope/deadlines; career relevance as participation factors | 6/11 = 55% for each | Most frequently selected factors in a question allowing up to three; not a ranked-choice or willingness-to-pay test. |
| Guidance; business feedback/reference as factors | 5/11 = 45% for each | Supports including named help and client feedback in the next concrete offer. |
| Portfolio display; payment amount as factors | 3/11 = 27% for each | Lower selection frequency does not establish that pay is unnecessary or students will accept a lower fee. Retain the paid model. |
| At least two hours/week stated availability | 8/11 = 73% | Five report 2–4 hours, three 5–7; three report less than two. Calendar-specific capacity and sustained participation still need records. |
| Would review a specific brief, payment and schedule | 4/11 = 36% | Stated next action, not acceptance. Three would attend an information session, one wants updates, three want no action now. Do not combine these into signups. |

Sources: P005, T007, P009, T021, P024–P025. The obstacle table T011 needs
reconciliation before using its exact distribution: **6 + 5 + 5 + 3 + 2 + 2 =
23 selections**, exceeding **11 × 2 = 22** allowed by its “up to 2” label.
Possible issues include the response limit, counts or denominator; the report
does not identify which. Preserve the original and correct only against raw
responses. Other reported percentages are consistent with rounding; availability
and next-action percentages summing to 99% are normal rounding effects.

```text
260,000 × 10% × 75% × 50% × 20% = 1,950 ≈ 2,000 students / academic year
```

The report's comparison of **36% willing to review** with the **20% readiness
assumption** compares different endpoints. Its wording that the assumption is
a “pilot-cohort result” is not supported by the supplied counts: **20% remains
an assumption, not a measured pilot rate**. Once the offer log is available,
calculate:

```text
pilot readiness rate = unique eligible recipients completing all readiness steps
                       ÷ all unique eligible recipients of a suitable real offer
```

Include declines and non-responses, review each work sample and compatible
availability, and record briefing attendance and acceptance by the disclosed
deadline. Follow [the existing definition](analysis.md#2-challenge-a4-with-concrete-offers).
Even a complete small-cohort rate would be a labelled sensitivity input, not
validation of the annual Madrid rate. Track repeated offers without counting a
student more than once across the year.

## Delivery measures: reported results versus measurable validation

All nine dimensions now have a reported outcome. None has enough underlying
data in the attachment to calculate a numerical benchmark or a success rate.

| Measure | Reported actual outcome (T032; P034) | Obtain before treating it as a measured benchmark |
|---|---|---|
| M1 — Setup | Kickoff on promised date; organiser supplied client and agreed brief. | Promised/actual dates; student and organiser setup time, including client acquisition. |
| M2 — Pay and hours | Agreed student pay received; hours including setup/revisions within commitment. | Pay/expenses/hours per student and payment dates; separate business receipts and funding source. |
| M3 — Fit | Skills, language and timetable matched; no unexpected exam availability. | Offer/constraint records and mismatches across all recipients, including non-joiners. |
| M4 — Traceability | Contributions linked to version history, artifact, feedback, acceptance and permission. | Inspect those linked records per student; retain missing or disputed attribution. |
| M5 — Access | Required access on time; setup/transfers within budget. | Tool/access steps, planned/actual effort and unresolved blocks. |
| M6 — Repeatability | A subsequent campaign reached milestones; withdrawals/delays retained. | Full campaign register, process versions, dates, withdrawals and delays; no denominator inferred from the prose. |
| M7 — Quality | Original criteria accepted by deadline after bounded rework; clients report use. | Dated original criteria, review/acceptance, scope changes, defects and rework hours. |
| M8 — Support | Within agreed budget; substantive work by students, no mentor takeover. | Actual/precommitted support hours and who performed each intervention, including unpaid organiser effort. |
| M9 — Clarifications | Briefs sufficient; clarifications/revisions within budgets. | Planned/actual minutes and reasons per clarification/revision, including asynchronous work. |

These results reduce uncertainty about whether the process can work. Without
cost totals, they do not establish margins, affordability of support or delivery
without a paid project manager. The report does not establish a particular team
size, that teammates were strangers, or a measured dropout rate.

## Business test: preserve the decision rule

The [Experiment Card](../experiment-card.md) requires **at least 2 of the
original 10 qualifying businesses** to **both sign and pay the 10% deposit
within 10 days**. Interest, a walkthrough, student compensation or client
acceptance is not that conversion. The source explicitly says charters were
not signed, so it does not demonstrate a qualifying commercial commitment.

It also does not establish that all ten qualifying pitches occurred and their
response windows closed. Therefore record **threshold not demonstrated;
cohort result unresolved**, rather than inventing a final 0/10 score. If the
completed log establishes zero, use the card's existing zero-conversion branch;
if exactly one, use its separate five-pitch extension; if at least two, use its
continue branch. Do not count later conversions retrospectively inside closed
windows or change a threshold after seeing results.

Reconcile how funded student offers and payments were supported while business
charters remained unsigned: identify the payer, amount, deposit status and any
separate pilot arrangement. Organiser/sponsor funding can support a delivery
test but cannot validate business payment demand. Record any deviation from
the charter/deposit-before-delivery sequence explicitly.
