# Competitive-analysis audit and next validation work

**Date:** 2026-10-01 · **Scope:** the updated six-column screenshot, applied to
SkillBridge's Madrid student segment. **Status:** documentation audit complete;
updated with the user-supplied actual pilot findings. The original protocols
below remain proposals; reported execution and remaining gaps are distinguished.

**Finding:** most ingredients were present, but target-user behaviour was not a
separate column, full segment fit was unconfirmed, and several comparisons had
no operational measurement. The [competitive table](competitive-analysis.md)
now states the six fields explicitly. Completing the table does not supply the
missing behavioural evidence.

**Latest evidence:** the [pilot assessment](pilot-assessment-2026-10-01.md)
records stronger problem evidence, actual reported offer commitments, delivery
and recruiter comparisons. V1 and V3–V5 now have reported support, with counts
and underlying records missing. V2's commercial threshold remains unresolved.
Do not read the proposed sample sizes below as the actual pilot sample.

## 3. Make the comparison measurable

Use the same definitions for SkillBridge and whichever alternative is actually
available to a participant. Record unavailable information as **unknown**, not
zero. The task and output may differ across alternatives; report that difference
instead of presenting unlike tasks as a controlled comparison.

| ID / screenshot dimension | SkillBridge interpretation and measure | Evidence to retain |
|---|---|---|
| **M1 — Setup friction** | Minutes of active effort and calendar days from seeking an opportunity to a usable start; number of applications, access gates and abandoned steps. Separate waiting from productive work. | Dated access walkthrough, application/offer timestamps, step log and reason for abandonment. |
| **M2 — Price** | Student fees/expenses, payment actually received and total hours including setup/rework. Effective cash return = (received pay − direct expenses) ÷ total hours, only when all inputs are known and hours exceed zero. Track business payments separately. | Actual offer terms, time records and payment evidence; a promised payout is not received income. |
| **M3 — Custom fit** | Compatibility with role/skills, language, hours, exam calendar, team arrangement and portfolio goal. Count mismatches and changed requirements before acceptance. | Student constraints recorded before the pitch, exact offer and documented mismatch/change. |
| **M4 — Traceability** | Can a reviewer trace a student's named contribution to a dated artifact, scope, feedback and acceptance, with sharing permission? Record each item as present, absent or unknown. | Version history, role statement, artifact/demo, client review and permission record. A certificate alone does not establish every item. |
| **M5 — Integration constraints** | Tools/accounts/permissions needed to join and deliver; blocked steps, manual transfers, context changes and time spent resolving access. | Workflow walkthrough and access/support log. Do not assume particular tools were used from an application asking for GitHub. |
| **M6 — Repeatability** | Across successive real campaigns using the same support process: students reaching milestones, projects accepted on time and variation in support/rework. Report every attempt. | Fixed process version, scheduled/actual dates, dropouts and client acceptance per project. Two attempts are an early signal, not reliable scale. |
| **M7 — Accuracy / quality** | Acceptance criteria passed before and after rework; defects, changed scope and fixes. Define task-specific checks with the client before work. | Baseline criteria, review results, rework time and explicit acceptance. If the student's work is rewritten by a mentor, record that contribution. |
| **M8 — Interventions** | Number and duration of mentor, teammate, organiser and client interventions; who did the work and whether it was advice or a rescue/rewrite. | Support/time log including preparation and async work. A scheduled 15-minute meeting is not the total support cost. |
| **M9 — Prompt burden** | The screenshot's repeated AI prompting is not our primary job. Measure the analogous burden: repeated brief clarifications, instructions, handoffs and revisions needed to make work usable. If AI is used, separately record prompting/checking effort when relevant. | Clarification/revision log with reason and elapsed effort. Ordinary client communication is not automatically waste. |

**Before any offer:** record actual scope, compensation, student payment split,
dates, hours, support owner, portfolio permission, acceptance criteria and a
maximum support budget. The pilot reports agreed terms/budgets, but their actual
values were not supplied. Recover them from the pilot records. Use observed baseline
effort and feasible team capacity to set budgets; do not invent a market price
or claim that a desired delivery time has already been achieved.

## 4. Prioritised validation sequence

Sample sizes below are proposed practical targets, not statistical guarantees.
Record the chosen protocol before gathering results and preserve deviations.
The new tests are diagnostic proposals; the existing business test retains its
own confirmed threshold and decision branches.

| Test | Status after actual pilot report | Remaining validation |
|---|---|---|
| V1 — Behaviour and segment | Reported choices and recurring obstacles; pilot profile fits the target description. | Participant-level eligibility, dates/artifacts and counts; survey fit and recruitment coverage. |
| V2 — Access and business funding | Local competitor access unknown. Businesses reportedly interested but did not sign. | Complete original-cohort log and apply the unchanged charter/deposit rule; explain pilot funding. |
| V3 — Commitment | Readiness actions and acceptance reported; some personal projects displaced. | Unique eligible offer denominator, completed-readiness numerator, deadlines and declines/non-responses; A4 remains unmeasured. |
| V4 — Delivery | Positive M1–M9 outcomes and subsequent campaign reported. | Campaign register, all attempts and actual/precommitted budgets, pay, time and acceptance records. |
| V5 — Evaluation | Recruiters reportedly found contributions clearer. | Reviewer/pair counts, protocol, artifacts, ratings and counterevidence; no hiring uplift established. |

The [assessment](pilot-assessment-2026-10-01.md) maps each M1–M9 reported result
to the records still needed. First recover existing evidence; do not repeat
fieldwork solely because a prior plan said it was pending.

### V1 — Establish actual behaviour and segment fit first

**Hypothesis:** the target segment already tries to create useful project
evidence and encounters a concrete, recurring obstacle worth addressing.

Start with the two existing students, if available, to close the missing fields
and inspect their reported work. Then aim for **eight new student interviews
across at least two institutions**, including a public institution where access
can be arranged. Recruit through general classes/programmes as well as relevant
communities. Confirm campus, programme/year, internship history and referral
access; label people outside the segment as comparison cases. Track the two
follow-ups separately so they are not presented as new independent participants.

Reconstruct one recent trigger → alternatives → choice → action → outcome
sequence. Invite a voluntary artifact walkthrough. Use the
[fieldwork template](validation-record-template.md), which includes a script.
Record actual use, awareness, attempted access, rejection and unknown separately.
Do not fill five category quotas by assigning students to hypothetical behaviours.

**Measure:** number of confirmed segment matches, recent episodes and artifact-
supported episodes out of the actual sample; time/cost/friction by alternative.
Report recurrence as counts, not a regional market share.

**Decision:** a repeated specific obstacle supports targeting that problem for
V3; record who and how many independently reported it. If accounts instead show
adequate existing work or no urgent need, narrow or revise the segment/value
proposition. Missing artifacts or weak segment fit leave the claim unresolved.
Do not demand that students have tried a named competitor to count their need.

### V2 — Check local access and funded opportunities

**Hypothesis:** suitable real projects are both accessible to our students and
worth funding by businesses. Treat these as two separate questions.

For alternatives, document a real access route or an explicit barrier using a
consenting student's walkthrough and current terms. If no Riipen route is found,
record “access unconfirmed” unless an authoritative source confirms a specific
restriction. Use the best available alternative in V3, not an inaccessible
programme shown as a false choice.

For SkillBridge, prepare the actual talent profiles and pricing method required
by the [business Experiment Card](../experiment-card.md). That card requires
**both a signed charter and a 10% deposit within 10 days**, with a primary
threshold of **at least 2/10 qualifying businesses**. Keep its original
denominator and exact extension/zero-conversion rules. The exploratory business
interviews are not formal pitches. No businesses are contacted by this audit.

**Decision:** follow the existing card for paid delivery. Local access findings
may support a distribution opportunity, but they do not prove preference for
SkillBridge. Insufficient funded seats prevent a meaningful student offer test;
they must not be disguised as student rejection.

### V3 — Observe a real choice and a commitment

**Hypothesis:** an eligible student chooses and commits to a specific campaign
despite the effort and responsibility of the best alternative available to them.

Before business pitches, use V1 to prepare an actual available talent pool, as
the existing card requires. After a business commitment and a decision to
continue, make real student offers, finalise the team and meet the card's
five-business-day kickoff requirement. V1 must not delay that deadline. If the
talent pool cannot support it, resolve capacity before pitching businesses.

Freeze the offer terms and a feasible cohort size before outreach. Cohort size
depends on real funded seats and matching capacity; do not default to 100 offers
without that capacity. Never promise the same unconditional seat to multiple
students. Track capacity-blocked or waitlisted people separately from eligible
students who received a genuine offer.

Show the actual hours, dates, pay, role, support and output for available options,
including continuing the current approach and “not now.” Record complementary
use as well as replacement; a student may continue applying while doing a
campaign. Use the readiness definition in [the market plan](analysis.md):
work-sample review, compatible recorded availability, briefing and acceptance of
the real terms. Its ten-day student window is an outer observation limit; a
specific paid pilot must use a disclosed deadline that permits the earlier
business kickoff obligation. Report any shorter window rather than pooling it
as an identical test.

**Measure:** ready-to-join students / unique eligible students who received a
genuine suitable offer. Retain declines and non-responses. Record the previous
alternative and what time/activity was displaced. Report kickoff and completion
separately; a waitlist signup is not a commitment.

**Decision:** substitute the observed rate into A4 as a labelled sample-specific
sensitivity calculation, preserving the model's other assumptions. A rate below
20% challenges that assumption under the tested terms; a rate at or above 20%
is compatible with it for that cohort, not validation of the annual Madrid rate.
Use refusal reasons to change the offer or urgency hypothesis. Report counts,
source, period and capacity limits before drawing a conclusion.

### V4 — Check delivery quality and support burden

**Hypothesis:** students can produce accepted, attributable client work within
the agreed workload and the team's feasible support budget.

Track the first **two funded campaigns, if secured**, using M1–M9 and the same
basic support process. Define acceptance criteria and support/time budgets per
campaign before kickoff. Do not force identical budgets onto different scopes;
record scope differences when comparing outcomes. Include abandoned and delayed
campaigns in the report. Track acceptance at the agreed deadline, rework,
withdrawals, actual compensation and later obligations.

**Decision:** accepted work within the stated budgets supports another bounded
pilot. Delivery requiring substantial unplanned rescue challenges the current
support/skill model even if the client is satisfied. Failure or delay triggers
the existing charter terms and investigation of scope, skills and support; do
not erase the attempt or retroactively relax its acceptance criteria. Two
successful campaigns would not establish scalable operations or profitability.

### V5 — Test whether the resulting evidence helps evaluation

**Hypothesis:** a real campaign record lets a junior-tech recruiter assess the
student's contribution and job-relevant ability more clearly than their existing
project evidence.

Once real artifacts exist, ask **five recruiters or hiring managers who assess
junior tech candidates** to review anonymised pairs: a student's existing work
and their campaign evidence. Use equal presentation space and rotate order;
hide platform/student identity where possible without inventing or removing
substantive evidence. Ask reviewers to explain what contribution, ability and
client acceptance they can verify, what is missing, and whom they would invite
to discuss the work. Record ratings separately for each dimension, ties and
reasons. Multiple ratings of the same pair are not independent student outcomes.

**Decision:** clearer assessment with specific artifact-based reasons supports
the proof-of-work format for further testing. Ties or preference for existing
work challenge its incremental value. At best this is evidence about recruiter
evaluation; it does not prove improved hiring rates. If no real campaign
artifact exists, keep V5 unrun—an illustrative mockup is only a concept test.

## 5. What happens next

The next useful action is to **reconcile the existing pilot evidence and close
the commercial test**. Resolve the survey's 23 selections against its maximum
of 22, recover the offer and business denominators, and link actual delivery and
recruiter records. Then fill the remaining V1–V5 gaps and decide the next bounded
test under the existing business rule. Reported delivery is not itself a passed
charter/deposit test.

Use one [validation record](validation-record-template.md) per actual episode
or test unit. Leave results empty until collected. Label evidence as reported,
observed, provider documentation or interpretation; distinguish stated future
preferences from past behaviour. Preserve contradictions and missing fields.

**Completion boundary:** the six-column comparison and validation materials are
explicit and updated with reported actual results. The idea has stronger early
support; commercial conversion, regional demand and hiring impact remain
unvalidated. This audit analyses supplied fieldwork; it does not create new
observations or retroactively certify the proposed protocols as completed.
