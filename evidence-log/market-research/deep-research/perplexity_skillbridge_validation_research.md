# SkillBridge Validation Research

**Research cutoff date: 3 October 2026.** All web sources were checked on that date; Spanish source titles are preserved.

## Decision brief

**Recommendation: do not invest further in product build. Run a narrow, time-boxed commercial validation sprint focused on completing the existing 10-business test and testing one tightly defined delivery model.** The central unproven assumption is not student interest but whether a Madrid small business will pay an inexperienced student team a price high enough to cover coordination, QA, rework, payments and legal/compliance overhead.

Desk research supports a plausible price ceiling for simple landing pages—roughly €390–€900 from Madrid agencies—but does not establish that businesses will accept student delivery at that price. Kit Digital’s application window closed in late October 2025, so it should not be treated as available demand support. [web:3][web:16][web:19]

Proceed only if the existing test produces at least the pre-registered threshold of signed charters plus 10% deposits. Otherwise, pause product investment and resolve the blocking commercial and legal assumptions before another outreach round.

## Evidence-integrity check

The supplied pilot evidence is **reported, not independently verified**. It contains useful directional signals but material gaps:

- The 11-student survey shows stated pain: 9/11 wanted to demonstrate experience beyond coursework; 9/11 wanted to explain individual contribution; 8/11 said their latest project had no external user/client. These are **stated preferences**, not commitment. [cite:user-provided]
- Four students said they would review a concrete brief, payment and schedule. This is weaker than acceptance, participation or completion. [cite:user-provided]
- Claims of genuine offer acceptance, student payments, accepted client work, a later campaign and recruiter evaluation are potentially important, but lack denominators, cohort definitions, dates, artifacts and payment records. They cannot currently support a conversion or retention rate. [cite:user-provided]
- Businesses’ interest and willingness to pay are not commitments; no signed charter or deposit is reported. [cite:user-provided]
- The obstacle table’s 23 selections against an apparent maximum of 22 is unresolved. **Exclude it from quantitative conclusions**; web research cannot repair missing internal records. [cite:user-provided]

These are **hypotheses for outreach**, not validated demand. The strongest initial offer is probably a **single landing page for an active campaign**, not a full website: the task is bounded, the trigger is often time-sensitive, and acceptance criteria can be explicit.

### Price evidence

Published Madrid prices are **advertised prices**, not observed SkillBridge transactions:

- E2D: landing básica from €390; landing pro from €590; premium from €890. [web:16][web:19]
- YAG: campaign landing page from €390. [web:20]
- LocalWeb Studio: landing page from €250 plus VAT. [web:21]
- Desarrollo Web Madrid: landing/one-page site €450–€900. [web:27]
- Malt lists landing-page freelancers at widely varying day rates, including €100–€750/day; its published designer average is €219/day. These are profile or market signals, not quotes for equivalent scope. [web:24][web:26]

A credible SkillBridge price must be compared like-for-like: discovery, copy, design, build, revisions, hosting/domain, analytics, QA, post-launch fixes, VAT and support. A €390 agency landing page may exclude much of what a nervous client expects from a “real” provider.

### Substitutes and buying process

Businesses can delay, use Wix/Webflow/WordPress templates, ask an existing employee, use a contact, or hire a low-cost freelancer. The relevant question is not “do landing pages cost money?” but “will this owner pay an unproven student team rather than a known freelancer or agency?” **Not found within this search:** Madrid-specific observed purchase data showing businesses choosing inexperienced student teams over freelancers.

### Kit Digital

Kit Digital targeted pymes, microenterprises and self-employed people, but the final open application calls closed at the end of October 2025. [web:3] Therefore, for a sprint starting now, treat it as **not currently available** unless a new official call is confirmed. Even where subsidies exist, they support defined digital solutions through authorised agents; they do not demonstrate willingness to pay inexperienced student teams.

**Desk research supports:** a price band, reachable segments and bounded task definitions. **Only SkillBridge’s own test can establish:** signed commitment, deposit payment, delivery acceptance, refund risk and profitable economics.

## 2. Legal feasibility and delivery economics

This is a potential blocker, not an administrative detail. SkillBridge cannot simply label paid student work as “experience” if the arrangement resembles employment, a placement or a regular commercial service.

### Operating arrangements

| Model | When plausible | Material pilot implications |
|---|---|---|
| University-linked external academic internship | Only where a university agrees, a suitable “convenio de prácticas” exists and the work fits academic rules | Since 1 January 2024, students in formative or external academic placements are included in Social Security; terms depend on the placement arrangement. [web:10] |
| Employment / “contrato en prácticas” | If SkillBridge or the client employs the student under an employment contract | Requires payroll, Social Security, labour-law compliance and likely higher fixed cost; not a lightweight pilot model. |
| Genuine independent contractor | Only if the student truly controls methods, timing and client relationship, and is not economically dependent on SkillBridge | Student may need alta as autónomo in Social Security and Hacienda before starting a habitual economic activity. [web:31][web:32] |
| SkillBridge as intermediary/vendor | If SkillBridge contracts the client and subcontracts or coordinates student contributors | Requires clear contracts, payment flows, IP, liability, QA and tax treatment; classification of each student remains fact-specific. |

Spanish Social Security guidance says a person performing a habitual, direct, lucrative economic activity outside an employment contract must register as a self-employed worker (“trabajador autónomo”). [web:31] That makes a casual “pay students through the platform” model legally risky unless qualified local advice confirms another structure.

### Non-EU students

For non-EU students, ordinary study authorisation does not automatically permit employment. A residence authorisation for internships requires a placement under a “convenio de prácticas” or “contrato de trabajo en prácticas”; under a convenio, it does not authorise work except in the specific employment-contract case. [web:4][web:6] Social Security states that non-EU students in qualifying placements under DA 52ª can be registered even without a work permit because they are treated as assimilated employees, not ordinary workers. [web:2][web:7]

**Implication:** SkillBridge should segment student eligibility by nationality/residence status and work authorisation before making any paid offer. This is a screening requirement, not a legal opinion.

### Other pilot-relevant obligations

- **Client contract:** fixed scope, deliverables, acceptance test, revision limit, deadline, handover, IP transfer upon payment, confidentiality, limitation of liability and refund/deposit rule.
- **IP and portfolio:** obtain written client permission for public portfolio use; separately obtain student consent for name, contribution description and artifacts.
- **GDPR/LOPDGDD:** avoid giving students access to personal data; use test data where possible; define processor/controller roles if client data is processed.
- **University policies:** obtain written university approval before recruiting through careers services or presenting work as an academic placement.
- **Qualified local advice is required** before signing client contracts, paying students, handling non-EU participants, or claiming any internship/labour classification.

### Break-even model

Illustrative model for one **€700 landing-page campaign**, excluding VAT. All costs except published market benchmarks are labelled assumptions.

| Item | Low case | Base case | High case | Basis |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| Client receipt | €500 | €700 | €900 | Market advertised range; not a validated SkillBridge price. [web:16][web:19][web:27] |
| Student compensation | €150 | €250 | €350 | Assumption; must be set before outreach |
| Acquisition cost | €50 | €100 | €200 | Assumption: outreach time, tools, sample ads |
| Coordination and QA | €75 | €150 | €300 | Assumption: founder/mentor time cash-equivalent |
| Rework/refund reserve | €50 | €100 | €250 | Assumption; inexperienced-team risk |
| Payment/platform costs | €10 | €20 | €40 | Assumption |
| Post-delivery support | €25 | €50 | €150 | Assumption; 2–4 weeks |
| **Cash cost** | **€360** | **€670** | **€1,290** |  |
| **Cash margin** | **€140** | **€30** | **-€390** |  |

Founder time is excluded above. If two founders spend 30 hours each per campaign and value time at €20/hour, economic cost rises by €1,200. On that assumption, even the €900 high case is economically loss-making unless student pay, coordination hours or acquisition cost fall sharply—or several campaigns share fixed coordination.

**Break-even conditions:** at €700 revenue, cash break-even requires total delivery cost below €700. At the base-case cost structure, the project barely breaks even before founder labour. A sustainable model likely needs either: (a) higher prices with stronger scope control, (b) smaller teams and reusable templates, (c) university/subsidised funding for student compensation, or (d) a productised workflow that cuts coordination and QA time.

**Not found within this search:** observed SkillBridge delivery hours, rework rates, refund rates or client-funded payment records. These must come from the pilot.

## 4. Student commitment and recruiter value

The pilot pain points are credible: students want external validation and a way to show individual contribution. But willingness to review an offer is not sustained participation. Real client work adds deadlines, coordination, unclear scope, rework and opportunity cost—especially during exams or internship-application season.

For recruiters, attributable client work may improve evaluation by showing artifacts, role and client feedback. However, no evidence found here demonstrates that it increases interview invitations or hiring outcomes for Madrid tech students. Any such claim should be avoided until SkillBridge collects matched before/after outcomes with a comparison group.

The practical student proposition should therefore be modest: **paid, bounded, portfolio-visible work with a named contribution and client acceptance**—not “guaranteed career advancement.”

## 5. Market estimate check

The working estimate uses an anchor of 260,000 students, but the Comunidad de Madrid reports **377,840 students** across public universities, affiliated centres, private universities, UDIMA and Madrid-area UNED associated centres in 2024–25. [web:1] The 260,000 anchor therefore appears to exclude some categories or use a narrower institutional definition; the report should state exactly which institutions and enrolment concept it includes.

The estimate \(260{,}000 \times 10\% \times 75\% \times 50\% \times 20\% = 1{,}950\) is a chain of assumptions, not observed demand. The 20% “ready to commit” figure is especially unsupported: the pilot’s 4/11 stated willingness to review an offer cannot be substituted for it. [cite:user-provided]

A more defensible framing is:

- **Eligible population:** needs a defined programme list, campus geography, degree level and deduplication rule.
- **Reachable students:** depends on actual university, society and referral channels.
- **Ready under a funded offer:** requires observed sign-up, charter acceptance, completion and payment data.

Keep market sizing secondary. A regional market of thousands does not matter if the first ten businesses will not sign and deposit.

## Three proposed next experiments

### Experiment 1 — Complete the existing business test

- **Maps to:** Assumption 1.
- **Recruitment:** the original 10 qualifying businesses only; no new cohort.
- **Hypothesis:** at least 2 of 10 will sign a charter and pay a refundable 10% deposit within 10 days.
- **Method:** preserve the pre-registered pitch, pricing method, scope definitions and conversion rule.
- **Observable action:** signed charter **and** deposit received.
- **Numerator/denominator:** conversions / all 10 original qualifying businesses, including non-responses after windows close.
- **Window:** until every pitch has been delivered and its 10-day deposit window has closed.
- **Minimum resources:** 20–40 founder hours; €0 new cash unless a project is secured; must have delivery capacity and refundable-deposit funding before pitching.
- **Decision rule:** follow the existing branches exactly; do not retrospectively alter thresholds.
- **Inconclusive result:** if pitches or windows are incomplete, the result is “not yet decided,” not 0/10.

### Experiment 2 — Paid student readiness micro-task

- **Maps to:** Assumption 4.
- **Recruitment:** 10–15 target-segment students who consented after seeing a concrete brief; record city, year, programme, work authorisation status and prior experience.
- **Hypothesis:** at least 60% of students accepting a paid micro-task will complete it by the deadline.
- **Method:** one 3–5 hour bounded task using non-confidential sample material; explicit pay, deadline, deliverable and consent form.
- **Observable action:** completed deliverable meeting acceptance criteria.
- **Numerator/denominator:** completed tasks / students who accepted the task.
- **Window:** 10 business days from acceptance.
- **Minimum resources:** €150–€500 student payments; 10–20 founder hours; legal review of payment method.
- **Decision rule:** ≥60% completion supports a small paid pilot; <40% suggests supply/commitment is a blocker; between 40–59% is inconclusive.
- **Consent/privacy:** informed consent, minimum personal data, right to withdraw before work starts, no deceptive “guaranteed job” claims.

### Experiment 3 — Delivery-economics shadow test

- **Maps to:** Assumptions 2 and 3.
- **Recruitment:** only businesses that convert in Experiment 1; do not create fake demand.
- **Hypothesis:** a defined landing-page campaign can be delivered within the agreed deadline with total tracked delivery cost below 80% of client receipt.
- **Method:** for each secured project, log founder hours, student hours, pay, revisions, QA, rework, payment fees, refund exposure and post-delivery support.
- **Observable action:** accepted handover plus completed cost log.
- **Numerator/denominator:** campaigns meeting cost/deadline criteria / campaigns delivered.
- **Window:** through client acceptance plus 14 days.
- **Minimum resources:** dependent on secured projects; reserve at least one project’s student compensation plus a 30% contingency before kickoff.
- **Decision rule:** continue only if at least one project is delivered on time, accepted, and cash cost is below 80% of receipt; otherwise narrow scope or pause scaling.
- **Inconclusive result:** one project cannot establish a rate; it can only validate feasibility and expose cost drivers.

## What next, what would change it, and what to avoid

**What to do next:** complete the existing 10-business test exactly as designed, while separately obtaining a limited legal review and running the paid student micro-task. Do not build more product until a real client has signed, deposited, and a project has been delivered within a tracked cost envelope.

**What would change the recommendation:** two or more charter-plus-deposit conversions would justify a small delivery pilot; a qualified lawyer identifying an inexpensive compliant payment structure would reduce the legal blocker; evidence that a €700–€900 price repeatedly covers delivery costs would justify continued testing. Conversely, zero completed pitches, no deposit, or a legal structure requiring employment-level costs would justify deferring product investment.

**Claims to avoid today:** “businesses will pay,” “students are job-ready,” “SkillBridge improves hiring,” “the market is 2,000 students,” “Kit Digital funds this,” and any claim that client acceptance proves profitable delivery. The current evidence supports only a testable hypothesis, not a validated business.
