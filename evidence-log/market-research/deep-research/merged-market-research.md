# SkillBridge Madrid — consolidated deep research

**Compiled:** 2026-10-03. **Inputs:** five user-supplied AI research reports, each
dated 2026-10-03. **Purpose:** merge their substantive findings into one decision
document, preserving attribution, disagreements and the project's existing
evidence boundaries.

## Decision synthesis

The common recommendation is to **continue learning through a bounded commercial
and delivery test before expanding product investment or outreach**. The reports
prioritise business-funded commitment, a workable operating arrangement and the
full cost of delivery over further refinement of the student population estimate.
This is agreement between research outputs, not five independent validations of
the business. [CL] [CX] [GE] [GR] [PX]

The most consistent initial offer hypothesis is a narrowly scoped landing or
brochure website for an owner-led business with a current reason to act. The
reports disagree about whether price, managed delivery, social impact or access
to student talent provides an advantage. No report supplies a traceable record
of a qualifying Madrid business purchasing this specific SkillBridge offer.

The consolidated decision sequence is:

1. Reconcile the existing business, student and delivery records. Do not score
   incomplete pitches or response windows as a final failed test.
2. Establish the actual contracting, student-payment and delivery arrangement,
   with its costs and responsibilities, before assuming capacity to fulfil new
   offers. The reports' legal assertions are conflicting research leads.
3. Apply the unchanged business Experiment Card. For projects the decision rule
   authorises, record all student, acquisition, coordination, QA and support
   costs alongside client acceptance.
4. Use those observations to decide whether to repeat, narrow, reprice or pause.
   A successful small test is evidence to continue learning, not regional demand,
   profitable scale or improved hiring outcomes.

These are synthesis recommendations, not a record that any new test, outreach,
product change or expenditure has been approved or performed.

## 4. Delivery economics — consolidate the model, not the assumptions

Across all five reports, the common concern is that inexpensive student labour
can be outweighed by acquisition, coordination, QA, rework and continuing support.
No supplied report contains measured SkillBridge project-level economics.

Use separate cash and economic views:

```text
Cash contribution = net project revenue − actual cash costs
Economic contribution = cash contribution − unpaid labour valued explicitly

Break-even price = fixed/per-project costs / (1 − proportional fees/reserves)
                  only for costs genuinely proportional to price

Observed acquisition cost per business conversion =
  (outreach cash spend + acquisition hours × stated hourly value) / conversions
```

State treatment of tax, refunds and pass-through expenses. Avoid counting the
same founder hours in acquisition, coordination, QA and an additional founder
line. At zero conversions, observed cost per conversion is undefined, not zero.
A 10% deposit is a financing event, not the full project revenue or proof that
the remaining balance will be collected.

### Recruiter evidence and its limits

benefits from internships, including a 1,248-application study, plus observational

effects remain unproven. Preserve actual artifacts, individual roles, acceptance
and evaluation records before claiming a credential advantage. Reported
motivation or compensation does not measure sustained commitment through exams.

### Historical business rule retained

Use the [full Experiment Card](../../experiment-card.md), not an abbreviated AI
summary. Exactly ten unique qualifying businesses receive a concrete offer with
real available student profiles; one pre-defined pricing method applies, although
scope and price can vary. Both the signed charter and 10% deposit must arrive
within ten days. Non-responses stay in the completed primary denominator.

- **At least 2/10:** continue under the card's delivery/team-formation obligations.
- **Exactly 1/10:** five new qualifying businesses under the same conditions,
  scored separately. Zero additional conversions means stop scaling while
  fulfilling the one secured project; at least one means continue with the
  secured projects and halt further outreach.
- **Confirmed 0/10:** interview decliners and run the historical ten-new-business,
  one-week micro-task/$10 registration-fee branch. This is a new test; the original
  10% conversion definition does not become a fee-registration metric.
- The prescribed kickoff milestone remains **within five business days of the
  decision to continue**. Project completion follows its separately agreed date.

If only some windows have closed, report counts and pending windows without
calling a partial denominator the final primary conversion rate. Missing
signature/payment records are unknown, not evidence of completed conversion.

CL/CX provide a useful statistical critique. Under an illustrative independent,
constant-probability binomial model, the chance of at least two conversions out
of ten is about 8.6%, 26.4%, 62.4% and 85.1% for underlying probabilities of 5%,
10%, 20% and 30%. A two-sided 95% exact interval after 2/10 is approximately
2.5–55.6%. These calculations do not make a selected, mixed-offer cohort
representative. A pass is a minimum behavioural gate, not a validated market
conversion rate or acquisition cost. No threshold is changed by this merge.

## 8. Consolidated validation agenda

### Priorities

The reports differ in whether legal structure or commercial conversion ranks
first. Treat both as consequential prerequisites for a credible paid offer,
while preserving business trust as the confirmed sprint question.

| Priority | Assumption to resolve | Consequence if wrong | Record required |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | A qualifying buyer funds the concrete offer. | Commercial model lacks demonstrated demand. | Original pitch, offer/version, deadline, signature and deposit trail. |
| 2 | A workable contracting/payment/student-status arrangement fits the project. | Proposed offer may be impractical or have different costs/responsibilities. | Actual money/contract flow, applicable institutional/authoritative requirements, written arrangement-specific clarification and quotes. |
| 3 | Delivery can meet acceptance within an affordable full-cost envelope. | Cash-positive work may rely on excessive unpaid support. | All labour, acquisition, QA/rework, direct costs, support and client acceptance. |
| 4 | Suitable students accept and sustain the actual terms. | Seats, deadlines or quality cannot be supplied consistently. | Eligible offers, readiness actions, declines/non-responses, starts, withdrawals and completions. |

This ordering is a synthesis of impact, uncertainty and dependencies, not a
quantitative scoring result. It does not establish that student supply is
already abundant or that it can never become the constraint.

Set project-specific scope, hours, compensation and budgets before work, including the existing kickoff deadline. | A well-documented accepted project can support replication under its measured economics. It does not establish a success rate, annual demand or scale. Evaluate cash and unpaid labour separately. |

Other source proposals remain distinct options: refusal interviews, the
historical zero-conversion micro-task, an independent paid student task, agency
overflow discovery, a founder-time shadow log, and later recruiter comparisons.
Do not pool their outcomes. In particular, PX's completion rate among people who
already accepted a micro-task is a completion diagnostic, not A4 acceptance among
all eligible recipients. A technical practice task without a business buyer is
not commercial conversion.
