### 1. DECISION BRIEF
**Recommendation: Defer scaling the current offer and resolve blocking legal and unit-economic assumptions.** 

The current 2–4-week sprint must focus on establishing a legal framework for student compensation and mapping true delivery economics. Presenting inexperienced students as a commercial web-development service creates an immediate collision between Spanish labor law (which prohibits uncontracted commercial work disguised as training) and business expectations (which demand professional accountability). 

While PyMEs in the Madrid region have a verified need for digital assets, the market is heavily distorted by the *Kit Digital* subsidy program. Businesses prefer certified *Agentes Digitalizadores* whose fees are entirely covered by European funds. SkillBridge cannot currently capture this subsidized demand. Furthermore, even at a theoretical cash break-even, the model relies on unsustainable unpaid founder hours for quality assurance (QA) and project management. 

We must pause outbound commercial sales once the current 10-business experiment concludes, seek qualified local legal counsel regarding *Prácticas Académicas Externas*, and recalculate pricing based on actual QA time.

### 2. BUSINESS DEMAND AND ACCESS

**Promising Initial Segments:**
1. **Micro-PyMEs (1–3 employees):** High need for basic digital presence (landing pages). Often lack the cash flow for agency retainers but might pay a one-off fee below €1,000. Decision-maker: Owner.
2. **Sole Traders (Autónomos):** Extremely high urgency and price sensitivity. Decision-maker: Owner.
3. **Small PyMEs (4–9 employees):** Have established revenue but outdated systems. Higher budgets but demand strict timelines and maintenance guarantees. 

**Current Solutions & Purchasing Process:**
A standard landing page for a Spanish PyME in 2026 costs between €450 and €1,500. Freelancers on platforms like Malt charge an average of €288/day for Front-End development in Madrid. The purchasing process usually involves requesting 2-3 quotes, reviewing portfolios, and signing a service level agreement (SLA) that includes bug fixes. 

**Objections to Student Teams:**
PyMEs will object to the lack of post-handover maintenance, unpredictable delivery schedules (due to exams), and the perceived risk of an unpolished final product reflecting poorly on their brand.

**The *Kit Digital* Distortion:**
As of 2026, Red.es continues to operate *Kit Digital*, offering €3,000 to Segment IV/V (0-3 employees) and €6,000 to Segment III (3-9 employees). **Crucial limitation:** PyMEs must spend this money exclusively through certified *Agentes Digitalizadores*. SkillBridge is not an agent. Therefore, our target businesses must pay out-of-pocket for SkillBridge, while our competitors (agencies) can offer "free" websites subsidized by the government.

### 3. LEGAL FEASIBILITY AND DELIVERY ECONOMICS

**Legal Feasibility (Spain):**
Operating this model in Madrid is highly complex. The team cannot arbitrarily declare work as "training." 
1. **Prácticas Académicas Externas (RD 592/2014):** To be legally classified as an internship, there must be an Educational Cooperation Agreement (*Convenio de Cooperación Educativa*) with the university. The work requires an academic tutor and a company tutor. 
2. **Social Security (RD-ley 2/2023):** As of January 1, 2024, all interns (even unpaid) must be registered with and quote to the Social Security system. 
3. **Employment vs. Freelancing:** If there is no university agreement, the students are performing commercial work. They must either register as *Autónomos* (costly for a student doing a single campaign) or SkillBridge must hire them as part-time employees subject to minimum wage and labor laws (*Estatuto de los Trabajadores*).
4. **Non-EU Students (LO 4/2000, RD 629/2022):** In 2026, international students with a study visa can legally work up to 30 hours per week, but this must be formalized correctly to avoid visa violations.
5. **Liability & IP:** SkillBridge needs contracts transferring IP from the student to the platform, and then to the client.

*Conclusion:* Desk research suggests the current ad-hoc paid model is legally precarious. Qualified local labor advice is mandatory before signing more clients.

### 5. STUDENT COMMITMENT AND RECRUITER VALUE

**Student Commitment:**
Students abandon extracurricular projects when academic pressure (midterms/finals) peaks. Compensation alone does not guarantee retention if the brief is vague. The primary driver of sustained participation in real client work is a strictly enforced timeline with pre-defined, bite-sized milestones, paired with the promise of a verified portfolio asset. 

**Recruiter Value:**
Recruiters do not value "participation." They value traceable competency. The incremental value of SkillBridge over a GitHub personal project is the presence of an external stakeholder. To maximize recruiter value, the final artifact must visibly prove that the student successfully navigated client feedback, scope constraints, and teamwork.

### 6. MARKET ESTIMATE CHECK

**Working Estimate:** 1,950 ≈ 2,000 students annually.
*Check:* The 260,000 proxy represents the *total* university enrollments in the Madrid region across all disciplines (Ministry of Universities Data, 2024-2025). 
- Tech/Informatics comprises roughly 5-7% of overall enrollments, not 10%. 
- The 75% filter for Y2-Y4 is mathematically sound (assuming 4-year degrees and high Y1 dropout rates).
- The 50% assumption for lack of referrals is plausible but unverified.
- The 20% commitment rate is highly aggressive for an unproven extracurricular model; 5-10% is a safer assumption for *sustained* commitment over a 4-week sprint.
*Recalculation:* 260,000 × 6% tech × 75% Y2-Y4 = 11,700 eligible students. 11,700 × 50% = 5,850 needing access. 5,850 × 10% commitment = **~585 ready-to-commit students annually in Madrid**. 
This remains sufficient to support a pilot, but proves that student supply is not the immediate bottleneck; B2B demand and legal mechanics are.

### 8. NEXT EXPERIMENTS

**Experiment 1: Reconcile and Conclude the Existing Business Test**
*   **Hypothesis:** The existing pipeline will yield definitive evidence of client willingness to transact, or clear reasons for refusal.
*   **Method:** Execute the prospectively defined rules. Ensure all 10 pitch windows have closed. If ≥2 convert, build the projects. If exactly 1 converts, pitch 5 more. If 0 convert, conduct exit interviews focusing on *Kit Digital* and trust objections.
*   **Minimum Resources:** 0 cash; 10 hours founder time.
*   **Decision Rule:** Follow the existing flowchart. An inconclusive result (e.g., businesses agree verbally but refuse deposits) means the price/risk ratio is currently unviable.

**Experiment 2: Legal Pathway Validation**
*   **Hypothesis:** There is a viable, low-friction legal mechanism to classify this work and process payments in Spain without becoming a fully liable web agency.
*   **Method:** Consult a Spanish labor lawyer (*asesor laboral*). Present three scenarios: (A) SkillBridge as an intermediary marketplace, (B) SkillBridge establishing *Convenios* with UPM/UC3M/UCM, (C) Hiring students as fixed-discontinuous workers. 
*   **Minimum Resources:** €150 - €300 cash; 5 hours founder time.
*   **Decision Rule:** If no path allows for operating with < 15% legal/tax overhead and without massive liability, pivot the model to a University-funded or SaaS-portfolio model.

**Experiment 3: Founder Time Audit (The "Shadow" Sprint)**
*   **Hypothesis:** A single student campaign requires more than 15 hours of unbilled founder intervention.
*   **Method:** Execute ONE internal or low-risk client project. Log every minute the founders spend on sales, Slack coordination, code review, client management, and payment routing. 
*   **Minimum Resources:** €300 cash (student pay); 20 hours founder time.
*   **Decision Rule:** If founder time exceeds 10 hours per project, the current price point is economically unscalable, requiring either automation, higher prices, or pivot.
