# SkillBridge: 2–4-week validation sprint research

**Research cutoff: 3 October 2026.** This is web desk research only. I reached BOE, Red.es and TGSS content mostly through secondary summaries, which are flagged in the source ledger. It is not legal advice. Spanish source titles are preserved.

---

## 1. Decision brief

**Verdict: narrow and gate; do not scale or invest further in product yet.** During the sprint, finish and reconcile the existing 10-business test. In parallel, resolve the legal operating structure, which blocks any paid delivery. Take no new deposits until a lawful structure and a funding source for student pay both exist.

The only decisive evidence is a signed charter plus a deposit within 10 days, and none is reported. Advertised freelance landing pages run about €400–1,000, and junior freelancers list about €177 per day. At €12–18 per student-hour, a three-student team therefore needs roughly 30–45 total hours to price competitively. Nobody has shown that is achievable. Paid student work in Spain is a regulated choice among university-linked prácticas, autónomo status and employment, not a free one. Kit Digital has no open call. Riipen's and Parker Dewey's paid models rely on subsidy or an employer-of-record structure. Market size is not the binding constraint.

Minimum resources: about 20–30 founder hours and one adviser consultation (fee unknown). Cash at risk is nil if no student is paid.

- **Student survey:** 9 of 11 chose each of the two difficulties (82%; exact 95% CI 48–98%). Only 4 of 11 (36%; CI 11–69%) said they would review a concrete brief, which is intention, not acceptance. City and year were not collected, so eligibility is unconfirmed.
- **Other cohorts:** offer acceptance, student payments, accepted client work, a second campaign and recruiter evaluation have no counts, overlaps, denominators or logs. I treat them as unverified assertions and keep them separate from each other and from the survey.
- **Business test:** no signed charters or deposits are reported. The qualifying-business count and the response windows are unresolved, so I do not score 0/10.
- **Obstacle table:** 23 selections against a maximum of 22, and no raw data attached. It stays unresolved and is excluded from all quantitative conclusions. Web research cannot repair internal records.

**Context.** Only about 37% of firms with fewer than 10 employees have both internet and a website (INE ETICCE, Q1 2025, published 22 Oct 2025; 36.99% for small firms against 84.46% for larger ones) [S3]. DIRCE (1 Jan 2025) counts 901,955 firms with 1–2 employees, 314,941 with 3–5, 132,013 with 6–9, and 134,826 with 10–49 (85,467 + 49,359) [S4]. More than half (54.4%) have no employees. **Prices.** I separate four kinds of evidence:

- **Advertised (seller blogs, low strength):** a freelance landing page runs €400–1,000 and an agency €1,500–4,000. A Madrid freelance landing page is quoted at €600–900. - **Platform-listed day rates (Malt, low to moderate):** IT freelancers with 0–2 years' experience average €177/day. Web designers average €231/day. These are self-set rates from active freelancers, with small Madrid samples [S6].

- **Completed transactions:** not found within this search.

Scope, support, IVA and maintenance are not like-for-like across these figures.

**Kit Digital.** The latest windows (III and IV) closed on 31 October 2025, and Red.es data show no new call as of 31 August 2026. Orden TDF/39/2026 (BOE-A-2026-2070, 28 January 2026) removed the fixed end date and requires reinvestment of unspent funds, but it does not open applications [S1]. Registration of digitalizer agents is a separate procedure. If a call reopened, subsidised spend would likely flow to registered agents, so students could not capture it without a partner (inference). The effect on willingness to pay is untested.

- **Supports:** a large website gap, and price anchors a student offer could undercut.
- **Contradicts:** cheap DIY and freelance substitutes, and a gap that may reflect low perceived need.
- **Missing:** objections to students, quoted prices, Madrid splits.

**Desk research can establish:** the size of the gap, price anchors and subsidy status.
**Only your commercial test can establish:** willingness to sign and pay a deposit to a student team, the content of objections, scope-to-price fit, and channel conversion.

## 4. Legal feasibility and delivery economics

### Arrangement (a): university-linked prácticas

RD 592/2014 governs external academic placements, and each university has its own framework [S10].

- UPM's convenio marco makes the extracurricular stipend mandatory, with a minimum amount the university publishes (BOCM, 23 Oct 2025).
- UC3M requires a signed convenio before any placement and bills the host €50 + IVA per month for management. UC3M also requires 110 completed credits, which roughly excludes second-year students.
- Since 1 January 2024, students in external practicums must be registered with Social Security whether paid or not. For paid placements, the entity funding the training acts as employer [S7].
- A payroll vendor reports 2026 fixed monthly contributions of €69.23 for common contingencies (about €3.46 after the 95% reduction) plus €7.95 for occupational risk, citing Orden PJC/297/2026 [S7]. That figure is moderate-strength and needs checking with TGSS.

Questions for advisers:

- Can SkillBridge be the "host entity" when the deliverable goes to a third-party client?
- Who counts as the "funder" for Social Security purposes?
- Is a formative stipend compatible with client acceptance obligations?
- Is the UC3M management fee per student or per entity? (The page does not say.)

### Arrangement (b): independent autónomo

Most sources give €80/month for the first 12 months. One source reports €88.64 for 2026, so verify with Seguridad Social [S8]. Professional B2B invoices carry 15% IRPF withholding, or 7% in the first three years. A flat €80 equals about 22% of a €360 payout (33% of €240, 15% of €540). Whether SkillBridge directing the work creates employment-like dependence is not researched and needs counsel.

### Arrangement (c): employment

Payroll costs are not modelled and are likely disproportionate for a pilot. Sourced cost: not found within this search.

### Other conditions

- **Non-EU students:** a study stay allows work, employed or self-employed, up to 30 hours/week if compatible with studies, under RD 1155/2024 (in force 20 May 2025) [S9]. Check each student individually.
- **IP and portfolio:** contracts need written IP assignment or licence and student consent for portfolio use. I did not research the specific LPI provisions.
- **GDPR/LOPDGDD:** EDPB guidance treats a service provider processing data for another as a processor, requiring a written Art. 28 contract [S15]. Forms and analytics on a built site trigger this.
- **Liability and insurance:** cost not found within this search.

### Funding models

- **Customer-funded:** see the break-even table below.
- **University-funded:** not found within this search. Universities appear as compliance channels that may charge hosts, not as payers.
- **Subsidised:** Riipen FuturePath pays students $23.34/hour, split 50-50 between programme and employer. Its paid programmes are limited to eligible Canadian students [S11]. Parker Dewey's grant-funded free programme exhausted its funding on 20 May 2026 [S12]. Kit Digital is closed. These are benchmarks, not available Spanish options.

### Break-even model: conditions, not forecasts

Required price = cash cost ÷ (1 − 4%), where 4% is a 3% payment fee plus a 1% refund reserve. Both are assumptions.

| Scenario (n = 3 students) | Student cash cost | Break-even price, cash only | + founder time at €15/h | + founder time at €25/h |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Lean: 40 h total at €12/h; 10 founder h | €480 + €34 SS | ~€536 | ~€692 | ~€796 |
| Mid: 60 h at €18/h; 30 founder h | €1,080 + €34 | ~€1,161 | ~€1,629 | ~€1,942 |
| Heavy: 90 h at €22/h; 45 founder h | €1,980 + €34 | ~€2,098 | ~€2,801 | ~€3,270 |

Assumptions:

- Social Security is one month at the vendor-reported fixed rate (€11.41 per student).
- Hours, pay, refund and fee figures are my illustrative assumptions, not observed data.
- Founder hours of 10, 30 and 45 are labelled assumptions.
- Founder hourly values of €15 and €25 bracket the roughly €22/h implied by Malt's 0–2-year average.
- Excluded: acquisition, insurance and any UC3M-type fee. If that fee is per student, add about €156 per month.
- Acquisition adds founder hours per conversion of (hours per pitch) ÷ (conversion rate). That is 5–10 hours at 20%, 10–20 at 10%, and 20–40 at 5%, with 1–2 hours per pitch assumed.

**Reading the table.** At a €900 price, 60 hours at €15/h leaves a cash margin of about −€70, so no founder time is affordable. At 40 hours and €12/h it leaves about €350, which funds roughly 14–23 founder hours. Competing inside the advertised freelance range (€400–1,000) therefore requires student productivity near a junior freelancer's. That is unproven. Cash and economic costs diverge sharply once founder time is valued.

## 6. Student commitment and recruiter value

### Commitment

Comparable programmes pay on completion with logged hours and approval. Riipen requires employer and programme approval of each invoice. Parker Dewey holds client payment in escrow until completion. No completion, dropout or sustained-participation statistics for any comparable programme were found within this search. Pilot evidence is intention only (4 of 11). Opportunity cost is the freelance rate (about €22/h implied). Team coordination and timing evidence: not found.

### Recruiter value

Audit studies are causal but test a different treatment:

- A Belgian field experiment (1,248 fictitious résumés, Nov 2015–Apr 2016) found a 3-month unpaid internship raised interview invitations by 12.6% [S14].
- A US résumé audit found internships raised callbacks by about 14% [S14].
- **Limits:** the outcome is an initial invitation, not hiring. The treatment is an internship at a named organisation, not a contribution-traced micro-project. Transfer to Madrid software roles is uncertain.

Spanish evidence is observational:

- FBBVA-Ivie U-Ranking (2023) associates extracurricular prácticas with higher job quality.
- Universidad de Granada administrative data compare participants and non-participants a year after graduation.
- An analysis of 2010 graduates' curricular prácticas found shorter job search but no lasting matching or wage gain.

None of these removes selection into prácticas. Informática graduates already show near-full employment, so graduate-level gains may be small. The value would concentrate on first-internship interviews. The incremental value of attributable client work over existing projects was not found within this search. The pilot's "clearer evaluation" is unverified.

## 7. Market estimate check

The Ministry's *Datos y cifras del Sistema Universitario Español 2025/26* (2024-25 data, provisional) lists Madrid on-site enrolments of 261,707 in Grado, 61,238 in Máster and 18,737 in Doctorado [S2]. The regional Grado rows sum to the national on-site total, so UNED and online universities are excluded. Counts are programme enrolments, not de-duplicated people. They cover Madrid campuses, not residents; 31.2% of Madrid on-site Grado students in 2023-24 lived elsewhere. No newer Madrid figure was found. The national 2025-26 forecast is nearly 1.9 million students. Your anchor is correct but Grado-only, provisional and on-site-only.

**Rates.**

- **Relevant programmes:** nationally, Informática is 69,877 of 1,416,388 Grado enrolments (4.9%), all engineering 12.6% and mathematics/statistics 1.4%. Madrid splits were not found, so 10% looks generous unless broad engineering counts.
- **Years 2–4:** public on-site first-year entrants are 24% of enrolment (231,556/970,391), consistent with 70–80%. Cohorts are unequal.
- **No internship or referral access (50%) and ready to commit (20%):** no supporting source found. The 20% is weakest, since the pilot measured intention.

**Sensitivity** (base 261,707; 5–10%, 70–80%, 30–70%, 5–20%): roughly 140 to 2,900 students. Your 1,950 sits inside that range, which spans about 20×.

**Three populations.** The eligible population is the first three filters. The reachable population (course coordinators, placement offices, student clubs, 42 Madrid, your pilot list) is unmeasured. Readiness under a funded offer is observable only through paid-brief acceptance. Funded demand comes from paying businesses, not from dividing students by team size.

## 8. Critique of the ten-business design

With 10 pitches, a pass at 2/10 has low power. The calculation assumes independent pitches with a common conversion probability p. That is a simplification, because the businesses are selected, not sampled.

| True p | P(≥2/10 pass) | P(0/10) |
|---|---|---|
| 5% | 9% | 60% |
| 10% | 26% | 35% |
| 20% | 62% | 11% |
| 30% | 85% | 3% |

Exact 95% intervals are: 0/10 gives 0–31%, 1/10 gives 0.3–44%, and 2/10 gives 2.5–56%. In the 1/10 branch, five more pitches yield at least one conversion with probability 23% at p=5%, 41% at 10% and 67% at 20%.

A signed charter plus a deposit is strong evidence of an individual conditional commitment. It is weak evidence about a regional conversion rate. Mixed scopes and prices add noise. A refundable 10% deposit is a modest financial commitment. Acquisition cost per conversion could plausibly run from 5 to 20+ pitches. Do not retrofit thresholds; new experiments can set new prospective rules.

## 10. Three proposed experiments

These are drafts only. Do not contact anyone or spend money until approved. Offers must reflect real delivery capacity and funding, with no deceptive commitments.

### E1 — Reconcile and complete the business test (assumption 2; gated by 1 and 5)

| Field | Specification |
|---|---|
| Recruitment | Original qualifying businesses only. Pitch any remaining ones only after E2 passes and funding is in writing. |
| Hypothesis | At least 2 of 10 unique qualifying businesses sign a charter and pay a 10% deposit within 10 days (existing rule). |
| Method | Audit the pitch log: dates, window closure, signature timestamps, bank deposit records. Apply the existing branches unchanged. |
| Observable action | Signed charter plus recorded deposit within 10 days. |
| Numerator / denominator | Conversions ÷ unique qualifying pitches with closed windows (non-responses stay in the denominator). |
| Window | 10 days per pitch, plus 5 business days to kickoff. |
| Minimum cash / hours | €0 beyond deposit handling; about 6–10 hours to reconcile, plus 1–2 hours per remaining pitch (assumed). |
| Decision rule | Existing thresholds (≥2 continue; exactly 1 test five more; 0 investigate refusals). |
| If inconclusive | Any unrecoverable record means "unscored". Report k conversions of m closed pitches. Never score 0/10. |

### E2 — Legal-structure feasibility check (assumption 1; also informs 5)

| Field | Specification |
|---|---|
| Recruitment | Two placement offices (those of recruited students), one gestor or labour/tax adviser, and one data-protection check. |
| Hypothesis | At least one structure is confirmable in writing at a known all-in cost within model assumptions. |
| Method | Send an identical written scenario (money flows, tutor, client sign-off, IP, data) and request written answers on host entity, Social Security payer, fees and IP. |
| Observable action | Written replies and fee quotes. |
| Numerator / denominator | Structures confirmed in writing ÷ 3 asked (prácticas, autónomo, employment). |
| Window | 7–14 days. |
| Minimum cash / hours | Adviser fees unknown (get a quote before committing); 6–10 team hours. |
| Decision rule | One confirmed structure with a known cost: proceed to E3 setup. None, or conflicting answers: defer product investment. |
| If inconclusive | Conditional or slow replies mean the gate stays closed and no deposits are taken. |

### E3 — Funded delivery trial and student commitment (assumptions 3 and 4; also 5)

| Field | Specification |
|---|---|
| Recruitment | 4–6 students, verified Madrid, years 2–4, software/web/data (and study-stay status if non-EU), via university channels. Starts only after E1 yields at least 1 signed and funded project, or founders pre-commit funds in writing, and E2 passes. |
| Hypothesis | A 2–3 student team delivers one accepted landing page by the deadline within hour limits that leave a cash margin ≥0 at the sold price. |
| Method | Written participation agreement, time logs for all hours, milestone acceptance, a 14-day support log, and an exit survey. |
| Observable action | Students sign and complete milestones; the client gives written acceptance. |
| Numerator / denominator | Students completing all milestones ÷ students signed; accepted on-time campaigns ÷ started; total hours against the model. |
| Window | 3–5 weeks from kickoff; the sprint covers setup and the first milestone. |
| Minimum cash / hours | Student pay for one campaign (modelled €400–1,200); about 30–45 founder hours; E2 legal costs. |
| Decision rule | Continue if cash cost ≤ about 80% of price, founder hours are affordable at the chosen rate, and at least 2 of 3 students complete. Narrow if only some conditions hold. |
| If inconclusive | With n=1–2 this is cost observation, not a rate. Repeat once before changing price. |

**Consent and privacy across all three:**

- Use a lawful basis and minimal data.
- Take separate consents for participation, time logs, portfolio publication and client-name use.
- Store data in the EU with a stated retention period and a withdrawal route.
- Give students and clients written, accurate terms on pay, funding and deposit refunds.
