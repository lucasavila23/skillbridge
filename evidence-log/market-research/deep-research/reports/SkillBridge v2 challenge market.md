The evidence supports SkillBridge v2's product thesis and undercuts its lead revenue hypothesis. Demand for a hard-to-fake, company-calibrated work sample is real and getting stronger. UK graduate employers now get **140 applications per vacancy (205 in digital/IT)**, the highest since records began. A third of them are redesigning recruitment because of AI. Measured cheating on remote assessments roughly doubled in 2025. And no commercial platform certifies an individual's verified contribution inside a team. The market's payment pattern is close to unanimous in the other direction, though. Forage, HackerRank certifications, Kaggle, Handshake and Devpost hackathons are free to students. Companies, universities or sponsors pay. Students pay only for prep that promises a better outcome (LeetCode, Exponent) or for brand-name vendor exams (AWS, Microsoft at about $100). In Spain the student problem is acute: **24.5% under-25 unemployment**, **about 36% graduate over-qualification**, and **49% of students have never done an internship**. Those same students live on €800–1,500 a month. Most outcome numbers in this space ("4x more likely to get the job") are vendor claims with self-selected samples. The independent evidence is thinner and more modest: a Stanford RCT found that making a certificate visible on LinkedIn raised new employment by about 6%. Practically, SkillBridge should treat "company pays for the challenge, student gets the certificate free or for a small optional fee" as the default model, and test the student fee rather than build on it. Its defensible core is integrity plus individual attribution, not AI scoring, which incumbents already ship.

**How to read the labels.** Every figure carries one tag. **[independent]** means academic, analyst, employer-association or non-profit research with no commercial stake in the result. **[official statistics]** means a statistics office, central bank, ministry or regulatory filing. **[vendor claim]** means a platform or seller reporting on itself or its market, with no independent audit. **[secondary]** means a figure taken from press, an aggregator or a pricing tracker rather than the primary source. **[unverified]** means a figure whose origin or method could not be traced, or which comes from paid market-research press releases. Vendor claims are kept in separate sentences or table rows from independent evidence throughout.

## 1. Companies host challenges to screen at volume and build brand, and almost nobody audits the results

### Five motives, one dominant: screening a flooded early-career funnel

Companies use challenge platforms for five reasons. The first is a screening and hiring pipeline for early-career candidates. The second is employer branding among students. The third is developer adoption of their API or product (the hackathon-sponsor motive, funded from DevRel or marketing rather than HR budgets). The fourth is open innovation (Kaggle, Topcoder). The fifth is diversity hiring. The pressure behind the first motive is well documented. ISE's 2025 survey of UK graduate employers counted **140 applications per graduate vacancy and 205 for digital/IT roles [independent]**, against 38 in 2002/3 ([ISE survey 2025](https://ise.org.uk/_userfiles/pages/files/reports/student_recruitment_survey_2025.pdf); [ISE top 10 stats 2025](https://ise.org.uk/knowledge/insights/513/ise_top_10_stats_of_2025_you_need_to_know/)). The same body found that **79% of employers are redesigning or reviewing recruitment, one-third because of AI**, and **77% say former interns are more work-ready [independent]** ([ISE](https://ise.org.uk/knowledge/insights/513/ise_top_10_stats_of_2025_you_need_to_know/)). That last figure is the strongest independent argument for "see real work before you hire."

The vendors' own pitches match those motives. Forage sells employers "reducing early-stage workload and strengthening employer brand visibility" through short virtual job simulations, free to students **[vendor claim]** ([Seramount: Forage](https://seramount.com/hidden); [About Forage](https://www.theforage.com/about)). Kaggle pitches Featured Competitions as open innovation and reports **500+ hosted [vendor claim]** ([Kaggle](https://kaggle.com/c/about/featured)). Unstop says Walmart hired **300+ women through its "CodeHers" challenge [vendor claim]** ([Unstop blog](https://unstop.com/blog/walmart-flipkart-hires-juspay-gives-interview-offers-to-2473-engineers-through-hiring-challenges/amp)). In Spain, NUWE (Barcelona, founded 2020) sells gamified challenges and hackathons to Schneider Electric, Oracle, Mobile World Capital and CaixaBank, and raised a €739k second round **[secondary]** ([El Referente](https://elreferente.es/inversiones/nuwe-cierra-segunda-ronda-inversion-739-000-euros/)). Barcelona's publicly backed Talent Arena (launched March 2025, alongside MWC) now hosts the GSMA Open Gateway hackathon. That shows the API-adoption motive operating locally ([Mobile World Capital](https://mobileworldcapital.com/en/nota-de-prensa/the-first-edition-of-talent-arena-will-unite-the-global-developer-community-at-montjuic-venue-of-fira-during-mwc-barcelona-2025/)).

### What outcomes are reported: almost all vendor, all self-selected

The outcome data comes from platforms marketing themselves, and the numbers don't agree with each other. Forage claims simulation completers are **2x more likely to be interviewed and 4x more likely to receive an offer [vendor claim]** ([Seramount](https://seramount.com/hidden)). Elsewhere it says **"3.3x more likely to get hired" [vendor claim]** ([Forage/Seramount one-pager, 2024](https://seramount.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/12/Forages-Ecosystem-Approach-to-Talent-Acquisition-vf.pdf)), and on its About page "up to **2.6x more employable**" **[vendor claim]** ([About Forage](https://www.theforage.com/about)). None of these discloses a method. Each compares motivated completers with all applicants. Unstop reports Juspay issuing **2,610+ pre-placement offers** through hiring hackathons, but another Unstop headline gives **2,473 interview offers** for the same programme **[vendor claim]** ([Unstop](https://unstop.com/blog/walmart-flipkart-hires-juspay-gives-interview-offers-to-2473-engineers-through-hiring-challenges/amp)). HackerEarth claims **70% offer acceptance** for hackathon-sourced candidates **[vendor claim]** ([Unstop/HackerEarth metrics](https://unstop.com/blog/hackathon-success-metrics)). The research found **no independent study** of cost-per-hire, time-to-hire or challenge-to-offer conversion for any challenge platform.

### The dominant pain is AI fraud, concentrated at entry level

CodeSignal's proctoring data shows cheating or fraud attempts rising from **16% (2024) to 35% (2025)**, and **from 15% to 40% for entry-level** assessments **[vendor claim; CodeSignal also sells the detection product]** ([CodeSignal](https://codesignal.com/newsroom/press-releases/codesignal-detection-systems-identify-and-stop-record-high-cheating-attempts-as-assessment-fraud-more-than-doubled-in-2025/)). ISE's independent employer survey points the same way at lower levels: **cheating encounters doubled from 7% to 15%**, and **48% of employers worry that graduates' AI use misrepresents their abilities [independent]** ([ISE](https://ise.org.uk/knowledge/insights/513/ise_top_10_stats_of_2025_you_need_to_know/)). Greenhouse data, reported second-hand, has **39% of US hiring managers adding in-person interviews** and **only 19% "extremely confident" they would catch fraud [secondary]** ([FabricHQ](https://fabrichq.ai/blogs/state-of-cheating-in-interviews-in-2026-tools-trends-and-prevention)). Judging time, low-quality submissions and IP concerns come up often in organiser guidance, but nobody has quantified them.

**So what for SkillBridge.** Lead the company pitch with "screen a flooded funnel with real work you can trust," and put employer brand second. Skip "free innovation": short student challenges won't deliver it, and buyers know that. Price in the gap between HackerRank's ~$79–529/month screening tooling and Parker Dewey's $5k+ programmes. A per-challenge fee in the low hundreds to low thousands of euros fits that band. SaaS companies give you a second buyer, DevRel, who will pay for a challenge that requires using their API. Never quote Forage-style multipliers as proof. Instruct the pilot to log interview and offer rates for certified participants against other applicants to the same roles, because that comparison is missing from the whole market. The scoring design has to answer the fraud question explicitly, or employers will discount the certificate exactly where SkillBridge operates: entry level.

## 2. Students join to learn and get noticed, finish when stakes are real, and resent free-riders

### Motives: learning and career value, with prizes as the hook

The academic literature keeps finding the same motives. Students join hackathons to learn (mostly "incidentally, opportunistically, and from peers"), to network, to win prizes and to strengthen their CV, with fun and recognition close behind. Warner & Guo's survey of **256 college students [independent]** is the anchor study ([ICER 2017](https://pg.ucsd.edu/publications/student-perceptions-of-college-hackathons_ICER-2017.txt)), backed by later work on student hackathons ([arXiv 2402.05339](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2402.05339); [Springer 2024](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-031-53227-6_15)). When choosing a specific online hackathon, Devpost's participants rank **prize amount first, what they'll learn second, and the date and time commitment third [vendor claim]** ([Devpost via HackerNoon](https://hackernoon.com/hack-the-hackathon-what-devs-look-for)). Sponsor brand matters a lot. In TechTogether's self-survey, **65% said sponsorship made them more likely to apply to the sponsor [vendor claim, organiser self-survey, n=289, ~10% response]** ([TechTogether 2021](https://techtogether.mlh.io/2021_EmploymentReport.pdf)).

### Completion: low when free and open, much higher when something is at stake

Measured completion is low in open formats. Reich & Ruipérez-Valiente's analysis of 12.67M edX registrations found **3.13% overall completion in 2017–18, but 46% among paying "verified" learners [independent]** ([MIT TSL](https://tsl.mit.edu/research/the-mooc-pivot/); [Inside Higher Ed](https://www.insidehighered.com/node/7744)). In one Devpost online hackathon, **15% of 1,340 registrants submitted [vendor claim, single event]** ([HackerNoon](https://hackernoon.com/hack-the-hackathon-what-devs-look-for)). Forage simulations take about **2–6 hours [vendor claim, via university career pages]** ([Northeastern](https://careers.northeastern.edu/article/forage)), which is close to SkillBridge's 2–7 hour window. Forage reports **10M+ "engagements" [vendor claim]**, but no completion rate ([EAB](https://eab.com/about/newsroom/press/forage-job-simulations-surpass-10-million-student-engagements/)). Non-attendees name five deterrents: physical discomfort, lack of substance, an overly competitive atmosphere, an unwelcoming culture, and too little prior experience **[independent]** ([Warner & Guo](https://pg.ucsd.edu/publications/student-perceptions-of-college-hackathons_ICER-2017.txt)). Gender gaps run as wide as **1:15 women to men** at hackathons **[independent, literature review]** ([IEEE](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/abstract/document/9235555)). Curated leagues close much of the gap: MLH reported **almost 40% women or non-binary participants in 2020 [vendor claim]** ([MLH](https://blog.mlh.com/the-state-of-the-league-2020-09-08-2020)).

### Teams: free-riding is common, and structure reduces it

**63% of 250 undergraduates** perceived social loafing in group work **[independent]** ([Jimma University](https://repository.ju.edu.et/handle/123456789/6281)). The most disruptive behaviours were silence in meetings and skipping the final presentation **[independent]** ([Hacettepe](https://avesis.hacettepe.edu.tr/yayin/b5b5deb7-61f8-4cc0-9f79-b1777fece530/social-loafing-behaviors-of-university-students-in-group-works-the-reasons-and-possible-solutions)). Neither of those shows up in a commit log. Introducing structured peer assessment cut **free-riding complaints from 26% to 7% [independent]** ([JoSoTL](https://scholarworks.iu.edu/journals/index.php/josotl/article/view/2133)). Git metrics on **150 students in 8 projects** exposed unequal contribution **[independent]** ([UCR study](https://kimuk.conare.ac.cr/Record/KUCR_be02f4a97fa61e3ff36650a6df6e2165?lng=es)), but research also warns that objective git metrics and peer ratings often don't correlate **[independent]** ([CLEI](https://ejbeta.clei.org/cleiej/article/view/502)). One more caution for attribution: only **about 9% of code in hackathon repos was created during the event [independent]** ([EMSE 2022](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10664-022-10201-x)).

### Outcomes for students: self-reported only

TechTogether respondents said **31% got an interview and 13% an internship or job offer "because of" the hackathon [vendor claim, self-reported]** ([TechTogether](https://techtogether.mlh.io/2021_EmploymentReport.pdf)). No independent causal study links hackathon, Kaggle or simulation participation to employment.

**So what for SkillBridge.** In the student-facing copy, sell three things in this order: what you'll learn, which named company sees your work, and the exact time commitment. The 2–7 hour daytime format already removes two of the five named deterrents (sleepless events and wasted time). The real company brief addresses a third ("lack of substance"). For completion, a payment, a named employer and a hard deadline are the levers the MOOC data supports. Plan funnels at roughly 15% registrant-to-submitter for free open challenges and expect more only where stakes exist. The contribution record should combine commit and diff signals with CATME-style peer ratings, and it should be visible *during* the challenge as a deterrent. It also needs to separate code written in the window from code brought in. Beginner tiers and scored-not-ranked framing are the evidence-backed fixes for the gender and confidence gaps.

## 3. The entry gate narrowed while the queue grew, and Spain shows it most sharply

### Global: fewer junior roles, more applicants, weaker CV signal

The "experience paradox" is now documented with current data. SignalFire finds new grads are **7% of Big Tech hires, down more than 50% from 2019**, and **under 6% at VC-backed startups**. It describes companies "posting junior roles but filling them with senior ICs" **[independent, VC-published industry data]** ([SignalFire, May 2025](https://www.signalfire.com/blog/signalfire-state-of-talent-report-2025)). US software-development postings sat **about 35–36% below pre-pandemic levels in mid-2025 [independent, Indeed Hiring Lab]** ([Hiring Lab Q2 2025](https://www.hiringlab.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/HiringLab-B2B_Q2_03.pdf)). Using ADP payroll data, Brynjolfsson, Chandar & Chen measured a **13% relative employment decline for 22–25-year-olds in the most AI-exposed jobs**, software developers included **[independent]** ([Stanford/SIEPR, Aug 2025](https://siepr.stanford.edu/publications/working-paper/canaries-coal-mine-six-facts-about-recent-employment-effects-artificial)). Recent US graduates had **5.6% unemployment and 42% underemployment in Q2 2026 [official statistics, NY Fed]** ([NY Fed](https://www.newyorkfed.org/research/college-labor-market)). Career-office analysts argue these studies can't fully separate AI from interest rates and post-pandemic over-hiring ([Yale SOM](https://cdo.som.yale.edu/blog/2025/10/17/is-ai-killing-the-early-career-job-market-parsing-the-data-from-three-high-profile-studies/)). The cause is contested. The narrowing isn't.

Internships are the main way in. **63.1% of 2024-25 US interns converted to full-time hires [independent, NACE]** ([NACE 2026](https://naceweb.org/talent-acquisition/internships/intern-conversion-rate-hits-highest-mark-in-five-years)), and in-person interns convert better than hybrid ones (**58.5% vs 46%**) **[independent]** ([NACE 2025](https://naceweb.org/talent-acquisition/internships/intern-offer-and-conversion-rates-fall-acceptances-rise)). Internships themselves are getting scarcer, though: NACE forecast a **3.1% fall in intern hiring for 2025 [independent]** ([NJBIA citing NACE](https://njbia.org/survey-finds-intern-hiring-expected-to-dip-3-1-in-2025/)). Meanwhile volume per vacancy is climbing. On Handshake, students sent **16.5 applications each (+21%)** while postings fell **15%** and applications per vacancy rose **30% [vendor claim, secondary]** ([Business Insider NL](https://www.businessinsider.nl/graduates-are-taking-a-closer-look-at-smaller-companies-in-a-competitive-and-scary-job-market/)). UK entry-level tech postings are **down 31.9% since ChatGPT's launch [secondary]** ([The New Stack](https://thenewstack.io/whats-happening-with-entry-level-development-jobs/)). AI-written applications make CVs less informative: about **two-thirds of US candidates use AI to apply**, and **80% of recruiters rate incoming applications "mediocre at best" [secondary]** ([The Decoder](https://the-decoder.com/recruiters-are-drowning-in-a-flood-of-ai-generated-applications/)). Students without networks also miss the referral channel, where referred candidates are hired at about **30% vs about 7%** from other sources **[secondary, aggregator restating older Jobvite data]** ([Erin](https://erinapp.com/blog/employee-referral-statistics-you-need-to-know-for-2024-infographic)). The popular "75% of CVs auto-rejected by ATS" claim has no traceable source and should not be used.

### Spain and Madrid: high unemployment next to an unfilled tech shortage

Spain's under-25 unemployment was **24.54% (440,400 people) in Q1 2026 [official statistics, INE EPA]** ([INJUVE](https://injuve.es/sites/default/files/adjuntos/2026/07/EPAPResultados_2026-1T_16a24_NotInfo.pdf)), against **14.8% in the euro area [official statistics, Eurostat]** ([Eurostat](https://ec.europa.eu/eurostat/web/products-euro-indicators/w/3-04032026-ap)). Monthly Eurostat put Spain at **23.4% in June 2026, second in the EU [secondary]** ([uni2study](https://www.uni2study.com/articles/spain-italy-greece-youth-unemployment-20-2026)). Madrid does better: about **19.6% for under-25s in Q1 2025 [official statistics, older, unverified against the PDF table]** ([INJUVE 2025](https://injuve.es/sites/default/files/adjuntos/2025/07/EPAPResultados_2025-1T_16a24_NotInfo.pdf)). Higher education protects young people, with unemployment of about **6.8% [secondary]** for those with degrees ([Tesify](https://tesify.es/paro-juvenil-universitario-espana-2026-estadisticas-oficiales)), but it doesn't protect job quality. About **36% of Spanish graduates work below their qualification level, the highest rate in the EU [independent, Fundación CYD via press]** ([Aprendemas](https://www.aprendemas.com/es/blog/mundo-educativo/espana-es-el-pais-de-la-ue-con-mas-sobrecualificacion-el-36-de-los-graduados-ocupa-una-posicion-inferior-a-sus-estudios-167418); [Informe CYD 2025](https://www.fundacioncyd.org/descarga/10584/informe-cyd-2025/informe-cyd/)). At the same time the industry association DigitalES estimates **about 120,000 unfilled ICT vacancies, about 40,000 in software [secondary, date unclear]** ([Telefónica Think Big](https://blogthinkbig.com/por-que-hay-120-000-vacantes-de-empleo-tic-en-espana)). InfoJobs–Esade report that **just over 20% of 2024 job offers were in IT, and one in three candidates seeks IT work [independent, job-board data]** ([Esade](https://www.esade.edu/es/noticias/el-informe-infojobs-esade-estado-del-mercado-laboral-en-espana-2025-confirma-la-demanda-de)).

Students say what's missing. In the March 2025 "Ruta 44" survey with UCM's entrepreneurship chair (**n=400**), **about 75% said their training doesn't prepare them for work, 49% had never done an internship, 66.8% wanted more practical training, and 71.5% named the prior-experience requirement as the main barrier [independent]** ([Infocop](https://www.infocop.es/falta-de-practicas-reales-y-de-preparacion-laboral-principales-carencias-de-la-formacion-universitaria-segun-el-alumnado/)). Regulation could tighten supply further. The Estatuto del Becario bill went to the Congreso on 3 March 2026 with fines of up to **€225,018 [official]** for serious breaches, but it isn't in force ([El Debate](https://www.eldebate.com/economia/20260303/gobierno-da-luz-verde-estatuto-becario-manda-congreso-aprobacion-definitiva-cambia_391589.html); [Wolters Kluwer](https://www.wolterskluwer.com/es-es/expert-insights/estatuto-becario-cambios-empresas)).

**So what for SkillBridge.** The core story for Spain is a signalling failure: high youth unemployment and over-qualification alongside about 120k unfilled ICT roles. Position SkillBridge as proof of work for students who can't get the internship that would normally provide it, a substitute for the "experience required" line that 71.5% of students name. Present the 77% "former interns are more work-ready" figure and the in-person versus hybrid conversion gap together. Both show employers value having *seen* someone work, and a graded real challenge is the cheapest way to deliver that. If the Estatuto passes, internships become more costly and more regulated, so non-employment proof of skill becomes more valuable. Treat that as upside, not a premise. For a Madrid pitch, use the Spain-wide figures and say plainly that Madrid-specific 2026 data wasn't found.

## 4. Employers say they value skills; their hiring behaviour barely moves, and AI is eroding remote tests

### The say-do gap is the central credibility risk

Vendor surveys report near-universal adoption of skills-based hiring. TestGorilla finds **85% of employers use it and 76% use skills tests**, but only **16% use work-sample assignments [vendor claim]** ([TestGorilla 2025](https://www.testgorilla.com/skills-based-hiring/state-of-skills-based-hiring-2025/)). The behavioural evidence tells a different story. Harvard Business School and the Burning Glass Institute tracked **11,300 large US firms** and found skills-based hiring changed outcomes in **"not even 1 in 700 hires"** in 2023, with **about 45% of firms dropping degree requirements "in name only" [independent]** ([Harvard Project on Workforce](https://pw.hks.harvard.edu/post/skills-based-hiring-the-long-road-from-pronouncements-to-practice); [HCAMag](https://www.hcamag.com/ca/specialization/recruitment/in-name-only-firms-dropping-degree-requirements-fall-short-in-practice/477331)). NACE reports that **nearly 90% of new-grad employers look for problem-solving evidence and more than 80% for teamwork [independent]** ([NACE](https://naceweb.org/about-us/press/the-attributes-employers-look-for-on-new-grad-resumes-and-how-to-showcase-them)). Those two attributes are exactly what SkillBridge claims to verify.

### Micro-credentials: vendor enthusiasm, modest but real independent effect

Coursera's commissioned survey reports **96% of employers say a micro-credential strengthens an application** and **90% would pay 10–15% more [vendor claim]** ([Coursera](https://blog.coursera.org/new-coursera-report-shows-strong-employer-and-student-roi-for-industry-micro-credentials-higher-starting-salaries-greater-work-readiness-reduced-training-costs)). Google says **75% of its 250,000+ US certificate graduates report a positive career outcome within six months [vendor claim, self-reported, no control group]** ([Google](https://blog.google/company-news/outreach-and-initiatives/grow-with-google/google-career-certificates-impact-report-may-2025/)). The stronger independent evidence is Athey & Palikot's randomised trial with **800,000+ Coursera certificate earners**. Making the credential easier to share on LinkedIn raised the probability of a new job within a year by **about 6% (+8–9% for jobs related to the certificate)**, concentrated among the least employable learners (**+12% for the bottom tercile**) **[independent]** ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.00247v1)). EU and US bodies identify trust as the main barrier. Cedefop calls employer trust "a key policy challenge" **[independent]** ([Cedefop](https://www.cedefop.europa.eu/en/news/labour-market-value-microcredentials-conduit-better-work)). Northeastern finds **38% of employers struggle to understand what a credential represents, 36% to validate that it was earned, and 32% find quality too varied to trust [independent]** ([Northeastern](https://cps.northeastern.edu/wp-content/uploads/2024/08/NU_MicrocredentialsSummaryReport_08-21-2024.pdf)). No Spanish employer survey comparing certificates with degrees was found. Spain regulates university micro-credentials in line with the 2022 EU Council Recommendation, which sets standards for stackability, portability and verification through European Digital Credentials ([European Commission](https://education.ec.europa.eu/education-levels/higher-education/micro-credentials); [Educaweb](https://www.educaweb.com/noticia/2025/01/30/formaciones-cortas-aprendizaje-permanente-21740/)).

### Portfolios and take-homes: low trust, now damaged by AI

Recruiters skim. The initial CV screen in Ladders' eye-tracking study averaged **7.4 seconds [vendor claim, 2018]** ([HR Dive](https://www.hrdive.com/news/eye-tracking-study-shows-recruiters-look-at-resumes-for-7-seconds/541582)). Only **11% of recruiters and 23% of developers say portfolio review reflects on-the-job ability [vendor claim, CoderPad 2026]** ([CoderPad](https://coderpad.io/state-of-tech-hiring-2026/)), largely because nobody can tell who did the work. AI has made the problem worse. In interviewing.io's experiment, **73% of candidates secretly using ChatGPT passed verbatim-LeetCode screens and no interviewer caught one [vendor claim]** ([interviewing.io](https://interviewing.io/blog/how-hard-is-it-to-cheat-with-chatgpt-in-technical-interviews)). Gartner predicts **1 in 4 candidate profiles will be fake by 2028**. In its survey, **6% of candidates admitted interview fraud** and **29% of AI-using applicants used it for assessment answers [independent, analyst]** ([Gartner](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2025-07-31-gartner-survey-shows-just-26-percent-of-job-applicants-trust-ai-will-fairly-evaluate-them)). Google reinstated at least one in-person round for engineers in 2025 **[secondary]** ([CXO Digital Pulse](https://www.cxodigitalpulse.com/google-reinstates-in-person-job-interviews-amid-rising-ai-cheating-concerns/)). Candidates dislike the current tests too. **77% of developers say assessments don't reflect job skills [vendor claim, HackerRank, 13,700+ respondents]** ([HackerRank](https://www.hackerrank.com/writing/real-world-software-skills-interview-challenges-vs-algorithm-tests)). Note that only **26% of candidates trust AI to evaluate them fairly [independent]** ([Gartner](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2025-07-31-gartner-survey-shows-just-26-percent-of-job-applicants-trust-ai-will-fairly-evaluate-them)), which matters for an AI-rubric product.

Hackathon hiring works best for the company that ran the event. Platform data (HackerEarth, Unstop) shows strong conversion when the hiring company hosts **[vendor claim]**, and no independent evidence shows that generic hackathon wins or Kaggle ranks raise callbacks elsewhere.

**So what for SkillBridge.** Recruiters will *say* a SkillBridge certificate is valuable, and that statement predicts little. Design the credential to plug into a step employers already use, such as an interview shortlist or a fast-track, rather than asking them to reweight their screen. The credibility stack, from strongest to weakest, is: a named company set and signed off the rubric; a one-click LinkedIn share plus a verification link (the RCT's mechanism); EU/Europass and Open Badges-compatible metadata; process evidence against AI fraud (commit history, a short live defence of the submission); and individual contribution evidence. The certificate is likely worth most to the issuing company and much less to third-party employers. That points to "hiring screen" as the stronger revenue line. The "AI rubric" claim should be balanced with human sign-off in student-facing copy, given that only 26% of candidates trust AI grading. Verified team contribution has no standard and no employer-valuation evidence. Treat it as a hypothesis to test in employer interviews.

## 5. Students won't pay for the signal itself; €10–30 is the ceiling worth testing

Everything SkillBridge's certificate competes with is free to the student. HackerRank offers **21 free skill certifications [vendor claim]** ([itknowledgelab](https://itknowledgelab.com/blog/hackerrank-free-skills-certifications-developers-2026)). Forage is "100% free, student-first" **[vendor claim]** ([Forage](https://www.theforage.com/about)). Kaggle and most student hackathons cost nothing. Students pay in three bands, and each band buys something different:

| What students buy | Price | Label |
|---|---|---|
| Interview prep (LeetCode Premium) | $35/mo or $159/yr; $99/yr group student plan ([Lodely](https://www.lodely.com/blog/leetcode-premium-cost)) | secondary |
| Interview prep (Exponent; interviewing.io mocks) | $79/mo; ~$225 per mock session ([Lodely](https://www.lodely.com/blog/exponent-pricing); [Lodely](https://www.lodely.com/blog/interviewing-io-pricing)) | secondary |
| Learning subscriptions (Coursera Plus; Google certificates) | $59/mo or $399/yr; $49/mo, ~€250 total ([listpoint](https://www.listpoint.co.uk/is-coursera-worth-it-complete-2024-review-of-pricing-features-and-career-value/); [emprendedores](https://emprendedores.es/gestion/google-universidad)) | secondary |
| LinkedIn Premium Career (EUR) | ~€30–40/mo ([waalaxy](https://blog.waalaxy.com/en/linkedin-premium-cost/)) | secondary |
| Brand-name vendor exams | AWS Cloud Practitioner $100; Microsoft Fundamentals ~$99 with 30% student discount ([AWS handbook](https://www.awscertificationhandbook.com/guides/aws-cloud-practitioner-exam-cost/); [Microsoft Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-au/answers/questions/1826328/how-can-i-avail-student-discount-for-microsoft-cer)) | secondary / vendor claim |
| Madrid bootcamps | ~€4,500–7,500+ ([genbeta](https://www.genbeta.com/desarrollo/ironhack-formando-a-los-programadores-que-las-empresas-necesitan)) | secondary |

Stated value doesn't translate into spending. **83% of US community-college students say certifications validate their skills [vendor claim, CompTIA]**, but that survey didn't measure willingness to pay ([CC Daily](https://www.ccdaily.com/2025/03/students-say-industry-recognized-certifications-provide-value/)). Freemium benchmarks are sobering. Duolingo, a best-in-class case, converts about **9% of monthly active users to paid [secondary, reporting company results]** ([Motley Fool](https://www.fool.com/investing/2026/02/16/3-key-takeaways-from-duolingos-2025/?.tsrc=rss)). Coursera's FY2025 10-K shows consumer revenue of **$502M growing through subscriptions while one-off Specialization purchases declined [official statistics, SEC filing]** ([SEC](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1651562/000165156226000015/cour-20251231.htm)). That suggests one-off certificate purchases are the weaker motion. Spanish students live on about **€800–1,500 a month [secondary]** ([studyineurope](https://www.studyineurope.eu/study-in-spain/for-british-students/cost-of-living/)), with **37% going on accommodation** for students living away from home **[official statistics, EUROSTUDENT VIII cross-country average]** ([Eurostudent](https://www.eurostudent.eu/download_files/documents/Eurostudent8_2024_240618_Chapter_B8_bf.pdf)). Bizum has about **30M users and is Spain's #2 e-commerce payment method [secondary]** ([Infobae/EFE](https://www.infobae.com/espana/agencias/2026/01/19/el-volumen-de-operaciones-con-bizum-crecio-un-53-en-2025-y-se-situo-en-67751-millones/)), so a small one-off payment is easy to make.

The business-model evidence lines up. Handshake is free for students and charges universities roughly **$8,000/year** plus large employers for branding **[secondary]** ([Sacra](https://sacra.com/research/handshake)). Forage charges employers. Triplebyte, which ran employer-paid, assessment-vetted matching, shut down in March 2023 after chasing volume ([Karat](https://connect.karat.com/tb-candidates); [Dealroom](https://app.dealroom.co/news/note/triplebyte-venture-scale-destroys-product)). Candidate payment shows up only for prep, where the student expects a better outcome. It doesn't appear for the signal itself.

**So what for SkillBridge.** The lead hypothesis, "students pay a certification fee first," runs against every comparable model found. It also creates a credential-mill incentive: if students pay, the platform is pushed to pass them, and that damages the employer trust the certificate depends on. Northeastern's 32% "quality too varied" and 36% "hard to validate" findings are the risk here. The recommended test has three parts. Make the base certificate free, funded by the company that posts the challenge. Test an optional student fee of **€10–30** (one month of LeetCode or Coursera), via Bizum, for extras such as a detailed engineer feedback report, a verified LinkedIn badge bundle or retake attempts. Never charge for passing. Run the €100 anchor only once the issuing companies are brand names, because €100 competes with AWS and Microsoft. Also treat the team's n=17 stated-value survey as a ceiling, not a forecast.

## Implications for the ERRC grid and value curve

The evidence suggests these moves against the incumbent factors (Devpost/MLH hackathons, HackerRank/CodeSignal tests, Forage simulations, Kaggle, NUWE).

| ERRC | Factor | Evidence basis |
|---|---|---|
| **Eliminate** | Overnight, multi-day event format | Physical discomfort and time cost are named deterrents [independent] |
| **Eliminate** | Charging students for the pass/score itself | Every comparable signal is free; credential-mill risk [vendor claim + independent trust data] |
| **Eliminate** | Unsupported "Nx more likely to be hired" claims | Forage figures conflict (2.6x/3.3x/4x) and lack method [vendor claim] |
| **Reduce** | Prize size as the main draw | Prize ranks first for choosing an event [vendor claim], but learning and career value dominate stated motives [independent] |
| **Reduce** | Winner-takes-all ranking | Competitive atmosphere deters women and newcomers [independent] |
| **Reduce** | Engineer judging hours per submission | AI pre-score plus top-N human review; judging time is the hidden sponsor cost (unquantified) |
| **Raise** | Integrity and provenance of the score | Entry-level fraud 15%→40% [vendor claim]; employer cheating encounters 7%→15% [independent]; Google back to in-person [secondary] |
| **Raise** | Employer involvement and brand on the credential | Hosting company's own events convert best [vendor claim]; sponsorship drives intent to apply, 65% [vendor claim] |
| **Raise** | Visibility where recruiters look (LinkedIn, Europass) | Stanford RCT, +6% new employment [independent] |
| **Raise** | Job realism of the task | 77% of developers say tests aren't job-relevant [vendor claim]; employers value observed work, 77% interns more work-ready [independent] |
| **Create** | Verified individual contribution inside teams | No commercial platform offers it; free-riding at 63% [independent]; peer assessment cut complaints 26%→7% [independent] |
| **Create** | Company engineer sign-off on an AI rubric | Only 26% of candidates trust AI-only evaluation [independent] |
| **Create** | Stackable, EU-standard profile across challenges | 2022 EU Recommendation on stackability and portability [official] |
| **Create** | Measured outcome reporting (interview/offer rates vs other applicants) | No independent outcome data exists anywhere in the category |

On the value curve, SkillBridge should sit **low** on event length, prize emphasis and student price, where Devpost and MLH sit high on the first two. It should sit **high** on integrity, job realism and employer involvement. HackerRank is high on integrity but low on realism. Forage is high on realism and employer brand but low on integrity and individual differentiation. SkillBridge should be **uniquely high** on individual team attribution and human-signed AI grading, where every incumbent is at zero. The one factor where the evidence argues for changing the plan rather than the curve is price. Move "student pays" from the lead revenue line to an optional, tested add-on, and lead with the company paying per challenge, with the hiring screen and sponsorship stacked on top.

## Conclusion

The research changes the question SkillBridge v2 should ask. "Will students pay to be certified?" is mostly answered: not for the signal itself, and not at scale. The better question is "Will a Spanish SaaS or data company pay a few hundred to a few thousand euros to receive pre-graded, provenance-checked work from students it would never otherwise see?" Everything independent in this evidence base (record application floods, employers redesigning processes around AI, the value of having watched someone work) points to yes. But no study yet proves it, which is why measured interview and offer rates from the first pilots would be SkillBridge's most valuable asset and the one thing no competitor publishes.

The second shift concerns what the moat is. AI rubric scoring is standard everywhere now, and remote scores are losing credibility. The defensible product is the trust layer around the score: a named company's sign-off, process evidence that the student did the work, and an individual contribution record for team challenges that no commercial platform offers. That last feature is also the least validated, since no employer has yet been shown to value it. Test it early in employer interviews, before it becomes the headline of the pitch.
