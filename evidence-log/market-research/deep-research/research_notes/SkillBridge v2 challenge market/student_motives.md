# Student (supply-side) motives and behaviour on challenge platforms: hackathons, coding/data competitions, virtual job simulations

Research date: 2026-10-12. Labels: **[Independent]** = peer-reviewed or academic; **[Vendor]** = a platform or organiser reporting on its own product; **[Org survey]** = a non-profit or organiser self-survey; **[Official stats]** = a statistics office. "SR" = self-reported; "Measured" = observed or behavioural data.

## 1. Why students take part: motivations

### Takeaway
Across the studies, the same four motives keep coming up: learning or skill-building (often learning from peers rather than formal teaching), prizes, networking or meeting people, and career or CV value, with fun and recognition close behind. When students pick a specific online hackathon, Devpost's own survey ranks prize amount first, what they will learn second, and the date and time commitment third.

### Cited Findings
- **[Independent, 2017]** Warner & Guo, "Hack.edu" (ICER 2017). Methods: 6 interviews, 256 college-student survey responses (42% female), and observation at one event. Attendees gave both social and technical reasons for going. Learning happened "incidentally, opportunistically, and from peers" rather than through formal instruction. — [Warner & Guo, ICER 2017](https://pg.ucsd.edu/publications/student-perceptions-of-college-hackathons_ICER-2017.txt)
- **[Independent]** In a 308-questionnaire study (public-sector hackathons and citizen-sourcing), recognition, learning, financial rewards and fun shaped attitudes toward taking part. — [BAR / ANPAD](https://bar.anpad.org.br/index.php/bar/article/view/353)
- **[Independent]** Participants are drawn by "the possibility of winning prizes, learning new technologies, and meeting new people to expand their network." — [SBC/SEMISH paper](https://sol.sbc.org.br/index.php/semish/article/download/11312/11175)
- **[Independent]** Nolte (Springer chapter) ran a cross-sectional survey of hackathon participants covering their motives (building a startup product, advancing their career) and perceived benefits (learning, networking). Participants with entrepreneurial motives were more likely to found a startup afterwards. — [Springer 2024 chapter](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-031-53227-6_15)
- **[Independent, 2020]** Simonofski, Amaral de Sousa, Clarinval & Vanderose, "Participation in Hackathons: A Multi-Methods View on Motivators, Demotivators and Citizen Participation" (Belgium; interviews plus quantitative methods). I could not retrieve the abstract details. — [UNamur research portal](https://researchportal.unamur.be/en/publications/participation-in-hackathons-a-multi-methods-view-on-motivators-de/fingerprints/)
- **[Independent, 2024]** A CHASE 2024 short paper (arXiv 2402.05339) on software-engineering students found that qualitative themes centred on networking, team dynamics and skill development. — [arXiv 2402.05339](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2402.05339)
- **[Vendor, Devpost, 2023-06-24]** Survey of participants in Devpost's own online hackathon. Top criteria for choosing a hackathon: (1) prize amount, (2) what they will learn, (3) date and time commitment. Most time-consuming activities: (1) building the project, (2) coming up with an idea, (3) learning the required tools. Most helpful resources: example project code, chat support, and relevant project ideas. No percentages were published. — [Devpost via HackerNoon](https://hackernoon.com/hack-the-hackathon-what-devs-look-for)
- **[Independent]** Kaggle and competition-based learning: course studies report that Kaggle competitions raised intrinsic motivation, engagement and learning satisfaction. Research on Kaggle data found that community activity, past team experience and knowledge sharing predict whether people keep participating. One large-scale study covered 350,956 users across 2,789 competitions over 4 years. — [JSDSE 2021 (DOAJ)](https://doaj.org/article/e7576c71173b4bd5886b4eb7b8571213); [XJTLU CBL case study](https://scholar.xjtlu.edu.cn/en/publications/unlocking-the-potential-of-competition-based-learning-a-case-stud/); [MWAIS 2018](https://aisel.aisnet.org/mwais2018/46)
- **[Org survey, TechTogether 2021]** 38% of respondents list a TechTogether hackathon project on their résumé (SR). — [TechTogether 2021 Employment Report](https://techtogether.mlh.io/2021_EmploymentReport.pdf)
- **[Vendor, Stack Overflow 2024]** This survey does not ask about hackathons as a way of learning. Among people learning to code: 84.9% use other online resources, 55.6% school, 46.8% online courses or certifications, and 13.6% coding bootcamps. — [Stack Overflow 2024 Developer Survey](https://survey.stackoverflow.co/2024/developer-profile/)

### Inferences
- Prizes attract registrations, but learning and career value make up most of the stated motivation. A 2–7 hour paid company challenge should advertise three things: what the student will learn, the career signal it gives, and the time commitment. The last is a selection criterion in its own right.
- "Learning from peers" is a core benefit of hackathons. A solo-only format would lose it, so team options matter for motivation.

### Gaps
- I found no public MLH survey that breaks out participant motivations as percentages. MLH reports cover reach and demographics only (see §4).
- I did not retrieve the Kaggle "State of ML & Data Science" items on why people compete. That survey mainly asks about tools and jobs.
- I found no JetBrains Developer Ecosystem item on hackathons or competitions.

## 2. Completion, dropout and time spent

### Takeaway
Measured completion in open or self-paced formats is low. About 3% of all MOOC registrants completed in 2017–18, against about 46% of those who paid. In one Devpost online hackathon, 15% of registrants submitted. Forage simulations take roughly 2–6 hours, but I found no public figure for how many starters finish.

### Cited Findings
- **[Independent, Measured, 2019]** Reich & Ruipérez-Valiente, "The MOOC Pivot," *Science* 363(6423):130–131, Jan 2019. Dataset: all MIT and Harvard edX courses from 2012–2018, covering 5.63M learners and 12.67M registrations. Completion did not improve over 6 years. — [MIT TSL](https://tsl.mit.edu/research/the-mooc-pivot/); [GitHub data](https://github.com/jruiperezv/MOOC_Pivot)
  - Overall completion fell from about 6% (2014–15) to 3.13% (2017–18). — [Inside Higher Ed](https://www.insidehighered.com/node/7744)
  - Completion among paid "verified" learners was 46% in 2017–18, down from 56% in 2016–17 (about 50% in earlier years). — [Inside Higher Ed](https://www.insidehighered.com/node/7744)
  - Return rate: of 1.1M first-time MOOC takers in 2015–16, only 12% took another MOOC in 2016–17. The share of first-timers who came back fell from 38% (2012–13) to 7% (2016–17). — [Inside Higher Ed](https://www.insidehighered.com/node/7744)
- **[Vendor, Devpost, 2023, Measured, single event]** 7,873 site visitors, 1,340 registrants, 213 submissions from 202 individuals. That is a "submitter conversion rate of 15%" on a $3,500 prize budget. — [Devpost via HackerNoon](https://hackernoon.com/hack-the-hackathon-what-devs-look-for)
- **[University career pages repeating vendor info]** A Forage simulation takes "around five hours" on average. Other pages say 2–3 hours or 5–6 hours. — [WashU McKelvey](https://mckelveyconnect.washu.edu/blog/2023/11/06/forage-virtual-job-simulations/); [Northeastern](https://careers.northeastern.edu/article/forage); [UW Careers 2026](https://careers.uw.edu/blog/2026/07/08/forage-build-real-skills-with-employer-designed-job-simulations-4/)
- **[Vendor, EAB/Forage]** Forage simulations passed 10 million "student engagements." This counts engagements, not completions. — [EAB press release](https://eab.com/about/newsroom/press/forage-job-simulations-surpass-10-million-student-engagements/)
- **[Independent, Measured]** Nolte et al. surveyed 178 hackathon participants whose code was reused, 120 whose code was not, and 118 OSS developers who reused hackathon code. A "considerable number" of projects continue after the event, and short-term and long-term continuation are distinct. Only about 9.14% of code blobs in hackathon repos were created during the event. — [EMSE 2022, Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10664-022-10201-x); [NSF PAR](https://par.nsf.gov//servlets/purl/10341999)

### Inferences
- Plan funnels on measured benchmarks: roughly 15% of registrants submit in a low-stakes online hackathon, and roughly 3% complete a free MOOC. When something is at stake (payment, a named employer, a fixed deadline), completion rises sharply, as the 46% verified-learner figure shows. Short, deadline-bound company challenges should sit at the high end, but this is unproven.
- The roughly 9% in-event code share suggests hackathon output builds on prior work. Contribution records should separate code written during the challenge from code brought in.

### Gaps
- Forage does not publish its start-to-completion rate. I found no independent study of virtual job simulation completion.
- I found no cross-platform benchmark for registrant-to-submitter rates (Devpost, MLH, Kaggle). The 15% figure comes from one event.
- I found no independent time-on-task data for 2–7 hour challenges.

## 3. Team dynamics: free-riding, contribution attribution, peer assessment, git analytics

### Takeaway
Free-riding is common in student teams; about 63% perceived it in one survey. Structured peer evaluation such as CATME reduced free-riding complaints from 26% to 7% in one study. Git metrics can show unequal contribution, but they only measure code.

### Cited Findings
- **[Independent]** 63% of 250 undergraduates (Mettu University, Ethiopia) perceived social loafing in their group work. Four kinds were identified: being unavailable, tech loafing, poor work quality, and not contributing to discussion. — [Jimma University repository](https://repository.ju.edu.et/handle/123456789/6281)
- **[Independent]** In a survey of 374 university students, the most disruptive loafing behaviours were staying mostly silent in meetings and skipping the final presentation. Both wasted teammates' time and pushed extra work onto them. — [Hacettepe (avesis)](https://avesis.hacettepe.edu.tr/yayin/b5b5deb7-61f8-4cc0-9f79-b1777fece530/social-loafing-behaviors-of-university-students-in-group-works-the-reasons-and-possible-solutions)
- **[Independent]** CATME (Comprehensive Assessment of Team Member Effectiveness) is a web-based peer-evaluation tool. It rates five dimensions: contributing to the team's work, interacting with teammates, keeping the team on track, expecting quality, and having relevant knowledge, skills and abilities. — [ASEE PEER](https://peer.asee.org/17542); [UBC CATME report](https://elearning5.sites.olt.ubc.ca/files/2021/05/catme-report.pdf)
- **[Independent]** After a peer-assessment process was introduced, free-riding complaints fell from 26% to 7%. Instructors saw clear value; students were more divided. — [JoSoTL, Feb 2013](https://scholarworks.iu.edu/journals/index.php/josotl/article/view/2133)
- **[Independent, 2020]** Work on individualising teamwork scores from peer assessment came out of student feedback that equal team grades felt unfair. — [arXiv 2008.03854](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2008.03854)
- **[Independent, 2021]** A University of Costa Rica study used git metrics on 8 projects with 150 students. Visualising aggregated metrics with inequality measures and per-module contribution exposed unequal contribution, uneven development pace and poor integration practices. The authors present it as a feedback tool for instructors and students. — [Using git metrics to measure students' and teams' code contributions (2021)](https://kimuk.conare.ac.cr/Record/KUCR_be02f4a97fa61e3ff36650a6df6e2165?lng=es)
- **[Independent]** Team skills fit and diversity was one of three core factors in whether a project continued after a corporate hackathon. — [AJIC (Wits)](https://ajic.wits.ac.za/article/view/15564)

### Inferences
- A "verified record of each member's contribution" targets a pain students already report (free-riders, unfair equal grades). The evidence supports combining two signals: behavioural (commits, task logs) and peer-rated (CATME-style dimensions). Git covers code only, and the most disruptive loafing behaviours (silence in meetings, skipping the presentation) never show up in commits.
- Showing contribution data while the challenge is running acts as a deterrent, not just a post-hoc record. This matches the 26%-to-7% drop in complaints.

### Gaps
- I found no study of free-riding specifically within hackathon teams of 2–36 hours. All the evidence comes from semester-length course projects.
- I found no evidence that employers trust or use peer-assessed contribution scores.

## 4. Barriers: time, confidence, gender and diversity, format, cost, location

### Takeaway
The barriers named most often are: feeling underqualified or imposter syndrome, a competitive or unwelcoming culture, physical discomfort (overnight events with no sleep), and doubts that hackathons have substance. Gender gaps are large in general hackathons and much smaller in organiser-curated student leagues, where MLH reported almost 40% women or non-binary participants in 2020.

### Cited Findings
- **[Independent, 2017]** Non-attendees cited five deterrents: physical discomfort, lack of substance, an overly competitive atmosphere, an unwelcoming culture, and not enough prior coding experience. — [Warner & Guo, ICER 2017](https://pg.ucsd.edu/publications/student-perceptions-of-college-hackathons_ICER-2017.txt)
- **[Independent, literature review]** Women-to-men ratios at hackathons can reach 1:15, against about 1:5 in engineering academia or industry. Women attend 2.36 hackathons on average and men 4.28. Reasons for lower participation by women: imposter syndrome rooted in gender stereotypes, the image of hackathons as sleepless events for specialists, low self-esteem in STEM, toxic environments, and competitive atmospheres. Measures that helped: women-only events, gender-balanced admission, and social or feminist themes. — [Towards gender balance in modern hackathons (UNAM repo)](https://rdigef.unam.mx/handle/rdigef/972); [IEEE 9235555](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/abstract/document/9235555); [ASEE gender-diverse hackathons](https://peer.asee.org/an-investigation-into-gender-diverse-populations-in-hackathon-environments.pdf)
- **[Vendor/Org, MLH, 2020-09-08]** MLH 2020 season: 95,000 hackers and 1,200+ events. Almost 40% identified as female or non-binary, up 18% year on year, compared with about 20% female in US CS enrolment. 4% identified as Black or African and 7% as Latino. Fewer than 10% had attended an online hackathon before. 45,000+ hackers lost internships or jobs to COVID-19, and 40% expected to enter industry underprepared. — [MLH State of the League 2020](https://blog.mlh.com/the-state-of-the-league-2020-09-08-2020)
- **[Org survey, TechTogether]** On average 50% of TechTogether hackers (a gender-focused event series) had never attended a hackathon before, which suggests targeted formats bring in newcomers. TechTogether's report states that women and non-binary representation rose 18% to 39% from 2019 to 2020, attributing this partly to TechTogether and citing the MLH 2020 report. — [TechTogether 2021 Employment Report](https://techtogether.mlh.io/2021_EmploymentReport.pdf)
- **[Vendor, Devpost 2023]** Date and time commitment is the third-ranked criterion for choosing a hackathon. — [Devpost via HackerNoon](https://hackernoon.com/hack-the-hackathon-what-devs-look-for)
- **[Independent]** A study of participant experience looked at confidence and anxiety in hackathons (European Journal of Education, DOI 10.1111/ejed.12656). I could only see the title. — [CiteDrive listing](https://www.citedrive.com/en/discovery/navigating-the-hackathon-exploring-participant-experience-confidence-and-anxiety)

### Inferences
- A 2–7 hour daytime challenge format directly avoids two named deterrents: physical discomfort and time cost. A clear brief from a real company addresses "lack of substance." Offering solo or team entry, with beginner-tier challenges, lowers the confidence barrier.
- To counter the gender gap, the literature supports deliberate design: tiers for newcomers, balanced team formation, and less competitive framing (scored and certified rather than winner-takes-all).

### Gaps
- I found no hard data on cost or travel as barriers for students (most student hackathons are free, and some reimburse travel). Location is implied in Warner & Guo but not quantified.
- I found no recent (2023–2026) MLH demographic report. The 2020 figures are the latest I located.

## 5. Do hackathons, competitions or job simulations lead to interviews or jobs? (self-reported vs measured)

### Takeaway
Almost all of the outcome evidence is self-reported or comes from vendors. TechTogether: 31% got an interview and 13% got an offer "because of" the hackathon (SR, n=289, about 10% response rate). Forage says completers are 3.3 times more likely to be hired (vendor, methodology not disclosed). I found no independent causal study linking hackathon or simulation participation to employment.

### Cited Findings
- **[Org survey, SR, 2021]** TechTogether 2021 Employment Survey, n=289 (about 10% of 2020–21 attendees, so self-selection is likely; the report itself says to treat results as "trends"). Results:
  - 31% got an interview because of TechTogether or at an event.
  - 13% got an internship or full-time offer because of TechTogether.
  - 38% have a hackathon project on their résumé.
  - 84% say it pushed them toward a CS, computer engineering or IT major, minor or bootcamp; 87% toward tech employment.
  - On sponsors: 65% are more likely to apply to a sponsoring company, 38% more likely to recommend it, 28% more likely to accept a role there, and 10% say sponsorship has no effect.

  — [TechTogether 2021 Employment Report](https://techtogether.mlh.io/2021_EmploymentReport.pdf)
- **[Vendor, Forage/EAB, 2024]** "Foragers who complete job simulations with their target companies are 3.3x more likely to get hired compared to traditional applicants." Also: 86% "gained practical skills" and 95% have "a better understanding of the company" (both SR). No methodology is disclosed, and completers self-select, so this is not causal. — [Forage/Seramount (EAB) one-pager, ©2024](https://seramount.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/12/Forages-Ecosystem-Approach-to-Talent-Acquisition-vf.pdf)
- **[University pages repeating vendor claims]** Completers are said to be "more than twice as likely to get an interview and three times more likely to receive an offer" than people applying to the same company without the simulation. An older "71% more likely to be hired" figure also circulates. These differ from the 3.3x figure, and I could not trace the primary source or date for either. — [Villanova](https://connections.villanova.edu/resources/forage/); [Barry University](https://www.barry.edu/en/cel/forage-job-simulations/)
- **[Vendor blogs, low reliability]** Reskilll (an Indian hackathon platform) claims "92,000+ registrations," that people attend 3 hackathons on average before landing an internship, and interview-to-hire rates above 60%. The methodology is not shown. — [Reskilll blog](https://blogs.reskilll.com/how-hackathons-get-you-hired-real-data-92000-registrations/)
- **[Anecdotal, 2015]** A local newspaper reported that over 50% of students at a UC Santa Cruz hackathon got job offers. This is old and unverified. — [City on a Hill Press](https://cityonahillpress.com/2015/01/07/what-can-you-create-in-46-hours/)
- **[Independent]** Entrepreneurship outcome: participants with entrepreneurial motives were more likely to found startups after hackathons (survey association, not causal). — [Springer 2024 chapter](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-031-53227-6_15)

### Inferences
- The strongest credible signal is that sponsorship drives intent to apply (65% SR). For students, the company brand attached to a challenge is a large part of the draw.
- Vendor "x-times more likely" claims compare self-selected, highly motivated completers with all applicants. SkillBridge should not repeat them as proof that the format works, and should plan to collect its own measured outcomes (interview and offer rates for certified participants compared with others applying to the same roles).

### Gaps
- I found no independent, measured or causal study (for example, matched cohorts or employer ATS data) of hackathons, Kaggle competitions or virtual job simulations and hiring outcomes.
- Forage does not publish denominators, the comparison-group definition or dates for its hiring multipliers.

## 6. Spain / EU

### Takeaway
Spain has active student hackathons but almost no published research on why students take part. HackUPC (Barcelona) is the largest, with 700–900 participants per edition. Spanish students strongly feel they lack practical experience: about 75% feel unprepared and 49% have never done an internship (2025). That gap is SkillBridge's opening.

### Cited Findings
- **[University press]** HackUPC is described as Spain's first and largest student hackathon, organised by the student association HackersUPC at UPC Barcelona: 36 hours, teams building solutions to company challenges. Editions: about 900 students from around the world in 2023; 750+ from about 25 countries in 2024; 700+ in 2025 (2–4 May 2025). — [UPC press 2023](https://www.upc.edu/en/press-room/news/900-students-from-all-over-the-world-will-participate-in-the-2023-hackupc-to-solve-technological-challenges-in-36-hours); [FIB UPC 2024](https://www.fib.upc.edu/en/news/750-hackers-all-over-world-have-participated-hackupc2024)
- **[University press]** Around 20 Universitat de Lleida EPS students (the LleidaHack association) took part in HackUPC 2025 and won prizes, which shows a feeder pattern through university student associations. — [UdL EPS](https://eps.udl.cat/pdflink/ca/eb86524b-8c57-11e5-b827-7fb253176922/Estudiants-de-lEPS-membres-de-LleidaHack-guanyen-diversos-premis-a-la-HackUPC-i-la-LauzHack-2025.pdf)
- **[Independent/academic-agency, March 2025]** "Ruta 44" report by BTOB with Universidad Complutense de Madrid's Chair of Entrepreneurship and Innovation. n=400 university students aged 18–26 across Spain.
  - About 75% say their training does not prepare them for work; only 24.5% give a positive answer on job readiness.
  - 49% have never done an internship, and 66.8% say more practical training is needed.
  - About 71.5% say Spain does not support young talent enough, naming the prior-experience requirement as the main barrier.
  - 82.3% would emigrate for work, 65% of them preferring Europe.

  — [Infocop](https://www.infocop.es/falta-de-practicas-reales-y-de-preparacion-laboral-principales-carencias-de-la-formacion-universitaria-segun-el-alumnado/)
- **[Media]** A 2021 report found that six in ten Spanish university students do not feel prepared to work. — [Diari de Tarragona, 2021-04-12](https://www.diaridetarragona.com/sociedad/79413/seis-de-cada-diez-universitarios-no-se-ven-preparados-para-trabajar-20210412-0006-yidt202104120006_amp.html)
- **[Official stats, Eurostat, published 2024-02-22]** In 2023, 55% of EU residents aged 16–74 had at least basic digital skills, ranging from 83% (Netherlands) to 28% (Romania). Rates were 80% for the highly educated and 34% for those with low education. The age and sex breakdown is reported inconsistently: the Eurostat news page fetched shows ages 25–34 at 71% for women and 69% for men, while search snippets for the same item say ages 16–24 at 73% and 69%. Verify before quoting. — [Eurostat news 2024-02-22](https://ec.europa.eu/eurostat/web/products-eurostat-news/w/ddn-20240222-1); [Eurostat "Skills for the digital age"](https://ec.europa.eu/eurostat/statistics-explained/index.php/Skills_for_the_digital_age)

### Inferences
- Spanish students say outright that their main obstacle is "experience required" (71.5%) and the lack of real practice. Short real-company challenges with certified individual contribution speak directly to that framing, more so than prizes. This matches the stated motivations globally (§1).
- The HackUPC/LleidaHack pattern suggests recruiting students through university hacker and student associations.

### Gaps
- I found no survey on motivations or barriers specific to Spanish or EU hackathon participants. HackUPC and HackathonES (not located) publish participant counts only.
- I found no Spain-specific Eurostat breakdown for ages 16–24, and no data on Erasmus or EU participation in hackathons or competitions.
- I found no Spanish usage data for Forage, Kaggle or Devpost.
