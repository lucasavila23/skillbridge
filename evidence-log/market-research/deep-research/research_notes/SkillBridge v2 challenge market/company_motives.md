# Company (demand/sponsor) side of challenge platforms: motives, pricing, outcomes, pain points

Research date: 2026-10-12. Searches were US-indexed web search. "VENDOR CLAIM" marks figures a platform reports about itself, with no independent audit.

## Q1. Why do companies host challenges, hackathons, data competitions and job simulations on third-party platforms?

### Takeaway
Across platforms, five motives come up again and again: (1) a screening/hiring pipeline, above all for early-career candidates; (2) employer branding and awareness among students; (3) developers adopting the company's API or product (hackathon sponsors); (4) open innovation, i.e. solving a real data/ML problem (Kaggle, Topcoder); (5) diversity hiring (women-only coding challenges). Companies also use these platforms to push early-funnel work (role education, first-round screening) out of the recruiting team at scale.

### Cited Findings
- Forage describes itself as a "candidate experience platform for emerging talent" combining career discovery, skilling and recruiting. Employers use short, self-paced virtual job simulations so candidates "explore the role… before they apply". The pitch to employers is "reducing early-stage workload and strengthening employer brand visibility while streamlining the recruiting process from screening to interview to offer" (VENDOR CLAIM, from Forage's listing on Seramount/NACE buyers' guide) — [Seramount: Forage Job Simulations](https://seramount.com/hidden); [NACE buyers guide](https://ebiztest.naceweb.org/buyers-guide/detail.aspx?ID=87285)
- Forage's revenue model: always free for students, and companies pay to host programmes. Forage is now part of EAB — [About Forage](https://www.theforage.com/about)
- Hackathon sponsor motives as listed by organiser playbooks: recruiting ("watching how people think and collaborate"), product/API adoption (developers building on the platform, finding edge cases, becoming advocates), brand visibility, community goodwill, innovation. The recommended ROI metrics for API sponsors are API-key activation rate and 7-day retention, not vanity metrics (industry/organiser guidance, not independent research) — [BuilderBase hackathon playbook ch.3](https://intercom.help/builderbase/en/articles/14831408-hackathon-playbook-ch-03-business-model); [ch.17 sponsor ROI](https://intercom.help/builderbase/en/articles/14831425-hackathon-playbook-ch-17-sponsor-roi-reporting); [AngelHack: organise or sponsor?](https://angelhack.com/blog/should-i-organise-or-sponsor-a-hackathon-a-complete-guide/)
- Kaggle positions Featured Competitions as open innovation: "hundreds or even thousands of data scientists will compete to solve your problem". It has hosted 500+ featured competitions, and hosting includes technical advice, custom evaluation metrics and marketing across Kaggle (VENDOR) — [Kaggle: Featured competitions](https://kaggle.com/c/about/featured)
- Diversity hiring: Walmart reportedly hired 300+ female coders through its "CodeHers" coding challenge run on Unstop (VENDOR CLAIM, from an Unstop case-study blog) — [Unstop blog](https://unstop.com/blog/walmart-flipkart-hires-juspay-gives-interview-offers-to-2473-engineers-through-hiring-challenges/amp)
- Nuwe (Barcelona, founded 2020) sells gamified programming challenges/hackathons so companies can "retain and hire the best technical talent". It was started by four engineers frustrated with traditional technical interviews. Named clients: Schneider Electric, Oracle, Mobile World Capital, CaixaBank — [El Referente, Nuwe €739k round](https://elreferente.es/inversiones/nuwe-cierra-segunda-ronda-inversion-739-000-euros/)
- The Spanish government open-data portal frames hackathons as "a new way of capturing talent" (Feb 2022, older than the preferred window) — [datos.gob.es](https://datos.gob.es/es/blog/los-hackathons-una-nueva-forma-de-captacion-de-talento)
- Context that creates demand: UK employers received 140 applications per graduate vacancy in 2025, the highest since ISE records began in 1991. Digital/IT roles got 205 per vacancy (ISE Student Recruitment Survey 2025) — [ISE survey 2025 PDF](https://ise.org.uk/_userfiles/pages/files/reports/student_recruitment_survey_2025.pdf); [ISE top 10 stats 2025, 8 Dec 2025](https://ise.org.uk/knowledge/insights/513/ise_top_10_stats_of_2025_you_need_to_know/)
- 79% of ISE employers are redesigning or reviewing their recruitment processes. One-third are redesigning *because of AI*. 77% say former interns are more work-ready (ISE, Dec 2025) — [ISE top 10 stats 2025](https://ise.org.uk/knowledge/insights/513/ise_top_10_stats_of_2025_you_need_to_know/)

### Inferences
- The strongest pitch for SkillBridge's buyers is likely screening at volume plus employer brand with students. Application floods (140–205 per vacancy) make "see real work before the interview" valuable. "Innovation" (free solutions) is a weaker pitch for short student challenges.
- The "former interns are more work-ready" finding (77%) supports a claim that short real-work challenges stand in for internship signal. It is still an inference that a 1–2 week challenge carries comparable signal.
- API/product adoption is a separate buyer (DevRel/marketing budget, not HR). For SaaS companies, SkillBridge could sell challenges that require using the company's API.

### Gaps
- No primary statements found from Codility, CodeSignal, Bright Network, Wonsulting or Devpost case studies on stated employer motives (not fetched within the tool-call budget).
- No independent survey (SHRM/LinkedIn/Gartner/NACE) found that ranks motives for hosting challenges specifically.

## Q2. What do these platforms charge companies?

### Takeaway
There are two pricing families. Assessment SaaS publishes list prices (HackerRank roughly $79–$529/month). Challenge, hackathon and simulation platforms (Devpost, Kaggle, Forage, Nuwe, Unstop) mostly price on request. Published or reported figures cluster at low four figures per month for a hosted public hackathon, five figures for recruiting programmes, and $1k–$50k+ for sponsoring a hackathon.

### Cited Findings
- **HackerRank** (2025/2026): Starter $79/month billed annually ($99 monthly), 5 candidate attempts/month. Pro $419/month annually ($529 monthly), 30 attempts/month. Extra attempt $20. Enterprise is custom (USD; aggregated from pricing trackers, cross-check on hackerrank.com) — [Lodely: HackerRank pricing 2026](https://www.lodely.com/blog/hackerrank-pricing-2026); [PricingSaaS: HackerRank](https://pricingsaas.com/companies/hackerrank); [G2](https://www.g2.com/products/hackerrank/pricing)
- **Devpost**: no public corporate price list. Public online hackathons come in Basic/Mid/Full-service tiers plus marketing packages. One aggregator cites a platform fee of **$1,500/month** plus optional services (unverified third-party figure, date unclear). "Devpost for Teams" (private/internal hackathons) is an annual per-seat subscription. Student hackathons can often run free — [G2: Devpost for Teams pricing](https://www.g2.com/products/devpost-for-teams/pricing); [Stork.ai: Devpost](https://www.stork.ai/en/devpost)
- **Kaggle**: Featured Competition fees are not published. Kaggle's revenue comes from organisations paying to host. A scoping guide covers budget, data and timeline. Academic and non-profit hosts can apply for reduced or free hosting through the Research Grants Program — [Kaggle: Featured](https://kaggle.com/c/about/featured); [Kaggle "Meet Kaggle" slides](https://www.kaggle.com/static/slides/meetkaggle.pdf). Historical prize budgets: a $1M lung-cancer detection competition (Jan 2017, outside the preferred window) — [TechCrunch 2017](https://techcrunch.com/2017/01/12/kaggle-hosting-1m-competition-to-improve-lung-cancer-detection-with-machine-learning/amp/)
- **Topcoder**: on the reported pay-as-you-go model, the customer sets the prize and Topcoder adds an admin fee of 20% of the prize, capped at $250 (e.g. $200 web design prize + $40 fee). TopCrowd SaaS: Free (≤10 active users), Professional $9/active user/month, Enterprise on request. Enterprise contracts were described as >$200,000 with a fixed monthly platform fee plus per-project fees. LOW CONFIDENCE: these come from a job-board summary and a Harvard Digital Initiative student post, not Topcoder's own pricing page, and dates are unclear — [Betterteam: Topcoder](https://betterteam.com/topcoder); [Harvard D3: Topcoder](https://d3.harvard.edu/platform-digit/submission/topcoder-an-original-platform-for-crowdsourcing-solutions/)
- **Parker Dewey** (micro-internships, US): the employer sets the project fee. The average 10-hour project costs $222.22, and most are listed at $200–$600 (~$20–25/hr). Parker Dewey keeps 10% and the student gets 90%. Posting is free. An optional annual subscription starts at **$5,000**, and team recruiting programmes run **$10,000 to $100k+**. There is no fee on conversion to hire (VENDOR, published pricing) — [Parker Dewey pricing](https://www.parkerdewey.com/pricing); [Parker Dewey FAQ](https://www.parkerdewey.com/faq)
- **Riipen FuturePath** (Canada): the employer pays $11.67/hour per student and the programme matches it, so the student gets $23.34/hour. The deposit is 50% of the total owed, up to $750 for the first two learners (CAD presumed; source does not state currency) — [Riipen help: FuturePath employer payments](https://help.riipen.com/en/articles/11174197-riipen-futurepath-employer-payments)
- **Hackathon sponsorship/hosting budgets** (organiser guidance, USD, ~2024–2025):
  - Recommended starting budget for hosting is ~$10,000.
  - Medium events (100–200 participants): sponsorship tiers from Silver $1k–3k to Platinum $5k–15k.
  - Large events (500+): Silver $3k–10k to Platinum $15k–50k+.
  - Sponsoring an existing hackathon costs $500–$5,000. University hackathons charge a few hundred dollars, and Major League Hacking events reach low five figures.
  - Sponsorship typically funds 50–80% of a hackathon budget.
  - Sources: [BuilderBase playbook ch.3](https://intercom.help/builderbase/en/articles/14831408-hackathon-playbook-ch-03-business-model); [ch.5 finding sponsors](https://intercom.help/builderbase/en/articles/14831423-hackathon-playbook-ch-05-finding-sponsors); [Deepak Gupta sponsorship guide](https://guptadeepak.com/hackathon-sponsorship-guide/)
- **Forage**: free for students, and employers pay. No price is disclosed — [About Forage](https://www.theforage.com/about)
- **Nuwe** (Spain): no public pricing. The company raised €739,000 in its second round — [Capital Riesgo](https://capital-riesgo.es/es/articles/nuwe-cierra-su-segunda-ronda-de-inversi-n-de-739-000-euros/)

### Inferences
- A per-challenge price for SkillBridge in the low hundreds to low thousands of EUR sits between Topcoder's per-contest model (prize + ≤$250 fee) and hackathon sponsorship ($1k–15k). Parker Dewey's $5k–$100k programme tiers show companies pay five figures for early-career pipeline programmes.
- Assessment SaaS at $79–$529/month sets the reference price for "screening tooling". A challenge that only screens will be compared against that low price, so SkillBridge's premium has to come from branding plus graded real work.

### Gaps
- No verified published prices found for Codility, CodeSignal, HackerEarth, Unstop, Bright Network, Forage, Kaggle Featured, Devpost (official) or Nuwe. All are "contact sales" or were not fetched.
- No independent survey of typical corporate hackathon total cost (e.g., Gartner) found.

## Q3. What outcomes do companies or platforms report?

### Takeaway
Almost all outcome data comes from the platforms themselves (Forage "2x interview / 4x offer", Unstop/HackerEarth case-study hire counts). None of it is independently audited, and it suffers from selection bias, because motivated candidates self-select into simulations. I found no independent cost-per-hire or time-to-hire study for challenge-based hiring.

### Cited Findings
- **Forage** (VENDOR CLAIM, undated): employers are 2x more likely to interview and 4x more likely to offer candidates who completed a simulation. Students who complete a company's simulation are "4x more likely to land the job than the average applicant". Among completers, 95% report better understanding of the employer, 85% are "very likely to apply", and they are 4x more likely to accept an offer — [Seramount: Forage](https://seramount.com/hidden); [NACE buyers guide](https://ebiztest.naceweb.org/buyers-guide/detail.aspx?ID=87285)
- Forage's own About page claims students become "up to 2.6 times more employable", with no methodology, sample or date. Scale: 10M+ enrollments, 100+ employers, 1,000+ partner universities, 300+ simulations (VENDOR) — [About Forage](https://www.theforage.com/about)
- Note the internal inconsistency: "4x more likely to land the job" vs "up to 2.6x more employable" on different Forage-sourced pages. Neither discloses its method.
- **Unstop/HackerEarth case studies** (VENDOR CLAIMS, mostly India, dates mostly unstated):
  - Juspay ran two hiring hackathons and rolled out 2,610+ pre-placement offers. A different Unstop headline says "interview offers to 2,473 engineers", so the figures conflict or describe different stages.
  - Walmart hired 300+ women through CodeHers.
  - Flipkart hired 58 MBA students through Flipkart WiRED.
  - Soliton built a 1,228-candidate pipeline and shortlisted 100 for interviews.
  - Mobilewalla reached 100+ top-tier college candidates and interviewed the top 9.
  - Sources: [Unstop blog](https://unstop.com/blog/walmart-flipkart-hires-juspay-gives-interview-offers-to-2473-engineers-through-hiring-challenges/amp); [HackerEarth case study](https://www.hackerearth.com/recruit/?p=9454)
- **Kaggle**: 500+ featured competitions hosted. No host-reported business outcomes were retrieved (VENDOR) — [Kaggle Featured](https://kaggle.com/c/about/featured)
- Independent context, ISE (UK, Dec 2025): 77% of employers say former interns are more work-ready, which indirectly supports "try-before-hire" formats — [ISE top 10 stats 2025](https://ise.org.uk/knowledge/insights/513/ise_top_10_stats_of_2025_you_need_to_know/)

### Inferences
- Forage-style "Nx more likely" figures compare self-selected completers with all applicants, so they cannot separate the effect of the simulation from candidate motivation. SkillBridge should not repeat them as causal evidence, and could run its own matched comparison.
- Indian case studies show challenges working at very high volume (thousands of candidates, hundreds of offers). EU/SaaS buyers posting short challenges will run at much smaller scale.

### Gaps
- No independent (non-vendor) study found on cost-per-hire reduction, time-to-hire, or challenge→interview→offer conversion for any challenge platform.
- Did not retrieve HackerRank/Codility/CodeSignal customer case studies with time-to-hire numbers, or Kaggle host testimonials.

## Q4. What do companies dislike or find costly?

### Takeaway
The dominant pain point in 2025–2026 is AI-assisted cheating and fraud in remote assessments, and it is concentrated in early-career hiring. Employers are responding by redesigning processes and moving interviews back in person. Judging time, low-quality submissions and IP concerns are widely discussed, but I found few hard data on them.

### Cited Findings
- **CodeSignal** (press release, 25 Feb 2026; vendor data from its own proctoring, which also helps sell its detection product):
  - Cheating/fraud attempt rates on proctored assessments rose from 16% (2024) to 35% (2025).
  - Entry-level rates rose from 15% to 40%.
  - By region: APAC 48%, North America 27%.
  - Drivers: plagiarism, proxy test-takers and unauthorized AI.
  - Signals flagged: 35% off-screen referencing, 23% unusually linear typing, 15% high similarity to known answers.
  - Source: [CodeSignal press release](https://codesignal.com/newsroom/press-releases/codesignal-detection-systems-identify-and-stop-record-high-cheating-attempts-as-assessment-fraud-more-than-doubled-in-2025/)
- **ISE** (independent employer survey, UK, Dec 2025): 48% of employers worry graduates' AI use misrepresents their abilities (52% for school leavers). Cheating encounters doubled from 7% to 15%. One-third of employers are redesigning recruitment because of AI — [ISE top 10 stats 2025](https://ise.org.uk/knowledge/insights/513/ise_top_10_stats_of_2025_you_need_to_know/); [Prospects Luminate: integrity, inclusion, entry standards](https://luminate.prospects.ac.uk/early-careers-recruitment-in-2025-integrity-inclusion-entry-standards)
- Greenhouse's 2025 survey (as reported by a secondary blog; primary not fetched): 39% of US hiring managers are doing more in-person interviews to verify candidates. 59% of hiring managers suspect AI misrepresentation, but only 19% are extremely confident their process would catch fraud. Overlay tools (Cluely, Interview Coder) cost $20–50/month — [FabricHQ: state of cheating 2026](https://fabrichq.ai/blogs/state-of-cheating-in-interviews-in-2026-tools-trends-and-prevention); [LetsDataScience: firms add in-person interviews](https://letsdatascience.com/news/some-tech-firms-add-in-person-interviews-as-ai-weakens-remot-57fd5dac)
- Application overload: 140 applications per graduate vacancy (UK, 2025), driven partly by AI-written applications and Easy Apply — [ISE survey 2025](https://ise.org.uk/_userfiles/pages/files/reports/student_recruitment_survey_2025.pdf); [Wonkhe](https://wonkhe.com/blogs/graduate-jobs-and-recruitment-reality/)
- Hackathon sponsors churn unless they get a post-event ROI report within 1–2 weeks. Organisers are advised to report cost per participant and ROI (organiser guidance) — [BuilderBase ch.17](https://intercom.help/builderbase/en/articles/14831425-hackathon-playbook-ch-17-sponsor-roi-reporting)

### Inferences
- AI fraud is both a threat and an opening for SkillBridge. An "AI rubric + company engineer" grading model has to show provenance (e.g., live defence of the submission, commit history, short follow-up call), or employers will discount the certificate, especially for entry-level candidates, where fraud is highest (40%).
- Judging time is the hidden cost. An AI pre-score that cuts engineer review time is plausibly the core value, but no source quantified engineer hours per hackathon.

### Gaps
- No quantitative source found on judging hours per submission, the share of low-quality submissions, or companies' IP/legal concerns about spec work (e.g., IP assignment in hackathon T&Cs).
- Greenhouse primary report not fetched. Treat the 39%/59%/19% figures as secondary-reported.

## Q5. Take-home tests vs challenges in early-career hiring; is grading outsourced?

### Takeaway
Remote take-homes and online assessments are under pressure from AI. Employers are adding in-person or live stages instead of dropping assessment. Vendor platforms already handle grading automatically (auto-scored tests). Fully outsourced human grading of open-ended work is less documented.

### Cited Findings
- 79% of UK early-career employers are redesigning or reviewing recruitment, a third explicitly because of AI. Only 15% use AI exclusively in gamified assessments, and 62% expect to use AI within five years (ISE, Dec 2025) — [ISE top 10 stats 2025](https://ise.org.uk/knowledge/insights/513/ise_top_10_stats_of_2025_you_need_to_know/)
- Tech firms are adding in-person interviews because AI weakens remote tests (news, 2025) — [LetsDataScience](https://letsdatascience.com/news/some-tech-firms-add-in-person-interviews-as-ai-weakens-remot-57fd5dac)
- HackerRank prices by "candidate attempts" (5/month Starter, 30/month Pro, $20/extra attempt), so automated scoring is bundled into the per-attempt price, i.e. grading is effectively outsourced to the tool — [Lodely](https://www.lodely.com/blog/hackerrank-pricing-2026)
- Parker Dewey micro-internships ($200–$600 paid projects) are pitched as a way to "test drive" talent before hiring, an alternative to unpaid take-homes. The employer still evaluates the work itself — [Parker Dewey employer page](https://info.parkerdewey.com/employer/txst); [pricing](https://www.parkerdewey.com/pricing)

### Inferences
- A challenge format with a short live debrief could be positioned as "AI-resistant" compared with unsupervised take-homes, which fits the ISE-reported process redesign.

### Gaps
- No survey data found on what share of early-career employers use take-homes vs timed online tests, or who grades open-ended work (in-house engineers vs. vendors such as Karat-style interview outsourcing).

## Q6. Spain/EU-specific evidence

### Takeaway
Spain has a home-grown challenge-hiring platform (Nuwe, Barcelona) with large corporate clients and modest funding. Barcelona's Talent Arena (MWC Capital) is a new, publicly backed developer-talent event that includes hackathons. I found no Spain-specific pricing or outcome data.

### Cited Findings
- **Nuwe** (Barcelona, 2020): gamified coding challenges/hackathons for hiring and retaining tech talent. Clients include Schneider Electric, Oracle, Mobile World Capital and CaixaBank. It raised €739,000 in its second round (reported by El Referente; date not stated in snippet). Competitors listed by CB Insights: ChallengeRocket (PL), HackUp, Ninja Talent — [El Referente](https://elreferente.es/inversiones/nuwe-cierra-segunda-ronda-inversion-739-000-euros/); [CB Insights: ChallengeRocket vs Nuwe](https://www.cbinsights.com/compare/challengerocket-vs-nuwe-1)
- **Talent Arena** (first edition 3–5 March 2025, Fira Montjuïc, alongside MWC Barcelona) is an initiative of Mobile World Capital Barcelona with GSMA, Barcelona City Council, the Generalitat de Catalunya and Spain's Ministry of Digital Transformation. It covers AI, software engineering, cybersecurity and cloud, with multiple hackathons and 200+ speakers. The GSMA Open Gateway Hackathon and DevCon (network-API developer adoption) moved there in 2025 — [Mobile World Capital press release](https://mobileworldcapital.com/en/nota-de-prensa/the-first-edition-of-talent-arena-will-unite-the-global-developer-community-at-montjuic-venue-of-fira-during-mwc-barcelona-2025/); [Catalonia.com](https://catalonia.com/w/mobile-world-capital-launches-talent-arena-a-platform-for-digital-talent-at-barcelona-s-mwc)
- Spanish public-sector framing of hackathons as talent capture (datos.gob.es, Feb 2022) — [datos.gob.es](https://datos.gob.es/es/blog/los-hackathons-una-nueva-forma-de-captacion-de-talento)

### Inferences
- The GSMA Open Gateway hackathon moving to Talent Arena shows the "API adoption" motive operating in Barcelona. Telcos and SaaS firms there pay for developer engagement, a possible early buyer segment.
- Nuwe's client list (Oracle, Schneider, CaixaBank) shows that large Spain-based employers already buy challenge-based hiring. SkillBridge competes with Nuwe directly.

### Gaps
- Not found within budget: Ironhack hiring-partner terms, Codemotion sponsorship prices, Talent Arena 2025 attendance/outcome figures, Nuwe pricing or case outcomes, Sifted coverage of EU challenge platforms, and Bright Network (UK) employer pricing.
