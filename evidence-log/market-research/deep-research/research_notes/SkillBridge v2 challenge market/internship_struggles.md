# Why students and new grads struggle to land internships and entry-level tech jobs (global + Spain/Madrid, 2024–2026)

Research date: 2026-10-12. Every figure is dated; older data is flagged as older.

## Q1. The experience paradox: entry-level roles that require experience; internships as the hiring pipeline

### Takeaway
"Entry-level" postings often ask for experience that new grads can't have yet. Big Tech and VC-backed startups now post junior roles and fill them with experienced people (SignalFire, May 2025). That makes internships the main way in: about 63% of 2024-25 US interns were converted to full-time hires (NACE 2026).

### Cited Findings
- **OLDER (2018):** TalentWorks analysed more than 95,000 postings and found that 61% of full-time "entry-level" jobs asked for at least 3 years of experience. It is still the most-cited figure, but it is pre-2020 — [SHRM](https://www.shrm.org/in/topics-tools/news/talent-acquisition/entry-level-experience-requirements-hurting-hiring); [NoDesk summary](https://nodesk.co/articles/the-science-of-the-job-search-part-iii-61-of-entry-level-jobs-require-3-years-of-experience/)
- SignalFire State of Tech Talent 2025 (published 2025-05-20) names the "experience paradox": "companies are posting junior roles but filling them with senior individual contributors (ICs)." Big Tech = the top 15 tech companies by market cap; startups = companies funded by top-100 VCs — [SignalFire](https://www.signalfire.com/blog/signalfire-state-of-talent-report-2025)
- Burning Glass Institute, "No Country for Young Grads" (2025): employers say they struggle to fill early-career roles while graduates can't get in. It points to credential inflation and hiring requirements that don't match the roles. More than half of the US class of 2023 were in jobs that didn't need a degree one year after graduating — [Seramount/Burning Glass report page](https://pages.seramount.com/No-Country-for-Young-Grads.html); [CERIC summary, Aug 2025](https://careerwise.ceric.ca/2025/08/12/3-reports-that-caught-our-attention-the-week-of-aug-10-2025/)
- NACE 2026 Internship & Co-op Report (US): 63.1% of 2024-25 interns were converted to full-time hires, up almost 13% on 2023-24. Offer acceptance was 88.3%, up from 82.8% — [NACE](https://naceweb.org/talent-acquisition/internships/intern-conversion-rate-hits-highest-mark-in-five-years)
- NACE 2025 report (2023-24 interns): employers offered full-time jobs to 62% of interns, the lowest offer rate in five years (2023 interns: about two-thirds). The offer rate was 72% for in-person internships and about 56% for hybrid ones. Conversion was 58.5% for in-person interns and 46% for hybrid — [NACE](https://naceweb.org/talent-acquisition/internships/intern-offer-and-conversion-rates-fall-acceptances-rise)
- NACE forecast that intern hiring would fall 3.1% in 2025 — [NJBIA citing NACE](https://njbia.org/survey-finds-intern-hiring-expected-to-dip-3-1-in-2025/)
- US internship postings were 11% lower than a year earlier and "far below" 2022–2023 levels (2025, via The New Stack) — [The New Stack](https://thenewstack.io/whats-happening-with-entry-level-development-jobs/)

### Inferences
- The internship is the bottleneck. Interns get hired at about 60%+ rates, but there are fewer internships, and getting one already takes experience or proof of skill. A credible signal of skill that doesn't depend on having had an internship goes straight at this paradox.
- In-person interns convert better than hybrid ones. Employers seem to value having watched someone actually do the work, which is the same thing a work-sample challenge provides.

### Gaps
- I found no recent (2024–2026) replication of the TalentWorks 61% study by LinkedIn or Lightcast. There are 2025–2026 blog analyses (Interview Guys, SeekerScore), but their methods are weak, so I left them out.
- I found no LinkedIn Economic Graph figure on experience requirements in entry-level postings.

## Q2. Volume and competition: applications per role, applications per offer, response rates

### Takeaway
Competition is at record levels. In the UK, ISE members got 140 applications per graduate vacancy in each of the last two years, the highest since records began in 1991. In the US, Handshake postings fell while each student sent more applications. AI-assisted applying inflates volumes further.

### Cited Findings
- ISE Student Recruitment Survey 2025 (UK, 2024-25 cycle; 155 employers, more than 31,000 hires from more than 1.8M applications):
  - 140 applications per graduate vacancy, the record high, for the second year running. In 2002/3 it was 38.
  - Retail/FMCG/tourism had 290 per role.
  - School-leaver roles had 89 per role.
  - Sources: [ISE report PDF](https://ise.org.uk/_userfiles/pages/files/reports/student_recruitment_survey_2025.pdf); [ISE 5 trends](https://ise.org.uk/knowledge/insights/498/5_trends_you_need_to_know_from_ises_recruitment_survey_2025/)
- ISE 2025: graduate vacancies fell 8% while apprenticeships grew — [ISE](https://ise.org.uk/knowledge/insights/492/apprenticeships_rise_as_graduate_vacancies_drop_8)
- Handshake (US, class of 2025, as of March 2025):
  - Students had sent 16.5 applications each, against 13.6 a year earlier (+21%).
  - Postings on Handshake were down 15% year on year.
  - Applications per vacancy were up 30% year on year.
  - 56% of students were "somewhat" or "very" pessimistic (n=2,871, 658 institutions).
  - Source: [Business Insider NL citing Handshake](https://www.businessinsider.nl/graduates-are-taking-a-closer-look-at-smaller-companies-in-a-competitive-and-scary-job-market/)
- Greenhouse (ATS, about 175,000 live jobs) averages 254 applicants per job, and applications per recruiter are up 412%. This is reported second-hand and the date is unclear (2025) — [The Decoder](https://the-decoder.com/recruiters-are-drowning-in-a-flood-of-ai-generated-applications/)
- UK entry-level tech postings are down 31.9% since ChatGPT launched (late 2022). Junior roles are now about a quarter of UK tech vacancies (2025, via The New Stack) — [The New Stack](https://thenewstack.io/whats-happening-with-entry-level-development-jobs/)

### Inferences
- More applications per vacancy plus fewer vacancies means the chance of any single cold application succeeding keeps falling. That pushes students toward mass-applying, which in turn worsens the flood that recruiters have to filter.

### Gaps
- I found no reliable 2025–2026 figure for applications sent per offer received, or for response/ghosting rates, from Indeed Hiring Lab or Glassdoor. Blog figures exist, but I couldn't trace them to a primary source.

## Q3. AI in screening and AI-generated applications

### Takeaway
Most large employers screen applications through an ATS. AI is increasingly used for resume screening, and candidates widely use AI to write applications. The result is an arms race in which CVs carry less signal. Many of the headline statistics on this come from low-quality aggregators.

### Cited Findings
- SHRM 2025 Talent Trends: among organisations that use AI in recruiting, 66% use it to write job descriptions and 44% use it for resume screening (reported via an aggregator) — [Interview Guys summary](https://blog.theinterviewguys.com/?p=4581); [Staffing Industry Analysts](https://www.staffingindustry.com/news/global-daily-news/ai-adoption-in-recruiting-climbs)
- About two-thirds of US candidates say they use AI when applying (Career Group Companies 2025 Market Trend Report). In a separate Canva survey, 45% used AI to complete the application itself — [The Decoder](https://the-decoder.com/recruiters-are-drowning-in-a-flood-of-ai-generated-applications/)
- Recruiter reactions (survey cited by The Decoder, 2025):
  - 80% of recruiters rate incoming applications as "mediocre at best."
  - 19.6% would reject a candidate whose resume or cover letter was AI-generated.
  - Source: [The Decoder](https://the-decoder.com/recruiters-are-drowning-in-a-flood-of-ai-generated-applications/)
- ING Think (2025) argues that AI is undermining authenticity in recruitment and that application signals are getting weaker — [ING Think](https://think.ing.com/articles/ai-monthly-how-ai-is-challenging-authenticity-in-recruitment/)
- **Treat with caution:**
  - "98–99% of Fortune 500 use an ATS" (originally Jobscan).
  - "75% of resumes rejected by ATS before a human sees them" is a widely repeated claim with no traceable primary study, and is often called a myth.
  - Both appear on aggregator pages — [Stealth Agents](https://stealthagents.com/research/ai-resume-screening-statistics-2026)

### Inferences
- When CVs and cover letters are cheap to produce with AI, employers need signals that are hard to fake. Work samples, assessed challenges, and referrals all count. This is the core "why now" for company-calibrated challenges.

### Gaps
- I found no primary, methodologically strong source for the "75% auto-rejected" claim. Don't use it.
- I didn't find EU/Spain-specific figures on AI screening adoption.

## Q4. Junior tech hiring decline, 2023–2026, and generative AI

### Takeaway
New-grad hiring at Big Tech is down more than 50% from 2019. US software postings stayed about 35% below pre-pandemic levels through 2025. Payroll evidence shows a 13% relative employment drop for 22–25-year-olds in AI-exposed jobs. Recent US grads have unusually high unemployment and underemployment. Experts still disagree about how much of this AI causes, compared with post-pandemic over-hiring and interest rates.

### Cited Findings
- SignalFire (published 2025-05-20):
  - New grads are 7% of Big Tech hires, down 25% from 2023 and down more than 50% from 2019.
  - At startups they are under 6% of hires, down 11% from 2023 and down more than 30% from 2019.
  - Series A startups are 20% smaller than in 2020.
  - "37% of managers said they'd rather use AI than hire a Gen Z employee" (a survey SignalFire cites).
  - Source: [SignalFire](https://www.signalfire.com/blog/signalfire-state-of-talent-report-2025)
- Indeed Hiring Lab, US software development postings:
  - Down 9.5% year on year to Jan 2025 ([Hiring Lab, Feb 2025](https://www.hiringlab.org/2025/02/06/software-development-postings-remain-in-the-doldrums/)).
  - About 35% below early-2020 levels in April 2025, and about 36% below the pre-pandemic baseline in July 2025 (Hiring Lab quarterly B2B updates — [Q2 2025 PDF](https://www.hiringlab.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/HiringLab-B2B_Q2_03.pdf)).
  - Counter-signal from 2026: an OfficeChai write-up of Indeed data says US software postings were up 15% after Claude Code launched while overall postings fell 7%. This is secondary and I didn't verify it against Hiring Lab — [OfficeChai](https://officechai.com/ai/us-software-job-postings-are-up-15-since-launch-of-claude-code-while-overall-job-postings-are-down-7-indeed-data/)
- Brynjolfsson, Chandar & Chen, "Canaries in the Coal Mine? Six Facts about the Recent Employment Effects of AI" (Stanford Digital Economy Lab, Aug 2025):
  - Data: ADP payroll records.
  - Workers aged 22–25 in the most AI-exposed occupations, including software developers, saw a 13% relative employment decline, after controlling for firm-level shocks.
  - Older workers in the same jobs, and workers in less-exposed jobs, held steady or grew.
  - The losses are concentrated where AI automates the work rather than augmenting it.
  - Source: [SIEPR working paper](https://siepr.stanford.edu/publications/working-paper/canaries-coal-mine-six-facts-about-recent-employment-effects-artificial)
- Burning Glass Institute (2025):
  - US unemployment for 20–24-year-olds with a bachelor's degree rose from 5.2% (2018-19) to 6.2% (two years to June 2025).
  - Young graduates now have higher unemployment than any group except high-school-only workers.
  - Postings fell most sharply in jobs with high exposure to large language models.
  - Source: [CERIC summary](https://careerwise.ceric.ca/2025/08/12/3-reports-that-caught-our-attention-the-week-of-aug-10-2025/)
- NY Fed, "Labor Market for Recent College Graduates":
  - Q2 2026: recent grads aged 22–27 had 5.6% unemployment and 42% underemployment ([NY Fed](https://www.newyorkfed.org/research/college-labor-market)).
  - Earlier, in Q1 2025, recent-grad unemployment reached 5.8%, the highest since 2021, against 2.7% for all college grads ([Fox Business](https://www-ak-ms.foxbusiness.com/lifestyle/recent-graduates-face-noticeably-deteriorated-labor-market-unemployment-reaches-highest-level-years)).
- NY Fed by-major figures (ages 22–27), as reported second-hand: computer science unemployment 7.0%, computer engineering 7.8%. The same source says undergraduate CS enrollment fell 11% in 2025 — [Rockstar Developer University (aggregator)](https://rockstardeveloperuniversity.com/software-engineer-unemployment-statistics/). See Gaps: I couldn't verify these against the NY Fed table.
- Skeptical view: analyses from university career offices (UCSD, Yale, Buffalo, Oct 2025) argue that the three headline studies (Stanford, Burning Glass, SignalFire) can't fully separate AI from interest rates and post-pandemic over-hiring — [Yale SOM CDO](https://cdo.som.yale.edu/blog/2025/10/17/is-ai-killing-the-early-career-job-market-parsing-the-data-from-three-high-profile-studies/); [Phil Hill, On EdTech](https://onedtech.philhillaa.com/p/many-explanations-are-making-premature-analysis-on-recent-grad-unemployment)

### Inferences
- Whatever the cause, employers are hiring fewer juniors and asking more of the ones they hire. Candidates who can show they're productive from day one, including with AI tools, have a relative advantage.

### Gaps
- NY Fed CS and computer-engineering unemployment by major. The NY Fed page fetch didn't show the by-major table. Earlier widely cited figures (Feb 2025 release, 2023 ACS data) were about 6.1% for CS and 7.5% for computer engineering. That comes from memory and is unverified here, so check the NY Fed table directly before citing either number.
- I didn't find a LinkedIn Economic Graph metric for junior tech hiring.

## Q5. Referrals and network effects

### Takeaway
Referrals are a small share of applicants but a large share of hires. Referred candidates are hired roughly 4–7 times more often than cold applicants. This is the advantage that students without networks don't have.

### Cited Findings
- Referred candidates are hired at about 30%, against about 7% for other sources. Referrals are about 7% of applicants but 30–50% of US hires. Referral hires are 40% more likely to still be there after a year. All of these are aggregator restatements of Jobvite data, mostly from before 2024 — [Erin App](https://erinapp.com/blog/employee-referral-statistics-you-need-to-know-for-2024-infographic)
- Referred applicants are about 4x more likely to get an offer than company-website applicants and about 7x more likely than job-board applicants (aggregated, 2026) — [FMC Group](https://fmcgroup.com/applicants-referred-to-hiring-manager/)
- Ashby Talent Trends has a dedicated report on referral conversion (2024/25). I didn't fetch the figures, so check them — [Ashby](https://www.ashbyhq.com/talent-trends-report/reports/referrals)

### Inferences
- A company-calibrated challenge can stand in for a referral: it gives the hiring team a trusted, pre-vetted signal about a candidate they don't know.

### Gaps
- No primary, recent (2025–2026) study on referral conversion. Ashby's report is the best candidate. I found no Spain-specific data on referral hiring.

## Q6. Spain, the EU, and Madrid

### Takeaway
Spain has one of the highest youth unemployment rates in the EU: 24.5% for under-25s in Q1 2026, against about 14.8% in the euro area. Graduate over-qualification is the highest in the EU, at about 36%. At the same time, the tech sector reports around 120,000 unfilled ICT vacancies. Spain's internship rules are being rewritten: the Estatuto del Becario bill is in the Congreso but not yet in force as of Oct 2026.

### Cited Findings
- **Youth unemployment, Spain (INE EPA, Q1 2026):**
  - Under-25s: 24.54%, or 440,400 unemployed (women 25.79%, men 23.55%).
  - Young people with higher education: about 6.8%.
  - Sources: [INJUVE EPA Q1 2026 note](https://injuve.es/sites/default/files/adjuntos/2026/07/EPAPResultados_2026-1T_16a24_NotInfo.pdf); [Ara](https://es.ara.cat/economia/mercado-laboral/hay-detras-minimo-historico-jovenes-parados-espana_1_5614106.html). The 6.8% figure comes from [Tesify](https://tesify.es/paro-juvenil-universitario-espana-2026-estadisticas-oficiales), a secondary source.
- **EU comparison (Eurostat, 2026):**
  - Euro-area youth unemployment was 14.8% in Jan 2026 and again in June 2026 ([Eurostat release, Mar 2026](https://ec.europa.eu/eurostat/web/products-euro-indicators/w/3-04032026-ap)).
  - Spain was 23.4% in June 2026, second only to Sweden at 24.9% ([uni2study citing Eurostat](https://www.uni2study.com/articles/spain-italy-greece-youth-unemployment-20-2026)). Monthly Eurostat and quarterly EPA figures differ slightly because they're measured differently.
  - Spain was historically the highest in the EU; July 2025 reporting still had it top — [Infobae](https://www.infobae.com/espana/2025/07/10/espana-encabeza-el-desempleo-juvenil-en-la-union-europea-los-problemas-estructurales-del-mercado-laboral-persisten/)
- **Madrid:** under-25 unemployment in the Comunidad de Madrid was about 19.6% in Q1 2025, one of the lowest regional rates. This is older; I didn't find a regional figure for 2026 — [INJUVE EPA Q1 2025](https://injuve.es/sites/default/files/adjuntos/2025/07/EPAPResultados_2025-1T_16a24_NotInfo.pdf); search summary only, so check the PDF table.
- **Graduate mismatch (Fundación CYD and press, 2023–2025):**
  - About 36% of Spanish graduates work in jobs below their qualification level, the highest over-qualification rate in the EU and 64% above the EU average.
  - 13% of graduates do basic admin work and 11% work in hospitality or retail.
  - In 2022, the graduate employment rate was 83%, against an EU average of 87.4% (second-lowest in the EU).
  - STEM graduates are 18.8% of the total, against 25.1% in the EU.
  - Sources: [Xataka](https://www.xataka.com/magnet/espana-no-para-universitarios-37-sus-titulados-trabaja-puestos-no-cualificados); [Aprendemas](https://www.aprendemas.com/es/blog/mundo-educativo/espana-es-el-pais-de-la-ue-con-mas-sobrecualificacion-el-36-de-los-graduados-ocupa-una-posicion-inferior-a-sus-estudios-167418); [Informe CYD 2025](https://www.fundacioncyd.org/descarga/10584/informe-cyd-2025/informe-cyd/)
- **Tech talent shortage, Spain:**
  - DigitalES estimates about 120,000 unfilled ICT vacancies (70% more than in 2021), about 40,000 of them in software development. The date is unclear (around 2024) — [Telefónica Think Big](https://blogthinkbig.com/por-que-hay-120-000-vacantes-de-empleo-tic-en-espana)
  - Spanish tech talent grows about 4% a year, but many professionals emigrate — [Xataka](https://www.xataka.com/empresas-y-economia/talento-tecnologico-espana-crece-4-anual-se-tienen-que-ir-a-otros-paises-a-trabajar)
  - Adecco's 2025 ICT salary guide is a further reference — [Adecco Institute](https://www.adeccoinstitute.es/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/Guia-salarial-tendencias-y-salarios-TIC-The-Adecco-Group-2025.pdf)
- **InfoJobs–Esade "Estado del mercado laboral en España 2025"** (covers 2024 data):
  - 2.5M vacancies published and 4.2M people applied to at least one offer.
  - Just over 20% of offers were in IT.
  - One in three candidates is looking for work in IT.
  - Source: [Esade news](https://www.esade.edu/es/noticias/el-informe-infojobs-esade-estado-del-mercado-laboral-en-espana-2025-confirma-la-demanda-de)
- **Infoempleo–Adecco (2024 data, published May 2025):** 21.42% of Spanish job offers asked for a university degree, down 6 points. Ingeniería Informática is among the most-requested degrees, alongside ADE and Industrial engineering — [Bolsamanía/Europa Press](https://www.bolsamania.com/noticias/economia/economia-las-ofertas-de-empleo-que-piden-grados-universitarios-bajan-hasta-el-214-en-2024-6-puntos-menos-que-en-2023--19805130.html); [Merca2](https://www.merca2.es/carreras-mas-salidas-adecco)
- **Estatuto del Becario (Estatuto de las Personas en Formación Práctica No Laboral):**
  - Anteproyecto approved by the Consejo de Ministros on 2025-11-04 ([Legal Today](https://www.legaltoday.com/actualidad-juridica/noticias-de-derecho/el-gobierno-aprueba-hoy-el-anteproyecto-del-estatuto-del-becario-menos-practicas-extracurriculares-y-mas-compensacion-de-gastos-para-los-estudiantes-2025-11-04/); [PwC](https://periscopiofiscalylegal.pwc.es/aprobado-el-anteproyecto-de-estatuto-del-becario/)).
  - Proyecto de Ley sent to the Congreso on 2026-03-03, where it faces a difficult passage ([El Debate](https://www.eldebate.com/economia/20260303/gobierno-da-luz-verde-estatuto-becario-manda-congreso-aprobacion-definitiva-cambia_391589.html); [Wolters Kluwer, Mar 2026](https://www.wolterskluwer.com/es-es/expert-insights/estatuto-becario-cambios-empresas)).
  - Key provisions:
    - interns can't replace workers;
    - the internship must match the student's training programme;
    - interns get compensation for expenses and holidays;
    - fewer extracurricular internships;
    - the Inspección de Trabajo can access internship documentation;
    - fines of up to €225,018 for very serious breaches.
  - Reported (unverified against the BOE): STC 40/2026 of 26 May annulled the additional provision that mandated the Estatuto — [Wolters Kluwer](https://www.wolterskluwer.com/es-es/expert-insights/estatuto-becario-cambios-empresas). Separately, since Jan 2024, internships, including unpaid ones, must be registered with Social Security (RDL 2/2023). That fact is from background knowledge with no source fetched here, so verify it.

### Inferences
- Spain shows the paradox in its sharpest form: high youth unemployment and graduate over-qualification sit alongside an ICT talent shortage. That points to a signalling and matching failure, not just a lack of jobs.
- The pending Estatuto would make internships more costly and more regulated, so employers may offer fewer of them. Non-employment ways of proving skill, like challenges, would then matter more. This is speculative until the law passes.

### Gaps
- No InfoJobs figure on applications per IT offer, or per junior offer, for 2025–2026. The Esade page returned 403, so read the full PDF.
- No breakdown of the 2025 Infoempleo–Adecco report by experience required (the PDF couldn't be parsed).
- No Madrid-specific 2026 youth or graduate unemployment, and no Madrid tech-junior data.
- No verified 2025–2026 Spanish figure for IT-graduate unemployment. The CYD/INE employability-by-field data would need direct retrieval.
