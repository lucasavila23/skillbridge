# Credential Credibility: How Employers Value Certificates, Assessments, Portfolios and Challenge Results for Intern/Junior Tech Hiring

Research date: 2026-10-12. Source labels: **[IND]** = independent/academic/public body; **[VENDOR]** = company that sells assessments, credentials or hiring services (has a commercial interest in the finding); **[PRESS]** = secondary press coverage of a primary source.

## 1. Skills-based hiring: what employers say vs. what changes in practice

### Takeaway
Nearly every employer survey reports that most firms "do" skills-based hiring (65–85%). The best independent behavioural evidence (HBS/Burning Glass, 2024) finds the change shows up in fewer than 1 in 700 hires. Stated intent is a weak predictor of whether an alternative credential will actually get a candidate hired.

### Cited Findings
- [IND] HBS Project on Managing the Future of Work + Burning Glass Institute (Feb 2024), "Skills-Based Hiring: The Long Road from Pronouncements to Practice": the increased opportunity promised by skills-based hiring "has borne out in not even 1 in 700 hires" in 2023. The study covered 11,300 large firms, 2014–2023. Nearly 40% of firms removed degree requirements in that period, and the annual number of roles with dropped degree requirements rose almost fourfold. — [Harvard Project on Workforce summary](https://pw.hks.harvard.edu/post/skills-based-hiring-the-long-road-from-pronouncements-to-practice); [HR Dive/Trucking Dive coverage](https://www.truckingdive.com/news/employers-failing-at-skills-based-hiring/708245/)
- [IND] Same report: about 45% of firms that removed degree requirements changed them "in name only", with no meaningful change in hiring behaviour. About 37% ("Skills-Based Hiring Leaders", e.g. Walmart, Apple, GM, Target, Koch, Yelp) raised their hiring of non-BA workers by nearly 20%. — [HCAMag](https://www.hcamag.com/ca/specialization/recruitment/in-name-only-firms-dropping-degree-requirements-fall-short-in-practice/477331); [Brian Heger summary](https://www.brianheger.com/skills-based-hiring-the-long-road-from-pronouncements-to-practice-harvard-business-school-and-the-burning-glass-institute/)
- [IND/employer association] NACE Job Outlook 2025 (US employers of new grads, fall 2024): nearly two-thirds of employers say they use skills-based hiring "all or most of the time" for new grads. Nearly 90% look for evidence of problem-solving ability and more than 80% for teamwork. Initiative, work ethic and technical skills are each cited by more than 70%. — [NACE press release](https://naceweb.org/about-us/press/the-attributes-employers-look-for-on-new-grad-resumes-and-how-to-showcase-them); [Diverse Education](https://www.diverseeducation.com/hiring/article/15736198/skills-trump-gpa-employers-seek-problemsolving-teamwork-in-new-grad-hires)
- [IND/employer association] NACE also states that internship experience, especially a paid internship, is among the strongest signals for new-grad hiring, whether or not the employer screens on GPA. — [NACE Job Outlook 2025 PDF](https://www.ithaca.edu/sites/default/files/2025-09/nace-2025-job-outlook.pdf)
- [VENDOR] TestGorilla State of Skills-Based Hiring 2025: 85% of employers say they use skills-based hiring, up from 81% in 2024. 76% use skills tests. By type: 31% role-specific tests, 30% cognitive tests, 16% work-sample assignments, 23% self-report tests. 89% of employers using skills tests say they are satisfied with recent hires, vs. 85% overall. — [TestGorilla 2025](https://www.testgorilla.com/skills-based-hiring/state-of-skills-based-hiring-2025/); [TestGorilla 2024](https://www.testgorilla.com/skills-based-hiring/state-of-skills-based-hiring-2024)
- [VENDOR] UK (TestGorilla 2025): 77% of UK employers use skills tests, and 83% say they use skills-based hiring. — [Onrec](https://onrec.com/news/statistics/77-of-uk-employers-adopt-skills-testing-as-economic-and-ai-pressures-reshape-hiring)
- [VENDOR/platform data] LinkedIn Economic Graph, "Skills-based hiring" (March 2025): a skills-based approach would expand talent pools 6.1x globally, and 8.2x for AI roles. This is a modelled potential based on skills overlap, not observed hiring. — [LinkedIn Economic Graph PDF](https://economicgraph.linkedin.com/content/dam/me/economicgraph/en-us/PDF/skills-based-hiring-march-2025.pdf)

### Inferences
- The gap between the 85% "we do skills-based hiring" figure (vendor survey) and the "<1 in 700 hires" figure (behavioural data) is SkillBridge's core credibility risk. Recruiters will say they value a challenge certificate, but saying so does not mean they will change who they interview.
- NACE's top attributes (problem-solving, teamwork) line up directly with SkillBridge's two claims: a calibrated score on a real problem, and verified individual contribution in a team. Few existing credentials evidence teamwork in a verifiable way.
- A credential works best when it slots into a step employers already use, such as an interview shortlist or an internship pipeline, rather than asking them to re-weight their whole screen.

### Gaps
- No independent data found on skills-based hiring adoption specifically for intern/junior tech roles, as opposed to all roles.
- The HBS/BGI report is US-only and focuses on non-degree workers, not students who will have degrees. Its relevance to "certificate on top of a degree" is indirect.

## 2. Micro-credentials and online certificates: employer perception and outcomes

### Takeaway
Vendor surveys report very high employer approval of micro-credentials (85–96%). Independent evidence is more modest but real. A large Stanford RCT found that making Coursera certificates visible on LinkedIn raised new employment by about 6%, with gains concentrated among weaker candidates. EU bodies say employer trust is still the main barrier.

### Cited Findings
- [VENDOR] Coursera Micro-Credentials Impact Report 2025 (fieldwork Dec 2024–Jan 2025; >1,000 employers and 1,200 learners in 10 countries: US, UK, Brazil, France, India, Indonesia, Mexico, Saudi Arabia, Thailand, Turkey): 96% of employers say a micro-credential strengthens an application, and 85% are more likely to hire a holder. 90% would offer a higher starting salary, often 10–15% more. 87% hired at least one micro-credential holder in the past year. — [Coursera blog](https://blog.coursera.org/new-coursera-report-shows-strong-employer-and-student-roi-for-industry-micro-credentials-higher-starting-salaries-greater-work-readiness-reduced-training-costs); [eCampus News](https://www.ecampusnews.com/news/2025/05/02/new-data-on-micro-credentials/)
- [VENDOR] Country variation in the same report: 97% of Indian employers vs. 75% of UK employers would pay higher starting salaries to micro-credential holders. 94% of UK employers say micro-credentials strengthen an application. — [Mediainfoline (India)](https://www.mediainfoline.com/article/97-of-indian-employers-willing-to-offer-higher-starting-salaries-to-candidates-with-micro-credentials); [Onrec (UK)](https://onrec.com/news/news-archive/94-of-uk-employers-say-micro-credentials-strengthen-a-candidate%E2%80%99s-application)
- [IND, academic RCT] Athey & Palikot (Stanford GSB), "The value of non-traditional credentials in the labor market" (arXiv, v1 Apr 2024, v2 Nov 2025). Design: randomised experiment with more than 800,000 Coursera certificate earners in developing countries who lack college degrees; the treatment made it easier to share the credential on LinkedIn and sent reminders. Results: +17 pp credential sharing, +6% (≈1.0 pp) likelihood of a new job within a year, and +8–9% for jobs related to the certificate. Effects concentrate among low-baseline-employability learners (+12% for the bottom tercile, negligible for the top tercile). The LATE suggests sharing "more than doubles the baseline probability of getting a new job in scope for the credential." — [arXiv abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.00247v1); [arXiv v2 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.00247v2); [AEA registry](https://www.socialscienceregistry.org/trials/9438). Note: the abstract says 8% for related jobs and a secondary summary says 9%; versions differ.
- [VENDOR] Google Career Certificates: more than 250,000 US graduates, and 75% report a "positive career outcome" (new job, promotion or raise) within 6 months. The employer consortium has 150+ companies. This is self-reported and has no control group. — [Google blog, May 2025 impact report](https://blog.google/company-news/outreach-and-initiatives/grow-with-google/google-career-certificates-impact-report-may-2025/); [PhillyVoice](https://www.phillyvoice.com/google-career-training-certificates-pennsylvania-state-universities/)
- [IND, EU agency] Cedefop (2023), "Microcredentials for labour market education and training: the added value for end users": the value of a micro-credential depends on sector skill intensity, the national VET system and labour-market policy, and "ensuring employers' trust in the value of microcredentials remains a key policy challenge." Cedefop surveyed employers, employees, students and the unemployed in 8 languages, including Spanish. — [Cedefop news](https://www.cedefop.europa.eu/en/news/labour-market-value-microcredentials-conduit-better-work); [Cedefop "added value" survey call](https://www.cedefop.europa.eu/en/news/added-value-microcredentials-let-your-views-be-known-0)
- [PRESS, Spanish-language] A global study of more than 3,500 employers reports that micro-credentials already influence pay and selection, but "low awareness and lack of trust in their usefulness" is holding back acceptance. — [Infomercado](https://infomercado.pe/las-empresas-ahora-valoran-mas-las-habilidades-verificadas-con-microcredenciales-que-el-titulo-universitario/) (the primary study was not identified)

### Inferences
- The Athey & Palikot result is the strongest independent evidence that a third-party credential can move hiring outcomes. The effect is modest, and it works mainly where the candidate has no other strong signal. Students with no work experience applying for internships are exactly that group.
- Vendor "90%+ of employers value micro-credentials" figures should not be used as proof of demand in pitches without a caveat. They come from issuers and conflict with Cedefop's "trust is the challenge" finding.
- Visibility matters as much as existence: the RCT's mechanism was surfacing the credential where recruiters already look, on LinkedIn. SkillBridge's certificate should be shareable to LinkedIn in one click.

### Gaps
- Could not retrieve the full Cedefop 2023 employer survey statistics (site returned 403).
- No current SHRM, ISE (UK Institute of Student Employers) or Collegis/OCLC employer figures on micro-credentials were found in this pass.
- No independent study found that compares employer valuation of company-co-designed challenge certificates against generic MOOC certificates.

## 3. Portfolios and GitHub: do recruiters look, for how long, what they trust

### Takeaway
Initial resume screens take seconds: 7.4 s on average in the Ladders 2018 eye-tracking study, which is older than the 2022+ preference. Recruiters say they have little faith in portfolios as predictors (11% in CoderPad 2026). A credential has to communicate its value in a single line on the CV or LinkedIn.

### Cited Findings
- [VENDOR-run study, 2018] Ladders eye-tracking study: the average initial resume screen was 7.4 seconds, up from 6 seconds in its earlier recession-era study. Recruiters' eyes go to name, current and previous title and employer, dates, and education. Simple layouts with clear headings perform best. — [Ladders PDF](https://csuglobal.edu/sites/default/files/blog-files/theladders-eyetracking-studyc2.pdf); [HR Dive](https://www.hrdive.com/news/eye-tracking-study-shows-recruiters-look-at-resumes-for-7-seconds/541582)
- [PRESS/critique] The "6-second scan" has been challenged as an oversimplification of a multi-stage screen. — [ERE: "Is the 6-second resume scan a myth?"](https://www.ere.net/articles/is-the-6-second-resume-scan-a-myth)
- [VENDOR] CoderPad State of Tech Hiring 2026 (Feb 2026, ~650 developers and recruiters): only 11% of recruiters and 23% of developers say portfolio review reflects on-the-job ability. — [CoderPad report](https://coderpad.io/state-of-tech-hiring-2026/); [CoderPad blog](https://coderpad.io/blog/hiring-developers/new-research-the-2026-state-of-tech-hiring-what-ai-means-for-developers-and-hiring-teams/)
- [VENDOR, older] HackerRank survey: 84.1% of C-level executives said GitHub project work is an important indicator of skills, ranked above years of experience. The date is unclear and probably pre-2022. — [ITPro Today](https://itprotoday.com/it-management/developers-show-your-github-project-to-attract-job-offers)
- [Practitioner opinion, not data] Hiring managers report looking at 2–4 pinned repos (README, structure, tests, commit history) rather than portfolio sites. — [SOLTECH](https://soltech.net/what-do-hiring-managers-actually-look-for-in-a-github-portfolio/)

### Inferences
- Self-curated portfolios suffer from an attribution problem: the recruiter cannot tell who did the work, a problem made worse by AI-generated code. That probably explains the low 11% trust figure. A third-party-verified record of individual contribution targets this weakness directly.
- With a screen of under 10 seconds, the certificate needs a legible headline (company name + score/percentile + role) and a verification link for the later, deeper review.

### Gaps
- No 2022–2026 eye-tracking study of recruiters found. The Ladders 2018 figure is the latest widely cited one.
- No independent data found on how long recruiters spend on GitHub or portfolio links for intern candidates.

## 4. Take-home tests and coding assessments: prevalence, drop-off, AI cheating, return to in-person

### Takeaway
Online assessments are standard, but AI has seriously damaged their credibility since 2023. Interviewers could not detect ChatGPT-assisted candidates, Gartner predicts 1 in 4 candidate profiles will be fake by 2028, and Google reintroduced in-person rounds in 2025. Unproctored remote scores are now discounted, which works for and against SkillBridge.

### Cited Findings
- [VENDOR experiment] interviewing.io (2024): interviewers screened candidates who were secretly using ChatGPT, with three question types (verbatim LeetCode, modified LeetCode, custom). 73% of the verbatim-question cheaters passed, and no interviewer caught any cheater. — [interviewing.io blog](https://interviewing.io/blog/how-hard-is-it-to-cheat-with-chatgpt-in-technical-interviews); [Inquirer Technology](https://technology.inquirer.net/?p=132318)
- [IND, analyst] Gartner (press release Jul 31, 2025): by 2028, 1 in 4 candidate profiles worldwide will be fake. In a 2Q25 survey of 3,000 candidates, 6% admitted interview fraud (impersonating or being impersonated). In a 4Q24 survey of 3,290 candidates, 39% used AI during applications, and 29% of those used it for assessment answers. Only 26% of candidates trust AI to evaluate them fairly. — [Gartner press release](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2025-07-31-gartner-survey-shows-just-26-percent-of-job-applicants-trust-ai-will-fairly-evaluate-them); [HR Dive](https://www.hrdive.com/news/fake-job-candidates-ai/757126/)
- [PRESS] Google (2025): Sundar Pichai said engineering candidates would face at least one in-person interview round because of AI-assisted cheating. Google VP of Recruiting Brian Ong said virtual interviews had sped hiring by about two weeks but lacked credibility. Cisco and McKinsey were also reported to have reinstated in-person interviews. Note: the press summary says "online", which is likely an error for in-person. — [CXO Digital Pulse](https://www.cxodigitalpulse.com/google-reinstates-in-person-job-interviews-amid-rising-ai-cheating-concerns/); [Storyboard18](https://www.storyboard18.com/amp/brand-makers/google-to-reintroduce-in-person-interviews-amid-rising-ai-cheating-concerns-79624.htm)
- [PRESS] Rise of interview-cheating tools, e.g. the student-built tool profiled for helping candidates use AI in Google and other tech interviews. — [NBC/CNBC](https://www.nbcnewyork.com/news/business/money-report/meet-the-21-year-old-helping-coders-use-ai-to-cheat-in-google-and-other-tech-job-interviews/6178911/)
- [VENDOR] HackerRank: AI-assisted cheating on assessments has risen 5x, and it claims 95% detection accuracy with AI plagiarism and proctoring. Its 2025 Developer Skills Report surveyed 13,700+ developers in 102 countries. 77% of developers say assessments do not reflect job skills, "fueling frustration and drop-off", and 24% want LeetCode-style tests replaced with real-world project challenges. — [HackerRank AI proctoring](https://www.hackerrank.com/writing/best-ai-proctoring-for-testing-real-world-development-skills-2025); [HackerRank on real-world challenges](https://www.hackerrank.com/writing/real-world-software-skills-interview-challenges-vs-algorithm-tests)
- [VENDOR, low reliability] One AI-interview vendor flagged 38.5% of 19,368 AI-led interviews (Jul 2025–Jan 2026) for AI-assisted cheating, and 61% of those flagged still scored above the pass bar. — [FabricHQ](https://fabrichq.ai/blogs/state-of-cheating-in-interviews-in-2026-tools-trends-and-prevention)

### Inferences
- The trend points towards discounting unproctored remote scores. A SkillBridge score earned remotely without integrity controls is at risk of being treated as "possibly AI-made". Options: process evidence (commit history, contribution logs), live defence or demo sessions with the company, or a company-side follow-up interview.
- Candidate frustration with LeetCode-style tests (77% say they are not job-relevant) is an opening: real company challenges are more job-relevant and more engaging, which may reduce drop-off.
- The rule for an AI-era credential: evidence of process and individual reasoning is worth more than evidence of output.

### Gaps
- No public 2024–2026 Karat or interviewing.io data on junior-specific pass rates or take-home prevalence was retrieved.
- No independent figure found on candidate drop-off rates for take-home tests. Widely quoted numbers come from vendors and could not be verified.
- No CodeSignal report data retrieved in this pass.

## 5. Hackathons, competitions and Kaggle as hiring signals

### Takeaway
Hackathons are used as hiring channels, especially in India, but almost all quantitative evidence comes from hackathon platforms (vendors). No independent study links hackathon wins or Kaggle rank to interview rates.

### Cited Findings
- [VENDOR] HackerEarth: 70% of candidates sourced through hiring hackathons accepted the job offer, and hiring-hackathon leads convert at about 50%. — [Unstop / HackerEarth-cited metrics](https://unstop.com/blog/hackathon-success-metrics); [HackerEarth annual report 2014 (old)](https://www.hackerearth.com/annual-report-2014/)
- [VENDOR] Corporate hiring hackathons report interview-to-hire conversion above 60%. Top performers commonly get direct interview invitations that bypass resume screening. — [Unstop](https://unstop.com/blog/hackathon-success-metrics); [Reskilll (92,000+ registrations)](https://blogs.reskilll.com/how-hackathons-get-you-hired-real-data-92000-registrations/)
- [VENDOR/press] An Analytics India Magazine survey (cited by MathCo) found hackathon recruits have a lower chance of being "wrong hires", and over 40% of candidates from tier-2/3 colleges performed as well as top-pedigree students. — [MathCo / AIM](https://mathco.com/news/analytics-india-magazine-candidates-recruited-through-hackathons-have-a-lower-chance-of-being-wrong-hires/); [AIM survey](https://analyticsindiamag.com/how-important-are-hackathons-for-your-organisation-survey/)

### Inferences
- The value of a hackathon to employers comes from direct observation and the hosting company's own involvement. A win at the hiring company's own event converts well, while generic wins listed on a CV carry little weight. This supports SkillBridge's company-calibrated model, but suggests the certificate may be worth most to the issuing company and much less to third-party employers.
- The "pedigree-neutral" finding (tier-2/3 students matching top students) supports SkillBridge's pitch to students outside elite universities.

### Gaps
- No independent (academic or non-vendor) evidence found that Kaggle rankings or hackathon wins increase interview callbacks.
- No Spain/EU hackathon-to-hire data found.

## 6. What makes a third-party credential credible (issuer, verification, rigour, employer involvement, attribution)

### Takeaway
The evidence points to four drivers: issuer or employer brand, employer involvement in design, visibility where recruiters already look, and resistance to fraud. Individual attribution in team work is unaddressed by mainstream credentials, which makes it a potential differentiator but also an unproven one.

### Cited Findings
- [IND, policy] The EU Council Recommendation on a European approach to micro-credentials (adopted 16 June 2022) sets a common EU definition, standards and principles: transparency, quality assurance, stackability, portability and recognition "across institutions, businesses, sectors and borders". — [European Commission – European approach to micro-credentials](https://education.ec.europa.eu/education-levels/higher-education/micro-credentials); [DG EMPL adoption news](https://employment-social-affairs.ec.europa.eu/news/adoption-proposal-recommendation-european-approach-micro-credentials-2022-06-30_en)
- [IND, policy] The Commission published a progress report on implementation, based on a 2024 survey of authorities in the EU-27 plus Iceland, Liechtenstein and Norway. European Digital Credentials for Learning (via Europass) are the verification infrastructure for these credentials. — [European Commission](https://education.ec.europa.eu/levels/higher-education/european-approach-to-micro-credentials); [Europass micro-credentials webinar (May 2024)](https://europass.europa.eu/system/files/2024-05/Micro-Credentials-Webinar.pdf)
- [IND] Cedefop (2023): employer trust is the main policy challenge, and value varies by sector and national system (see Section 2). — [Cedefop](https://www.cedefop.europa.eu/en/news/labour-market-value-microcredentials-conduit-better-work)
- [IND, academic] Athey & Palikot: increasing the credential's visibility on LinkedIn, plus platform verification, produced employment gains, with more certificate views on treated profiles. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.00247v1)
- [VENDOR] Google Career Certificates pair the credential with a 150+ employer consortium that has committed to consider graduates. This illustrates the employer-backed issuer model. — [PhillyVoice](https://www.phillyvoice.com/google-career-training-certificates-pennsylvania-state-universities/)
- [IND, analyst] Fraud concern raises the premium on identity-verified credentials. Gartner calls candidate fraud a cybersecurity risk "far more serious than making a bad hire". — [Gartner](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2025-07-31-gartner-survey-shows-just-26-percent-of-job-applicants-trust-ai-will-fairly-evaluate-them)

### Inferences
- Credibility stack for SkillBridge, strongest to weakest: (1) a named company set and graded the challenge, so the issuer brand is borrowed from the company; (2) a verifiable link or digital credential, ideally EDC/Open Badges 3.0 compatible so it can sit in Europass and LinkedIn; (3) a transparent rubric and calibration; (4) identity and process integrity against AI fraud; (5) individual contribution evidence.
- Aligning with the EU Recommendation's standard elements (learner identity, issuer, learning outcomes, assessment type, quality assurance) is a cheap way to look credible to EU employers and universities.
- "Verified individual contribution in team work" has no established standard and no employer-valuation evidence. Treat it as a hypothesis to test in interviews with employers, not a known value driver.

### Gaps
- No studies found measuring employer trust in Open Badges or blockchain credentials specifically, or showing that recruiters click verification links.
- No research found on how employers evaluate individual-attribution records from team projects (e.g. contribution analytics, peer assessment).
- Europass EDC issuance volumes were not found.

## 7. Spain/EU: certificates vs degrees (titulitis)

### Takeaway
Spain has a formal micro-credential policy, funded through the Recovery Plan and aligned with the 2022 EU Recommendation, and universities are issuing micro-credentials. No Spanish employer survey was found comparing certificates with degrees. Employer trust is the main unresolved barrier EU-wide.

### Cited Findings
- [PRESS/Spanish] Spain has regulated micro-credentials since 2021 in line with the EU Council strategy, funded largely through the Plan de Recuperación, Transformación y Resiliencia (NextGenerationEU). — [Educaweb (Jan 2025)](https://www.educaweb.com/noticia/2025/01/30/formaciones-cortas-aprendizaje-permanente-21740/)
- [PRESS/Spanish] Universities are scaling micro-credentials, e.g. UIB (Balearic Islands) reskilling about 2,000 professionals (Sept 2025) and UPV launching micro-credentials (Jul 2025). — [Menorca.info](https://www.menorca.info/balears/noticias/2025/09/15/2468669/total-2000-profesionales-recalificaran-las-microcredenciales-uib.html); [UPV](https://www.upv.es/entidades/epsg/2025/07/25/microcredenciales-upv/)
- [IND, Spanish employer survey, adjacent] Adecco/Infoempleo (Oct 2025): Spanish firms' top concerns are rising costs (67.19%) and absenteeism (57.81%). The "talent crisis" (lack of suitable profiles, turnover) ranks fourth at 46.88%, and 46.88% plan new hires in late 2025. — [Adecco Group press release PDF](https://www.adeccogroup.com/-/media/project/adecco-group/adeccogroup/spain-content/2025-press-releases/2025-10-30--ndp-adecco-infoempleo-nuevo-mercado-laboral.pdf)
- [IND, EU] The Cedefop survey was run in Spanish among other languages, but country-level employer results were not retrievable. — [Cedefop](https://www.cedefop.europa.eu/en/news/added-value-microcredentials-let-your-views-be-known-0)
- [VENDOR] France was among the 10 countries in Coursera's 2025 employer survey; Spain was not included. — [eCampus News](https://www.ecampusnews.com/news/2025/05/02/new-data-on-micro-credentials/)

### Inferences
- In Spain, a certificate will be read as a complement to a degree, not a replacement. Titulitis makes degree-replacement positioning risky, but SkillBridge's users are degree-track students anyway.
- University micro-credential programmes are potential partners or co-issuers that could lend institutional brand and EU-framework alignment.

### Gaps
- No quantitative Spanish employer survey found on certificates vs. degrees or on the value of skills tests and portfolios for interns. Candidates for follow-up: Fundación CYD reports, CEOE, Fundación BBVA-Ivie, InfoJobs annual report, Randstad Research, and the Ministerio de Universidades micro-credential evaluation.
- "Titulitis" is widely discussed in Spanish press, but no data-backed source was retrieved in this pass.
