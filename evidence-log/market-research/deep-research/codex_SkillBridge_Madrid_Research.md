SkillBridge Madrid: Independent Market, Product, and Pilot Decision Research
Verdict and executive summary
Verdict: change the offer and operating model, then proceed only with another tightly bounded paid pilot if the existing commitment experiment actually passes its pre-specified gate. Do not scale outreach yet.

The attached markdown contains useful evidence that students experience the proposed problem, plus reported evidence that some work was delivered and accepted. But the most important commercial claim—that qualifying Madrid businesses will commit their own money to projects delivered by inexperienced student teams—is still unverified from the materials available to me. The baseline reports business interest and willingness to pay, but unsigned charters; the funding source of reported student payments is unspecified; offer denominators and cost records are missing. The 11-student survey is also small, eligibility/overlap are unresolved, and the reported 23 obstacle selections cannot be reconciled with the apparent maximum of 22 without the raw export. 

Accordingly, I would not interpret the current evidence as either “validated demand” or “failed demand.” The existing ten-business experiment must first be reconciled: a conversion is exactly a signed charter and 10% deposit within ten days from a qualifying pitch. No final 0/10 should be declared until all ten businesses satisfy the eligibility/pitch conditions and their response windows have closed. 

Desk research nevertheless supports continuing to test rather than pausing the concept entirely. Madrid has a large small-business base; nationally, 81.6% of active enterprises had no more than two salaried employees at 1 January 2025. Among Spanish enterprises with fewer than ten employees, 79.9% had internet access in Q1 2025, but only 37.0% of internet-connected firms had a website. That establishes a sizeable digitalisation gap, not a willingness-to-pay estimate. 

Competition is already strong. Businesses can hire freelancers, agencies, use DIY website platforms, ask existing contacts, postpone the project, or—in the recent past—use subsidised Kit Digital suppliers. Kit Digital itself closed new applications on 31 October 2025 after roughly 850,000 awards; Red.es subsequently reported more than 860,000 grants and more than 500,000 completed projects. Its website category allowed up to €2,000 for qualifying microbusinesses, but those were subsidised purchases and must not be interpreted as €2,000 of unsubsidised willingness to pay. 

The recommended initial offer is therefore narrower than “student teams for digital projects”:

Primary test segment: an owner-led microbusiness with an already-operating business, an identifiable near-term reason to improve its web presence, and a concrete decision-maker. The task should be a fixed-scope brochure/landing website, preferably on an established managed CMS, with no custom authentication, payments, sensitive customer data, complex integrations, or open-ended maintenance.

Secondary, exploratory segment: a small web/digital agency with a bounded overflow task such as CMS content migration, browser/device QA, or another output the agency can independently inspect. This could reduce verification risk because the buyer understands the work, but I found no primary evidence in this search establishing Madrid agencies' willingness to buy such work from student teams. It should therefore remain an experiment, not a validated segment.

The decisive unresolved issue is not student population size. It is whether the combination of business-funded price + student compensation + acquisition + coordination + QA + rework + legal/payment administration leaves acceptable economics.

Research method. I treated the supplied markdown as the project baseline, not as independent verification; prioritised current Spanish government/statistical/legal sources; used provider pages only for provider offers/prices; and used original academic studies for recruiter-value evidence. Where direct Madrid transaction evidence was unavailable, I did not substitute stated intention for purchases. I cannot confirm the reported pilot transactions beyond the supplied description because the underlying charters, invoices, bank/payment records, acceptance records and cost logs were not attached. 

Business demand and commitment
For this report, microenterprise means fewer than 10 employees plus no more than €2 million annual turnover or balance-sheet total; small enterprise means fewer than 50 employees plus no more than €10 million turnover or balance sheet, following the official EU/Spanish SME definition. “Sole trader” describes legal form, not size: a self-employed owner can operate a microenterprise. 

Spain had 3,310,824 active enterprises at 1 January 2025. Of these, 1,800,443 had no salaried employee, 901,955 had one or two, 314,941 had three to five and 132,013 had six to nine. INE therefore reports 81.6% with at most two salaried employees. Madrid accounted for 526,588 active enterprises in the regional DIRCE data retrieved for this research; that total should not be converted into a SkillBridge TAM without Madrid-specific size, task-need, budget and buyer-readiness filters. 

Chart calculation: direct plotting of INE DIRCE counts at 1 January 2025; “0” means no salaried employees, not necessarily nobody working in the business. 

The strongest demand-side rationale for a simple website is an observable gap, not demonstrated purchase intent. In Q1 2025, 79.9% of Spanish firms with fewer than ten employees had internet access; among that connected subset, 37.0% had a website and 37.8% used social media. Only 8.6% of the overall sub-ten-employee group sold through e-commerce in the reference period. A business may therefore lack a site for many reasons—low value, reliance on marketplaces/social media, adequate word-of-mouth, DIY capability, postponement, or lack of urgency—not merely because it cannot find a supplier. 

Kit Digital is valuable contradictory evidence. It demonstrates that hundreds of thousands of Spanish firms accepted subsidised digitalisation projects and that “website and basic internet presence” was sufficiently standardised to have a €2,000 category ceiling for the 0–2 employee segment. But the grant paid a large part of the purchase, and new applications ended on 31 October 2025. It is therefore historical evidence of solution-category uptake under subsidy, not present-day unsubsidised demand for SkillBridge. 

Current alternatives also constrain pricing. Malt's live provider data in this research showed an advertised average of about €244/day for active experienced WordPress webmasters and about €168/day for those with 0–2 years' experience; Malt itself warns that local subgroup representativeness can be small, and these are provider-listed averages rather than verified completed-project prices. 
 WordPress.com simultaneously offers low-cost self-service website plans and automated site-building tools, creating a materially cheaper DIY substitute even though software subscription is not comparable to a finished client service.

What the ten-business experiment can establish
The existing rule is operationally useful but statistically imprecise. With ten independent pitches, every conversion changes the observed rate by ten percentage points. Using a two-sided 95% Clopper–Pearson exact binomial interval:

Outcome	Observed conversion	Approx. 95% exact interval	What it establishes
0/10	0%	0–30.8%	No commitment occurred in this offer/cohort; it does not establish zero market demand.
1/10	10%	0.25–44.5%	At least one behavioural commitment; population conversion remains highly uncertain.
2/10	20%	2.5–55.6%	Two actual commitments under the exact offer; still a very weak estimate of population conversion.

For (k>0), the lower exact bound is (B^{-1}(0.025;k,n-k+1)); the upper is (B^{-1}(0.975;k+1,n-k)), with the corresponding boundary case for (k=0). The test is therefore best viewed as a minimum evidence gate, not market-rate estimation.

Likewise, customer-acquisition cost cannot be inferred from 2/10 alone. For a pilot:

[ CAC=\frac{\text{direct outreach spend}+\text{founder acquisition hours}\times\text{hour value}}{\text{business-funded conversions}} ]

At zero conversions, realised CAC per conversion is undefined rather than €0. At one or two conversions, the actual time and cash records must be used; no “realistic CAC” should be inserted retrospectively.

Delivery economics and legal feasibility
The most important legal/product-design question is currently unanswered: who is selling the service, who is performing it, who receives the client's money, who pays the students, and who invoices whom? The answer changes employment, university-placement, Social Security, tax, IP, data-protection and payment obligations.

Employee versus independent-contractor status cannot be selected merely by contract label. Spanish labour law looks to remunerated services performed within another party's organisation and direction, while self-employment is defined around work carried out habitually, personally, directly, on one's own account and outside another party's organisation/direction. SkillBridge's proposed supervision, team assignment, deadlines and QA therefore make factual control important. 

University placements are not administratively free. UPM examples show that the host must execute a cooperation agreement and individual documentation before commencement; one ETSIT policy also specifies compulsory stipends and limits on weekly placement hours. These are school-specific rules, not universal Madrid rates. 
 Students in qualifying academic/formative placements are now within Social Security coverage; the official 2026 table for remunerated placements lists a total monthly contribution of €77.18 in the stated category, of which €65.67 is attributed to the company and €11.51 to the student. Exact applicability to SkillBridge would need confirmation for the chosen placement arrangement. 

For qualifying non-EU students holding a Spanish higher-study authorisation, the immigration guidance states that compatible employment/self-employment may be authorised automatically for higher studies, subject among other things to a maximum 30 hours per week; individual permit status still has to be checked. 

IP also has to be explicit. Spanish law provides that software is initially authored by its creator(s), with common ownership possible for jointly created software; employer ownership has a special rule for software created by salaried employees within their duties. That employer rule should not be assumed to transfer independent students' code automatically to a client. Written client/student/SkillBridge contracts should therefore specify source-code ownership or licence, third-party/open-source dependencies, portfolio display rights and the contribution record. 

For GDPR, any party processing personal data on the client's instructions may require an Article 28 processor arrangement; AEPD stresses that such contracts need specified content rather than a generic statement. A simple brochure site with no production customer dataset materially simplifies this risk. 

Parametric break-even rather than an invented take rate
No campaign price, student compensation, team size or take rate is established, so the appropriate model is:

[ P_{BE}= \frac{ C_{students}+C_{rework}+C_{mentor/QA}+C_{tools}+C_{acquisition}+C_{ongoing}+C_{founder} }{ 1-f_{payment} } ]

where founder time should be shown separately from cash outlay.

Student demand, market sizing and recruiter value
The supplied student evidence is directionally useful but insufficient for sizing. Nine of eleven respondents reportedly struggled to show experience beyond coursework, nine struggled to explain their individual contribution and eight lacked an external user/client on their latest project; only four stated they would review a concrete brief, payment and schedule. The latter is consideration intent, not acceptance of an actual project. 

The supplied market model is:

[ 260,000\times10%\times75%\times50%\times20%=1,950. ]

The more directly matching Ministry SIIU table I found gives 264,733 undergraduate/first-and-second-cycle students in Madrid for 2024/25, marked provisional, rather than the supplied 261,707 anchor. 
 The difference is 3,026 students, or:

[ \frac{264,733-261,707}{261,707}=1.16%. ]

Substituting the newer-found base while leaving every assumption untouched gives:

[ 264,733\times0.10=26,473 ]

[ 26,473\times0.75=19,855 ]

[ 19,855\times0.50=9,927 ]

[ 9,927\times0.20=\mathbf{1,985}. ]

Thus the headline remains roughly 2,000 only because the four assumed conditional rates are unchanged. It is not new evidence for a 2,000-person annual ready market. A 2026 Madrid government release describes nearly 360,000 students across the broader regional university system and 20 public/private institutions, illustrating why undergraduate-only, all-level, institution-location and distance-learning denominators should not be mixed. 

The first bar is official provisional undergraduate/cycle enrolment; every subsequent bar applies an unvalidated SkillBridge assumption. 

A defensible programme rule should be fixed before re-estimating: include informatics/computer science, software engineering and explicitly data-oriented undergraduate degrees whose curriculum matches the tested tasks; exclude broad science/engineering degrees unless separately justified; count double-degree students once where the source permits; distinguish Madrid-based presencial students from students enrolled in national online/UNED provision; and then measure years 2–4, experience/referral gap and immediate paid-project readiness directly. This is a proposed taxonomy, not an official estimate.

FP Superior and bootcamp learners are credible adjacent hypotheses, but they should not be added to this denominator. I did not find within this search a reliable Madrid tech-only count for either that is definitionally comparable with the university base. They should be evaluated later on buyer demand, skills, timetable and legal placement route rather than used to enlarge the headline TAM.

The recruiter-value literature supports caution rather than a causal SkillBridge claim. A 2021 randomised résumé field experiment sent 1,248 fictitious applications to real openings and found that university internship experience increased the probability of an interview invitation by 12.6% on average. 
 Earlier résumé-audit evidence similarly found an internship callback advantage in another labour market. 
 But a 2026 Italian field experiment using 4,066 fictitious résumés found that a particular subsidised Youth Guarantee internship could produce a worse employer signal than a regular internship, demonstrating that labels and programme quality matter. 
 A randomised study of 1,787 engineering students also found effects from earlier professional-work exposure on educational/professional trajectories, but it does not establish that a short project credential causes hiring. 

The justified claim today is therefore: credible workplace experience can affect recruiter behaviour in some contexts. The unjustified leap is: SkillBridge's shareable contribution record improves hiring outcomes. That requires its own recruiter or hiring-outcome study.

The five sources to prioritise for the immediate decision are: the underlying SkillBridge pitch/payment records, which do not yet appear in the materials supplied; INE's 2025 microbusiness digital-adoption data; the actual existing experiment log; current university/legal guidance for the chosen student arrangement; and a fully instrumented first paid delivery. Web market-size research comes after those because none can substitute for SkillBridge's own transaction evidence. 
 

Records SkillBridge must collect itself
The current evidence gaps cannot be repaired by more desk research. The internal evidence file should contain, for each business pitch, a unique prospect ID, qualification basis, decision-maker, exact offer version, actual student profiles shown, price calculation method, pitch timestamp, ten-day deadline, outcome, signed charter and deposit evidence. For every secured project it should contain the client-funded invoice/payment trail, student-payment trail, team roster, student eligibility/status, planned and actual hours by person, founder/mentor/QA hours, revision requests, defects/rework, tools/direct cash cost, delivery date, client acceptance evidence, maintenance/support requests and any refund.

For students, preserve the original survey export before analysing it again. The 23-versus-22 selection discrepancy should be resolved from the raw survey configuration/export, not inferred or corrected. Maintain respondent eligibility fields and anonymous linkage keys so survey, interview and pilot-group overlap can be measured rather than guessed. 

For recruiter evidence, save the artifact shown, recruiter role/company type, evaluation question, response and whether the recruiter was blind to programme participation. Do not translate positive comments into hiring impact without later application/interview/offer outcomes.

What should we do next, what result would change that recommendation, and what claims should we avoid making today? Complete and audit the pre-existing ten-business experiment first; do not reset the denominator or move goalposts. If it produces at least two valid signed-charter-plus-deposit conversions, clear the contracting/payment/legal gate and deliver one tightly scoped, fully instrumented project within a predeclared time/cost envelope before further selling. Exactly one conversion triggers the existing five-business extension; a confirmed 0/10 triggers the historical one-week micro-task/$10 branch. A result that would materially strengthen the recommendation is repeated business-funded commitment followed by on-time client acceptance at a full economic cost compatible with the agreed price; a result that would reverse it is valid 0/10 commitment followed by failure of the micro-task branch, or delivery whose supervision/rework/legal overhead destroys the economics. Today SkillBridge should not claim validated business demand, a 20% or other market conversion rate, approximately 2,000 ready customers/students, proven hiring improvement, a defensible competitive moat, student payments funded by businesses, or profitable unit economics. The evidence currently supports a bounded learning experiment—not scale.
