# Competitive alternatives — what students would choose instead

**Research date:** 2026-10-01 · **Scope:** Madrid-region tech undergraduates,
primarily years 2–4, seeking their first credible examples of practical work.
**Status:** Desk research, September interviews and actual pilot findings.
Campaign commitments and displacement of some personal projects are now
reported; comparative switching rates remain unmeasured. This compares five
representative alternatives, not a competitor census or market-share ranking.

## The choice we are analysing

The [current Job to Be Done](../jtbd.md) is: when an internship application asks
for project examples or something delivered, have practical work the student
can demonstrate and explain to an employer. Payment, a team and a certificate
are possible means of making progress; they are not requirements for an
alternative to compete for that student's time.

SkillBridge's proposed offer is a scoped, paid small-business campaign delivered
by a student team, with a record of work and business feedback. The actual
pilot report [L3] describes concrete commitments, student payment, accepted
delivery and clearer recruiter evaluation. This is early reported feasibility,
with underlying counts, artifacts, costs and budgets missing. Reliable access
at scale, commercial conversion and hiring impact remain unproven. Actual
payment amounts/allocation are unspecified; the prototype quote is illustrative.
See the [pilot assessment](pilot-assessment-2026-10-01.md) for evidence limits.

**Evidence labels:** **Published** means a provider describes the offer; it is
not independent proof of quality or hiring impact. **Reported** means our
teammate-supplied interview notes or user-supplied actual pilot report [L3],
as identified in each cell; underlying records have not been independently
checked. **Hypothesis** identifies our interpretation
of a student's choice. **Unknown** remains an explicit research gap.

## What the comparison changes

1. **Qualify the earlier uniqueness claim.** The September statement that no
   alternative combines the desired features is not established. Riipen's
   documented portfolio feedback and team-project workflow [R1], plus its
   certificate functionality [R3], overlap with core SkillBridge promises.
   Whether any accessible route offers the full proposed combination to this
   Madrid segment remains unanswered. Avoid “the only platform” positioning.
2. **Test access, timing and support as the possible advantage.** A plausible
   offer is: a first suitable paid client project, a bounded commitment, named
   help and permission to show one's contribution. The pilot reports these
   benefits in practice [L3]; recover the records and test recurrence under real
   business funding. The feature bundle is not known to be unique.
3. **Treat existing work and postponement as serious competition.** The current
   notes give more direct evidence about coursework than about platform use.
   The proposed benefit has to justify extra time and responsibility. Payment
   may help one student more than another; the interview findings are mixed.
4. **Measure useful proof, not just certificates issued.** Ask recruiters what
   they can verify about an individual's work from each artifact and what they
   would ask next. A business rating, brand name or polished demo does not by
   itself prove a better hiring outcome.

The practical first direction is to **complement applications and familiar
student workflows**, while testing a paid client campaign against the best
alternative actually available to each student. An unavailable foreign
programme should not be presented as a choice the student could make today.

## Concrete switching tests and decisions

These are comparison protocols, not new precommitted success thresholds. The
pilot reports actions relevant to CA2 and CA4 [L3], but does not establish that
these exact comparative protocols were run or supply switching rates. For each participant, first reconstruct a recent choice
without showing SkillBridge. Then present actual available options with their
real dates, hours, pay/cost, support, eligibility and output. Rotate presentation
order, permit combinations of options, and include keeping the current approach.

| Test | Evidence that would support switching | Evidence that would challenge it | Resulting action |
|---|---|---|---|
| **CA1: project access and support** | An eligible student with access to both routes chooses the real campaign, accepts its terms and identifies a concrete unmet need in the other route. | Their current route already supplies an adequate project, guidance and evidence; they prefer its credit or lower burden. | Narrow the proposition or explore complementing the institutional route. If only SkillBridge is accessible, record access-driven demand separately from competitive preference. |
| **CA2: campaign alongside applications** | The student reserves feasible hours and follows through while continuing the internship search. | It displaces a suitable placement or costs more preparation time than the student will accept. | Target the interval before a placement; reduce or reschedule work where feasible. Compare the final work sample, not application volume alone. |
| **CA3: simulation versus campaign** | After seeing actual terms and outputs, the student accepts live client responsibility for a specific additional benefit. | A short simulation adequately satisfies the immediate need, or the student prefers its flexibility. | Use simulation as optional preparation or focus on needs it does not satisfy. Do not interpret compliments about SkillBridge as switching. |
| **CA4: improve existing work versus start a campaign** | An observed bottleneck is removed, and the student produces attributable, shareable client work with acceptable extra effort. | Their existing project can provide comparably useful evidence sooner, or SkillBridge adds coordination without benefit. | Reuse the current workflow and offer only the missing support; revise the need for a new campaign. |
| **CA5: now versus later** | A concrete deadline and a feasible offer lead to the readiness actions already defined in [the market validation plan](analysis.md). | The student repeatedly defers after suitable offers, or the stated problem has no current consequence. | Revisit urgency, timing and A4; retain non-responses in the correct offer denominator and do not count hypothetical interest as adoption. |

Record anonymous participant ID, campus/programme/year, recent trigger, current
alternative, options genuinely available, exact offer terms, choice and reason,
what was given up, next action and follow-through. Obtain a work sample,
compatible availability and acceptance of actual terms before calling it a
readiness commitment. Track completion and client acceptance separately.

Report counts and denominators by prior alternative and recruitment source.
Small convenience samples reveal reasons and failures; they do not establish
Madrid-wide preference shares. Do not double-count students who use more than
one route. The five rows are alternatives, not five disjoint market segments.

## Connection to the market estimate and the business side

The [piano-tuners estimate](README.md) assumes that 20% of otherwise eligible
students commit when shown a suitable offer. Alternatives help explain why that
assumption could fail. Do **not** subtract separate guessed “competitor market
shares” from the estimate: substitution and availability already affect that
commitment rate. Use observed choices to revise A4 with the same denominator and
time window; new counts of eligibility belong in A1–A3 instead.

This table follows the student-first scope. Businesses have their own choices:
existing staff/friends and maintaining the current setup appear in UV-B01 and
UV-B02 [L1]. Those accounts do not establish current paid project supply. Keep
the [business charter-and-deposit experiment](../experiment-card.md) separate;
student preference cannot validate a business's willingness to fund a campaign.
The pilot adds business interest, accepted delivery and student payment, but
reports unsigned charters [L3] (P030). Reconcile the funding source and original
pitch/deadline log before scoring the commercial test.

## Coverage and remaining gaps

Five rows were chosen to cover the four categories in the example, including a
local institutional route and the workflow already reported by our users.
Freelance marketplaces, self-sourced client work, hackathons, open source,
student consultancies and other work-experience platforms remain candidates for
a later pass. Their omission does not imply irrelevance or lack of competition.
Ask what students actually considered; replace a representative row if another
route appears more frequently or creates a different switching problem.

Parker Dewey was screened as another project-work benchmark. Its public offer
describes paid micro-internships [P1], but the reviewed programme eligibility is
US-oriented [P2]. That is insufficient evidence of a generally available Madrid
route, and individual eligibility was not assessed. It is not counted as a
sixth alternative in this first table or labelled globally unavailable.

**Still missing:** local named-competitor usage evidence, independent review
samples, logged-in demos, live vacancy/project audits and underlying pilot
records. Offer acceptances, delivery and recruiter comparisons are now reported
in [L3], but their counts and source artifacts remain missing. Provider
descriptions establish features only. Survey willingness is distinct from
reported actions, and synthetic persona conversations are excluded.
