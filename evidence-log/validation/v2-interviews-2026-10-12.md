## V2-S01

**Interview date:** 2026-10-05. **PDF pages:** 1-2.

**Participant context:** Student; IE CSAI according to the source heading. Year of study and prior relevant internship history are not recorded.

**Original source heading:** Script 1 — IE CSAI Student


**Source script context before Q1 (not an answer):**

> Opening: "We're students working on a project about how people our age get their first
> tech role. I'd like to ask about things you've actually done recently — there's nothing
> to sell you. Can I take notes without your name?"


### V2-S01-Q1

Think about the last internship or junior role you applied to. Walk me through what the
application actually asked you for.

> "It was a junior backend role at a startup here in Madrid, through LinkedIn. CV
> obviously, then a field for a portfolio or GitHub link, and at the bottom a box saying
> 'tell us about a project you've worked on and what your role was.' Max 300 words. I
> spent longer on that box than on the rest of the form."


### V2-S01-Q2

What did you put in that box, or link to? Walk me through what you chose and why that
one.

> "I put the group project from my web development class — we built a booking site for a
> gym, a made-up gym. It was the only thing I had that looked finished. I remember
> thinking it's obviously a uni project and they're going to know that, but what else am I
> going to put, the Python exercises? So I wrote it up to sound as real as I could."


### V2-S01-Q3

Did anyone — a form, a recruiter, an interviewer — ask you to explain what you
personally did on it? What happened?

> "Yeah. In the first call he asked 'so which parts did you write?' and I kind of froze.
> We did it together, we were all in the same room for two weeks, we all touched
> everything. I said I did most of the frontend and some of the database, but I could hear
> myself and it sounded vague. He didn't follow up, he just moved on, which felt worse. I
> had nothing to show him that proved it was me."


### V2-S01-Q4

What happened with that application? And did you do anything differently afterwards?

> "Nothing. I never heard back, not even a rejection. That was March. After that I started
> a side project on my own — a thing that tracks football stats — specifically so I'd have
> something that was only mine. I got about 60% through it. I also asked a friend from my
> year who got into a consultancy if he could refer me, which felt a bit desperate but
> whatever."


### V2-S01-Q5

In the last six months, what have you built to have something to show? Take the most
recent one — how it started, roughly how much time, how it ended.

> "The football stats thing. It started because I saw a tutorial and thought I could do a
> better version. I worked on it three weekends in a row, pretty intensely, then exams hit
> and I stopped. It's still sitting in a branch. The annoying part is I don't even know if
> what I built is any good — nobody's looked at it. There's no point where someone says
> yeah, that's done, that's fine. So you either keep adding stuff or you stop."


### V2-S01-Q6

Have you used Forage, HackerRank, Devpost, Kaggle, anything like that? Walk me through
the last time.

> "I did two Forage ones, the JPMorgan one and a Deloitte one. They take four or five
> hours and at the end you compare your answer to the model answer and it says well done.
> It's fine, but it felt fake. Nobody checked anything I did. I put them on LinkedIn and
> nobody has ever mentioned them to me."


### V2-S01-Q7

Have you ever paid money for something to improve your chances of getting hired? What,
how much, and what made you decide right then?

> "I paid for LeetCode Premium for two months, about 35 a month. I did it because I had a
> technical interview coming up in two weeks and I panicked. And I bought a React course
> on Udemy on sale for 15 euros, although I did about a third of it. So yeah, I've paid
> when there was something specific coming up."


**Source script context after this answer (not an answer):**

> Now describe the idea, flat, including the price: "A company gives us a real, small
> brief — about four hours of work. You do it, alone or in a team of up to three.
> Automated tests and an AI rubric score every submission, and an engineer at that company
> signs off the rubric and reviews the top ones. You get a score, a breakdown, written
> feedback, and a record of what you personally did, on a link you can share. The score is
> free; the certified version costs €15."


### V2-S01-Q8

First reaction — what would make you not bother?

> "Timing, mainly. If it's November and I've got two deliverables due, four hours is a
> lot. And I'd want to know who the company is — if it's a name people recognise, the
> score actually means something to a recruiter. The other thing is what happens if I do
> badly. If a bad score is attached to my name somewhere public, that's worse for me than
> not doing it at all."


### V2-S01-Q9

Say you'd done it and the score came back. What would you actually do with it?

> "I'd put the link in my CV, under projects. And on LinkedIn — people post certificates
> on there constantly. And if I got to an interview I'd bring up the feedback, like, an
> engineer at this company said this about my code. That's a much better thing to say than
> 'I built a gym booking site for class.'"


### V2-S01-Q10

The certified result is €15. Is that something you'd pay for? What would it have to do
to be worth it? Can I send you the link when the pilot opens on 16 October?

> "Fifteen euros is two coffees. If I'd already done the work and the feedback was real —
> an actual engineer wrote it, not generated — I'd pay that, especially if I can put it on
> LinkedIn. What would make it worth it is the feedback being specific, like telling me
> what a junior at a real company would have done differently. Yeah, send me the link."


### V2-S01-Q11

If you saw this on a classmate's profile, what would make you think it meant nothing?

> "If everyone in my year had one. The second it's on fifty profiles it's noise, like
> those LinkedIn course badges. And if the money is what gets you the certificate rather
> than the work, then it's worthless, obviously. It has to be possible to fail it."


**Source script context after this answer (not an answer):**

> Close: "Who else should I talk to — anyone in your year applying right now?"


No response to the closing referral question is recorded. Q10 records agreement
to receive a pilot link; no link delivery, registration, submission or payment
is documented.


## V2-R01

**Interview date:** 2026-10-06. **PDF pages:** 2-4.

**Participant context:** Recruiter. The source introduction states tech intern/junior screening within the last 12 months; answers describe a summer internship intake.

**Original source heading:** Script 2 — Recruiter at Edukko “Tamara”


**Source script context before Q1 (not an answer):**

> Has screened at least one tech intern or junior in the last 12 months. 15 minutes. Bring
> two anonymised profiles: same CV, one with a SkillBridge certificate attached. Opening:
> "We're a university team researching how tech interns get screened. Fifteen minutes, no
> pitch — at the end I'll show you a mock profile and ask what you'd do with it. Can I
> take notes without your name or your company's?"


### V2-R01-Q1

The last intern or junior opening you screened — roughly how many applications, and how
long did you spend on each at the first pass?

> "For the summer internship we had around 400 applications for six places. First pass is
> me and one other person and we're spending ninety seconds each, less. You scan for a few
> things and if none of them are there you move on. It's not a careful process, I'll be
> honest with you."


### V2-R01-Q2

Walk me through the last candidate you rejected at CV stage that you weren't sure about.
What was missing?

> "There was one recently — good degree, decent grades, and the projects section was three
> lines of 'developed a web application using React and Node.' So did four hundred other
> people. There was nothing I could look at, nothing that told me whether this person can
> build something or whether they followed a tutorial. I'd have interviewed them if
> there'd been anything concrete. That one bugged me."


### V2-R01-Q3

When a candidate's only experience is coursework and personal projects, what do you do
with that? Do you open the GitHub link?

> "Sometimes. Maybe one in ten, if something else caught my eye first. And when I do open
> it, half the time it's a group project and I genuinely cannot tell which parts were
> theirs — the commits are all over the place, or there's one massive commit from one
> person. So it doesn't tell me much, which is why I usually don't bother."


### V2-R01-Q4

Do you run a take-home or technical assessment? At what stage, how long, and what does
it cost your team in hours?

> "Yes, after the first screen. It's meant to be three hours. Two of our engineers review
> them, twenty minutes each, so for a round of twenty candidates that's a lot of
> engineering time and they hate doing it. The drop-off is brutal too, we lose about a
> third who never submit. And now there's the AI problem — I don't know anymore whether
> I'm assessing the candidate or a model."


### V2-R01-Q5

Has a certificate or credential on a profile ever changed a decision you made? Which
one, and what happened?

> "AWS, yeah. Someone had the Solutions Architect associate and that moved them forward,
> because it's a real exam, it's proctored, you can't fake it, and it told me they'd put
> in the hours. Most of the others I skip. The difference is there's a pass mark that
> means something and somebody independent checked."


**Source script context after this answer (not an answer):**

> Now show both profiles, without explaining them.


### V2-R01-Q6

Same role, both of these in your pile. Which do you take forward, and why?

> "This one. There's something here I can actually look at — it says what the task was,
> what they scored, and there's a note from an engineer who read the code. The other one
> I'm just reading adjectives the candidate wrote about themselves."


### V2-R01-Q7

What in there do you actually trust, and what would you skip over?

> "The written feedback is the useful part, assuming a real person wrote it. The number
> I'd skip — 82 out of 100 means nothing to me on its own, I don't know what the
> distribution is. And I'd want to know whose rubric it is. If it's the hiring company's
> rubric and their engineer signed it, that's the part with weight."


### V2-R01-Q8

Would you accept this instead of your take-home? If not, what would it take?

> "Maybe — if the brief came from us and one of our engineers had signed it off. Then it's
> basically our take-home, done earlier. I'd still want thirty minutes live to check they
> can talk about what they wrote. But it would save the review time, which is the
> expensive part."


### V2-R01-Q9

What would make you suspect this was gamed, or that AI did most of it?

> "If I saw three of them and the submissions all looked the same. Or if there's no live
> component at all — anything done unsupervised at home right now, I assume a model
> helped. The fix is someone being able to explain their own work for ten minutes."


### V2-R01-Q10

If candidates from a university you recruit from routinely had these, would you start
asking for them? And would your company pay for access to candidates' results — out of
which budget, instead of what?

> "If it were standard at IE I'd notice its absence, which is the same thing. Paying —
> possibly. We already pay for LinkedIn Recruiter and we sponsored a hackathon last year,
> about eight thousand out of the talent-brand budget. If this replaced the take-home
> review time I could make that case. My head of talent signs off anything over five."


Q6-Q9 record reactions to a mock-profile comparison. The exact profiles shown
are not included in the PDF; no actual shortlist or hiring decision is recorded.


## V2-E01

**Interview date:** 2026-10-10. **PDF pages:** 4-5.

**Participant context:** Company engineer. Answers identify a possible challenge and review criteria; the respondent says payment is not their decision.

**Original source heading:** Script 3 — Nice strategies engineer “Jose Carlos”


**Source script context before Q1 (not an answer):**

> Engineer at a SaaS, engineering or data company who could own a brief. 10 minutes. You
> need a brief and a rubric out of this conversation. Opening: "We're a university team
> running a two-week pilot. I'm not asking for money or a hire — I'm asking whether
> there's a small piece of work you'd be curious to see ten students attempt, and whether
> you'd say what 'good' looks like."


### V2-E01-Q1

Is there something in your backlog that's real but nobody has time for — self-contained,
no production access, no customer data, maybe four hours for a decent junior?

> "Actually, yes. We've got a CSV import thing — customers send us messy spreadsheets and
> someone cleans them by hand every time. A script that validates the file and flags the
> bad rows would be genuinely useful, and it's been in the backlog about a year because
> it's never urgent. I could give you three example files with nothing sensitive in them."


### V2-E01-Q2

If ten students sent you solutions to that, what would you look at first to decide whose
was good? Give me four or five things.

> "Does it run on a file I give it that it hasn't seen. Does it handle the broken rows
> sensibly instead of crashing. Is the code readable — would I understand it in six
> months. Did they write any tests, even one. And is there a readme that tells me how to
> run it, because you'd be amazed. Those five, in that order."


### V2-E01-Q3

How long would it take you to review the top three? And what would make you say no to
doing this a second time?

> "Twenty minutes each, so about an hour. I'd do it again — honestly if one of them was
> good I'd want to talk to them, we're always looking. What would make me say no is if it
> turned into a thing with meetings and coordination. Send me three zips and a deadline
> and I'm fine."


### V2-E01-Q4

If an AI scored all ten against your criteria and you only saw the top three — would you
trust the filter? What would you check?

> "I'd spot-check one from the bottom, just to see that it's actually bad and the thing
> isn't ranking randomly. If that holds up once or twice I'd stop worrying about it."


### V2-E01-Q5

Who owns what the students produce? Is that a conversation you'd have to have with
someone?

> "I'd assume they keep it, it's their code. If we ever wanted to actually use the script
> I'd need to ask legal and that's a two-week thing. Easier if we say up front that it
> stays theirs and we're not using it."


### V2-E01-Q6

Would you interview someone off the back of this? And is this something a company would
pay for, instead of what?

> "If someone nailed it, yes, I'd forward them to our recruiter — that's free sourcing for
> us. Paying isn't my call. We spend on hackathon sponsorships, a few thousand last year,
> and we pay for a screening tool. If it saved the engineers the take-home review there's
> an argument. Talk to our talent person."


**Source script context after this answer (not an answer):**

> The ask before you hang up: "Can I send you four to six criteria in writing by Thursday
> and get a yes or an edit back? That's the only commitment I need."


No response to the final written-rubric request is recorded. The proposed
example files, written brief and rubric sign-off are not included.
