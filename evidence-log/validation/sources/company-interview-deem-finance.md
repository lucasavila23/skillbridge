# SkillBridge — Company Interview Script

2026-10-12

##Before the interview
Who: a recruiter who has screened at least one tech intern or junior in the last 12 months.
Length: 15 minutes.
Bring: two anonymised profiles for question 10: the same CV twice, one with a SkillBridge certificate attached.
Rule: no pitch. Show the mock profiles only at the end, without explaining them.

## Opening

> "We're a university team researching how tech interns get screened. Fifteen minutes, no pitch. At the end I'll show you a mock profile and ask what you'd do with it.

Record their answer to the consent question.

## Questions

### How you hire today

1. How do you currently find and shortlist junior talent or interns?
2. For your last intern or junior opening, roughly how many applications did you get, and how long did you spend on each at the first pass?
3. When you look at a junior CV, how confident are you that it reflects what they can actually do?
4. Do you default to candidates from certain universities? Why?

### The cost of getting it wrong

5. Have you ever hired someone who looked great on paper but couldn't do the job? What did that cost you?
6. Do you run a take-home test? How long is it, and how many engineer hours does reviewing it take?
7. When a candidate's best work is a group project, how do you tell what they personally did?

### What you've tried

8. Have you ever sponsored or run a hackathon to scout talent? How did it go?
9. Has a certificate on a profile ever changed a decision you made? Which one, and why did it count?

### Reaction to SkillBridge

10. *(Show both profiles without explaining them.)* Same role, both of these are in your pile. Which do you take forward, and why?
11. Would seeing a candidate's scored performance on a realistic challenge change how you shortlist?
12. What would make you trust an external scoring system enough to act on it?
13. What would make you suspect a result was gamed, or that AI did most of the work?
14. Would you accept this instead of your take-home test? If not, what would it take?

### Payment

15. Would you pay for ongoing access to a pool of scored, opted-in candidates? What feels reasonable?
16. Which budget would that come from, who signs it off, and what would it replace?

## Sample answers: Deem Finance 

**Participant:** a recruiter at a fintech company who screened interns in the last 12 months.



| # | Question | Answer |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | How you find and shortlist | Mostly LinkedIn and a university careers portal, plus employee referrals. One recruiter does the first pass and a hiring manager checks the shortlist. “Honestly, it's quick and a bit rough.” |
| 2 | Applications and time per CV | About 250 applications for four internship places; around two minutes per CV at the first pass |
| 3 | Confidence in a junior CV | Not very. Most project sections read the same, like “built a dashboard in Python,” and there's no way to tell a tutorial from real work |
| 4 | Defaulting to certain universities | Somewhat. Hiring managers favour a few Madrid business and engineering schools because past hires worked out. It's a shortcut when nothing else can be checked |
| 5 | A hire who looked good on paper | Yes. Last year an intern with a strong CV struggled with basic SQL, and it took a month of a senior analyst's time before that was clear |
| 6 | Take-home test and review cost | Yes, a 2-hour data task after the first screen. One engineer reviews each in about 25 minutes. Around a quarter of candidates never submit, and they suspect some use AI |
| 7 | Telling who did what in group work | Usually can't. They ask in the interview, but answers are vague, and they rarely open GitHub |
| 8 | Sponsoring a hackathon | Co-sponsored a university datathon last year, roughly €5,000. Good for the brand, but there was no follow-up process and they hired no one from it |
| 9 | A certificate that changed a decision | A CFA Level I did, because it's a hard, independent exam. A Google Data Analytics certificate didn't change anything |
| 10 | Profile comparison | Picks the certified profile: they can see the task and what an engineer thought of the work. The CV-only profile is just claims |
| 11 | Would a challenge score change shortlisting? | Yes, if the challenge looks like their actual work, such as data cleaning or reconciliation, not coding puzzles |
| 12 | What would make the score trustworthy | Their own rubric, an engineer's written note, the actual code, and seeing how the candidate ranked against others. A bare number would be ignored |
| 13 | Signs of gaming or AI | Near-identical code across candidates, or a suspiciously polished result for a four-hour task. Would want a 15-minute walkthrough |
| 14 | Replacing the take-home | Possibly, for the first screen, if their own team wrote the brief. They would still keep a live technical interview |
| 15 | Paying for access | Maybe, but per hire or per challenge rather than a subscription. Would compare it with what they pay for job boards |
| 16 | Budget, sign-off, what it replaces | The campus and employer-branding budget, approved by the HR director. It would replace part of the datathon sponsorship and some take-home review time |
