# Short student and business interviews

**Prepared:** 2026-09-22

**Status:** Interview guide for further research. Four teammate-reported
interview records were subsequently supplied on 2026-09-22; see the
[notes](interviews-2026-09-22.md) and [findings](findings-2026-09-22.md).
Those conversations reportedly occurred September 15–20, before this guide was
prepared; use of this script in those interviews has not been established.

## People to approach

- **IE University tech students:** the user proposed this recruitment pool.
  Confirm each participant's relevant coursework, experience and current
  application situation during the conversation.
- **Baya coffee shop:** the user proposed this business as a contact. Confirm
  the respondent's role and whether they decide on digital projects and spending.

Aim to hear different situations: students building projects regularly,
students facing an application deadline, and a business decision-maker. These
may help challenge the Tinkerer, Late Sprinter and Guarded Owner hypotheses.
Do not assign people a persona before hearing their experience.

## Opening — about 15 seconds

"We're working on a student project about getting practical experience and
helping small businesses get projects done. Could we ask a few questions about
your experience? Is it okay if we take notes without including your name?"

Allow roughly 5–8 minutes. Ask about past behaviour before describing the
proposed service. Follow the person's answer rather than trying to get a yes.

## Student interview

1. **Trigger:** "What happened the last time you looked for an internship or
   job? Was there a requirement that changed what you did next?"
2. **Current approach and skills:** "Tell me about your most recent coursework
   or personal project. What did you personally build, what did you adapt, and
   where did you need help?"
3. **Commitment:** "How did that project fit around your other commitments?
   What kept it moving, or made it stall? How did it end?"
4. **Practical outcome:** "Who actually used or reviewed the result? What did
   they ask you to change?" If useful, invite them to show a non-sensitive
   example they are comfortable sharing.
5. **Motivation:** "Why did you choose that project over the other things you
   could have done? Has being paid ever changed which opportunity you chose?"

After these questions, give a short description:

"We're exploring small, paid business projects completed by student teams."

Then ask: "What would you need to know before deciding whether to take one on,
and what could rule it out for you?"

That last answer is hypothetical interest or concern, not evidence that the
student will join, sustain their hours or deliver acceptable work.

## Business interview — intended for Baya's decision-maker

1. **Recent need:** "What's the last website, booking, menu or other digital
   task you needed done? What made it matter at that point?" Accept that there
   may be no relevant task.
2. **Current alternative:** "How did you handle it? Who did the work, and what
   happened?"
3. **Friction:** "What worked well, and what caused trouble? If you've paid
   someone for this kind of work before, how did that go?"
4. **Acceptance:** "How did you decide the job was finished properly? What
   did you need to check or change?"
5. **Commitment and trust:** "Before agreeing to that work, what did you need
   to know about the person doing it, the scope and the price?"

Then describe the same student-team idea and ask:

"What would you need to see before considering a student team? What would
make that unsuitable for your business?"

Do not imply that a team, price, delivery date or past success story has been
secured. This conversation is not a formal project offer.

## Capture and interpret the answers

Use a fresh copy of the [interview record template](interview-record-template.md)
for each actual participant.

- Record the actual date, an anonymous participant ID, role and source notes.
- Distinguish **reported behaviour**, **direct observation**, **stated interest**
  and **team interpretation**. Use quotation marks only for words recorded
  accurately; otherwise label the text as a paraphrase.
- Note what supports a claim and what contradicts or limits it. "No relevant
  experience" or "not interested" are useful findings too.
- Link each finding to a specific [JTBD element](../jtbd.md), persona claim or
  [assumption](../assumptions.md), and record any proposed change separately.
- A conversation can provide evidence about that person's experience. It does
  not establish delivery competence, sustained participation or demand across
  the market on its own.

## Keep the existing commercial test separate

An owner's interest in student work is not a deposit conversion. The
[Experiment Card](../experiment-card.md) requires both a signed charter and a
real 10% deposit within the response window, with a primary threshold of at
least two qualifying conversions from exactly ten pitched businesses.

An exploratory Baya interview is not automatically one of those formal pitches.
If a later conversation becomes a qualifying pitch, document it explicitly and
apply the same pricing method, deposit percentage and commitment conditions.
