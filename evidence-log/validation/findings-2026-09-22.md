# Findings from four teammate-reported interviews

**Reviewed:** 2026-09-22

**Source:** [Interview notes](interviews-2026-09-22.md), reported as conducted
between September 15 and September 20. Two IE students, Baya's owner and
Nolita's manager. Exact individual dates and original source materials remain
unavailable.

**Evidence strength:** Provisional participant-reported evidence for the
specific experiences described. Future preferences are stated intentions.
There are no independently observed project-delivery or payment results.

## JTBD implications

- Refine the trigger from an explicitly demanding job description to an
  application asking for project examples or delivered work. This is the event
  actually described in the notes.
- The desired progress is a practical example students can explain and
  demonstrate to an employer. The specific student-team business-project
  solution remains a team proposal.
- Current alternatives are coursework and collaborative student projects.
  Neither note establishes a solo-project default or use of SkillBridge.
- Keep money as one possible pull. Preserve the difference between the two
  students' motivations.
- Keep lack of client pressure as a working interpretation of Habit. The
  notes directly support manageable assignments and academic deadlines, but
  do not establish that avoiding a business caused the original project choice.
- Anxiety now has stated anchors: skills/support, team composition, finite
  commitments, portfolio permission, timing and expectations for urgent fixes.

## Consequences for the venture and next test

- **Product direction:** These accounts are relevant to gaining demonstrable
  experience and completing useful business work. They do not show that the
  product goal has been achieved or that the model is validated.
- **Business qualification:** Confirm a current unresolved task, a meaningful
  reason to outsource and decision authority before counting a formal pitch.
  Baya's past menu episode and Nolita's current lack of a task must stay distinct.
- **Student delivery:** Before relying on availability or coursework skills,
  inspect actual work and observe a bounded task, including the support and
  rework required. A stated few hours per week is not a measured commitment.
- **Handover and support:** Define who can edit the finished work and who owns
  future fixes before making an offer. The interviews identify this as a
  question to resolve, not a support service already promised.
- **Current sprint and commercial risk:** Business willingness to commit money
  remains untested. The existing business-trust Sprint Goal and deposit test
  therefore remain relevant; student capability and availability are explicit
  delivery risks to address before accepting work. These findings do not by
  themselves justify switching the primary test or changing its threshold.
