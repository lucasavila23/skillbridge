# Persona: The Late Sprinter

**Version:** 0.2 — 2026-09-22

**Grounding status:** Hypothesis-heavy, with limited participant-reported overlap.

**Use:** Research rehearsal and planning real-user validation.

This is one bounded behavioural hypothesis about a late-stage tech student
racing a graduation or internship deadline. It does not represent a verified
individual or all tech students. Proposed differences from
[The Tinkerer](tinkerer.md) concern urgency, team preferences, tool behaviour and
retention; earlier or more gradual Tinkerer engagement has not been established.

Version 0.2 reviews UV-S02, supplied as a teammate's interview notes. The user
identifies Student 2 as an IE student interviewed within September 15–20.
Deadline-concentrated coursework and timing concerns partly overlap with this
hypothesis, but graduation proximity, urgent search, certification dependence
and one-shot retention are unconfirmed. Student 2 is not labelled a validated
Late Sprinter.

## Target-user profile contribution

Unless a source is specified, the following profile remains a **working
hypothesis from the 2026-09-20 exercise**, not a description established for
Student 2.

- **Context, frequency and trigger:** A tech student late in their academic
  career — technically skilled but with almost no applied, real-world project
  experience. The situation is one-time and bounded, not recurring: it is
  triggered when a graduation date or internship requirement closes in and the
  student realises they are behind their peers on demonstrable experience.
- **Progress sought:** Get verifiable real-world project evidence on their CV
  fast enough to still land an internship before time runs out.
- **Most relevant current behaviour:** Crams a solo portfolio project while
  simultaneously applying to internships, racing to see whichever lands first.
  This is a working assumption, not a documented episode.
- **Current alternative:** The solo cram project itself, run alongside blind
  internship applications — there is no separate tool or workaround.
- **Main friction or consequence:** Solo cram projects do not resemble a real
  working scenario, and recruiters can tell — it counts against the CV rather
  than for it. This converges directionally with SUPPORTED desk research
  already in [Opportunity](../opportunity.md) (recruiters discount unverified
  experience claims), but that convergence does not make this student's
  specific account grounded.
- **Tool or service behaviour:** Under time pressure, follows platform
  defaults and avoids extra setup or configuration overhead — the opposite
  pattern from the Tinkerer, who adapts and customises tools.
- **Trust and control pattern:** Accepts fast, low-vetting matching into a
  team or project, trading placement control for speed — again the opposite
  of the Tinkerer's preference for control over technical decisions.
- **Verification and recovery pattern:** Would disengage from the platform if
  matching is slow, or if a project is too long or complicated for the
  remaining runway.
- **Failure threshold:** No certification issued at the end counts as failure,
  regardless of whether the project itself was completed.
- **Evidence anchors:** [UV-S02 Q1–Q6](../validation/interviews-2026-09-22.md#uv-s02)
  supplies partial overlap and limits, detailed below. It does not establish the
  proposed solo cram project, near-graduation timing or churn pattern.
- **Working assumptions:** One-time, bounded engagement; churns off the
  platform once an internship is secured (a one-shot user, not a sustained
  participant); accepts either a solo project or a small/fast team project
  interchangeably — the binding constraint is speed and low complexity, not
  whether the work is solo or team-based.
- **Known unknowns:** Whether engaging with SkillBridge under this profile
  actually improves the odds of landing an internship — the central question
  this persona exists to test.
- **Decision this Persona may affect:** Whether SkillBridge should offer a
  fast/small project track (solo or team — either satisfies this persona)
  with guaranteed certification on short engagements, alongside the standard
  full-length team-campaign model. A persona response cannot settle this; it
  requires real evidence.

## Evidence boundaries

### GROUNDED

These anchors are **participant-reported** in teammate-shared notes, not
independently observed work or a verified transcript:

- Q1: a summer-internship application requested an example of something
  delivered; the student struggled to provide one.
- Q2–Q4: a class Python dashboard involved data cleaning and charts, with a
  teammate handling deployment. Effort concentrated near the deadline, with
  no further work reported after submission and no use outside the course.
- Q5: the assignment seemed manageable; an interview example is currently
  prioritised, with pay described as helpful.
- Q6: timing, avoiding finals and avoiding immediate-fix obligations are
  stated conditions. A few hours per week is hypothetical availability.

This supports investigating deadline-related participation constraints. It does
not show an imminent graduation deadline or abandoning client work after an
internship offer. The reported project was collaborative coursework, not the
solo portfolio cram project in the original hypothesis.

### WORKING ASSUMPTIONS

The near-graduation trigger, solo cram alternative, low-vetting matching,
platform-default preference, certification requirement and "one-shot, then
churn" retention pattern remain planning hypotheses from 2026-09-20. Partial
overlap with UV-S02 must not be used to confirm those unreported attributes.

### UNKNOWN

The most important gap is whether engagement under this profile actually
improves internship-landing odds. No completion rate, matching-speed
tolerance, or actual churn event has been documented.

## Assumptions this persona should help challenge

1. **Sustained commitment (Area 1):** SkillBridge's core model assumes
   students commit real, sustained hours to a campaign. This persona is
   structurally the opposite case — a one-shot user who disengages the moment
   an internship is secured. Whether that is really how such students behave,
   and whether the platform can still extract value from a one-shot
   engagement, remains open.
2. **Credential credibility (Area 3):** This persona's central unknown —
   whether a verified campaign record actually helps a recruiter or ATS
   pipeline say yes — is Area 3's core must-be-true claim, tested here from
   the demand side rather than the recruiter side.

These correspond to Area 1 and Area 3 in
[the assumption map](../assumptions.md). This selection defines the
persona's research focus; it does not establish a new venture-wide risk
ranking or a completed test.

## Exactly three real-user validation priorities

1. **Sustained-commitment reality check:** Find a student genuinely near
   graduation with no internship secured. Ask them to walk through what
   they're actually doing right now, and whether they would disengage from
   any program the moment they land an offer.
2. **Speed vs. structure trade-off:** Present this student profile with two
   concrete options — a short solo project vs. a short small-team project,
   both carrying the same guaranteed certification — and observe which they
   actually pick and why.
3. **Outcome causality:** Find a student or recent graduate who completed a
   short, verified real-world project close to a deadline, and ask whether it
   actually surfaced in their internship search — was it mentioned,
   referenced, or did an employer ever respond to it specifically.
