## Job to Be Done

When a tech student whose experience comes from coursework and personal projects
is applying for internships or jobs, a listing explicitly requiring real-world
project experience prompts them to seek practical experience they can use to
demonstrate their ability to meet business needs.

## Working definitions and evidence status

| Lens | Element | Working definition | Basis or status |
|---|---|---|---|
| How the job begins | Circumstance | A tech student is applying for internships or jobs with experience from coursework and personal projects. | Team interpretation — carried forward from the persona answers; no recent real episode documented. |
| How the job begins | Trigger | They read an internship or job description explicitly requiring real-world projects and experience. | Team interpretation — specific trigger supplied by the user during the JTBD exercise; not linked to an observed application episode. |
| Progress | Desired progress | Gain practical experience they can use to demonstrate their ability to meet business needs when applying. | Team interpretation — synthesis of the user's experience goal, business-practicality concern and application context; wording confirmed by the user. |
| Progress | Solution hired | Personal projects and coursework are the hypothesised current approach for gaining experience. | Team interpretation — alternatives supplied by the user; actual use in the focal circumstance remains unverified. |
| Forces compelling change | Push | Their existing coursework and personal projects may lack the technical depth and business practicality needed to demonstrate the experience requested. | Team interpretation — combines the user's stated friction with the proposed trigger; its actual consequences remain unverified. |
| Forces compelling change | Pull | Earn money while gaining practical experience. | Team interpretation — the user identified a money incentive; no observed choice or change in commitment supports its effect yet. |
| Forces opposing change | Habit | Personal projects and coursework are familiar ways to gain experience without a business client depending on delivery. | Team interpretation — the user identified the absence of business pressure; academic deadlines may still apply. |
| Forces opposing change | Anxiety | They may be unsure whether coursework-level skills are enough to deliver work that meets a real business's expectations. | Team interpretation — inference from the skill-depth concern and added client responsibility, accepted by the user as a hypothesis; no reported real-user concern. |

**Relevant progress dimensions:** Functional — gain practical experience and
demonstrate its relevance to business needs. This is a team interpretation.
Emotional and social progress dimensions are not yet established.

## Validation gaps

- No recent real student episode establishes the trigger, current approach,
  decision or outcome. The eight elements above are not validated customer facts.
- Whether personal projects and coursework actually fall short, and whether
  later practical work improves the student's ability to demonstrate relevant
  experience, remain unverified.
- The effect of a money incentive on choosing an opportunity and sustaining
  commitment has not been observed. No effective amount or payment condition
  has been established.
- The proposed habit and anxiety need to be checked against actual behaviour
  and reported concerns. No actual switching decision has been documented.
