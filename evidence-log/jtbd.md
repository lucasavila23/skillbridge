## Job to Be Done

When a tech student with mainly coursework experience applies for internships
and is asked for project examples or something they have delivered, they want
practical work they can demonstrate and explain to an employer.

This refines the earlier trigger: both supplied accounts describe requests
during applications, rather than a job listing explicitly demanding professional
experience. The September notes do not establish the subsequent choice
sequence. The pilot report adds coursework chosen for immediate access and
some personal projects
postponed for accepted campaigns, with counts and participant overlap unspecified.

## Contradictions and validation gaps

- Actual pilot commitments, accepted delivery and recruiter comparisons are now
  reported. Recover offer counts, all campaign attempts and reviewer records;
  do not describe these tests as wholly unrun or their success rates as known.
- The pilot's business charters remained unsigned. Student compensation and
  accepted work do not establish business payment demand; reconcile funding
  and score the existing commercial test only from its qualifying records.
- Motive and timing differ across students. The survey's 3/11 no-action response
  is stated intention; availability and commitment rates need actual offer logs.
  Payment's lower selection frequency does not justify removing it.
- The obstacle table reports 23 selections despite an 11-person, up-to-two-choice
  limit of 22. Resolve the count/label discrepancy against the raw survey.
- No supplied result demonstrates improved hiring chances, regional demand,
  scalable support, or self-organisation by 5–6 strangers without a paid manager.
- Survey segment fit, cohort overlap, recruitment, dates and participant-level
  evidence are missing. Keep the September accounts and new report distinct.
