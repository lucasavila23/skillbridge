# Jobs to Be Done hypothesis

**Version:** 0.2 — 2026-09-22

**Status:** Working hypothesis with partial, participant-reported grounding.

**Review status:** Version 0.1 was confirmed by the user on 2026-09-17.
Version 0.2 is an evidence-informed synthesis of the teammate-reported interview
notes received on 2026-09-22; it is not a validated customer segment or outcome.

## Job to Be Done

When a tech student with mainly coursework experience applies for internships
and is asked for project examples or something they have delivered, they want
practical work they can demonstrate and explain to an employer.

This refines the earlier trigger: both supplied accounts describe requests
during applications, rather than a job listing explicitly demanding professional
experience. The notes do not establish what the students did next or whether
their recent projects were started in response to those requests.

## Contradictions and validation gaps

- Money is not a uniform primary motive: the two students emphasise different
  trade-offs. Neither answer establishes that payment sustains client work.
- Student 1 reports an exam pause and reduced scope; Student 2 reports
  deadline-concentrated effort and limits during finals. Interest in experience
  cannot be treated as dependable availability.
- Both describe needing technical help. Neither describes acceptance by a
  paying business, self-organisation among 5–6 strangers, or a completed
  SkillBridge campaign.
- The sequence from application trigger to search, choice, switching and
  outcome is incomplete. Follow up on one specific episode rather than joining
  separate answers into an invented timeline.
- No application outcome demonstrates that client work, certification or
  SkillBridge improves hiring chances. No recruiter response was supplied.
- Exact interview dates within September 15–20, interviewer identity and
  original source materials remain unspecified. The supplied wording has not
  been independently checked against a transcript.
