# Practice interviews — synthetic examples

**Status: FICTIONAL REHEARSAL ONLY. These interviews did not happen.**

These invented answers illustrate how a casual conversation might sound. They
are not quotes from IE University students, Baya or any actual participant.
They must not be copied into real interview records or counted as validation.

## Fictional student A — regularly builds personal projects

**Interviewer:** What happened the last time you looked for an internship?

**Practice answer:** "Some listings asked for practical project experience.
I had coursework to show, but I wasn't sure how to explain what it would be
useful for outside class."

**Interviewer:** What did you build most recently?

**Practice answer:** "A small web app. I adapted existing libraries and built
around them. It worked for the assignment, but nobody outside class used it."

**Interviewer:** What kept it moving, or made it stall?

**Practice answer:** "The class deadline kept me going. With my own projects,
I sometimes stop when other work gets busy. I haven't had a client waiting for
something yet."

**Interviewer:** How would a paid business project compare?

**Practice answer:** "The money would get my attention. I'd still need to see
the scope and deadline before saying yes. If it needed skills I don't have,
I'd need to know who could help."

## Fictional student B — facing an application deadline

**Interviewer:** What made gaining experience feel urgent?

**Practice answer:** "I'm applying now and want something concrete to discuss
in interviews. I started another portfolio project, but applications took
priority and I haven't finished it."

**Interviewer:** What would matter when choosing a business project?

**Practice answer:** "I'd need something small enough to finish soon. Getting
paid is attractive, but I wouldn't want to promise several months when I don't
know when an internship might start."

**Interviewer:** What if you received an internship offer during the project?

**Practice answer:** "I'd have to discuss the remaining work and a handover.
I can't honestly promise my availability would stay the same."

## Fictional café decision-maker — not Baya

**Interviewer:** What's a recent digital task you needed done?

**Practice answer:** "Keeping our online information up to date. We handled
small edits ourselves, but anything more involved kept getting put off."

**Interviewer:** Have you paid someone for that kind of work before?

**Practice answer:** "We've considered quotes, but I wanted a clearer idea of
what we'd actually receive before agreeing."

**Interviewer:** What would you need to see before considering a student team?

**Practice answer:** "Relevant examples, a small agreed scope, and a named
person I can speak to. I'd need a way to check the work before using it."

**Interviewer:** Would this conversation be enough for you to commit money?

**Practice answer:** "No. I'd need the actual project, price and terms first.
Being interested in the idea doesn't mean I'm ready to pay a deposit."

## Rehearsal takeaway

Even these fictional conversations contain limits: uncertain availability,
untested delivery skills and conditional business interest. They help rehearse
questions; they provide no evidence that SkillBridge's assumptions are true.
Record real answers separately using the
[interview record template](../../evidence-log/validation/interview-record-template.md).
